EMI License in Belgium

A Belgian EMI licence lets you issue electronic money and provide payment services from Belgium, then passport both across the EEA. The National Bank of Belgium (NBB) grants it under the Law of 11 March 2018. Plan the capital first: EUR 350,000 has to be paid up in the company when the licence is granted.

Who needs an EMI license in Belgium?

You need a licence to issue e-money, meaning stored monetary value you issue against funds and that others accept as payment. If your product holds a balance for later spending, it is e-money. If it only moves money between accounts, the payment institution licence in Belgium may be enough.

Other routes can fit:

  • A limited EMI is registered, not fully licensed. It is open to Belgian-law legal persons whose business plan shows average outstanding e-money of no more than EUR 1,500,000 and whose managers have no disqualifying record (Law Art 199). It cannot passport.
  • An EMI or PI licensed in another EEA state can serve Belgium through a branch, an agent or cross-border services without a Belgian licence (NBB guide, section 1).
  • Account information only is a registration route, not an EMI.
  • An EMI that wants to hold or transfer its own e-money tokens notifies its regulator under MiCA Art 60(4) at least 40 working days ahead. Other crypto services need CASP authorisation. See the crypto licence in Belgium.

Belgium EMI license requirements

Capital and own funds

The company needs EUR 350,000 of paid-up capital when the licence is granted (Law Art 173; EMD2 Art 4). An existing company can count issue premiums, reserves and retained earnings, but not revaluation surpluses, and at least EUR 140,000 must still be paid up in capital (Law Art 173). Own funds then never fall below the higher of that capital and the calculated amount. For e-money issuance that is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)). The NBB may set it up to 20% higher or lower under the national rules.

Management and fit and proper

The Law requires two managers responsible for management, called effective leaders in the NBB guide. Directors and qualifying shareholders go through suitability checks. The guide asks for an internal audit charter, a compliance charter and a risk management charter showing three lines of defence, and refers to the NBB internal control regulation of 19 May 2015.

Substance

The file gives the head office and registered office address (NBB guide, section 2.2). We found no written rule on local staff numbers, so ask at the intake meeting what the NBB expects for your model.

AML officer

Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a business-wide risk assessment. The NBB guide lists a dedicated NBB AML team among its contacts (section 5.1). See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

Funds received for e-money are protected by segregation in a credit institution or central bank account, by secure low-risk assets, or by an insurance or guarantee from outside the group (EMD2 Art 7; PSD2 Art 10(1)). Card-funded money has to be safeguarded no later than five business days after issuance (EMD2 Art 7(1)).

ICT and DORA

The PSD2 Art 5(1) file includes ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, an incident procedure, business continuity plans and a security policy.

How to get an EMI license in Belgium

  1. Decide whether you need a full EMI or the limited regime, and set up a Belgian company.
  2. Ask for an intake meeting with the NBB. The guide encourages a first informal discussion of your business plan, and names the NBB Fintech contact point at Fintech@nbb.be.
  3. Prepare the file from the NBB application guide: programme of operations, three-year business plan, capital evidence, governance, safeguarding, ICT, AML and qualifying holder details.
  4. Send an application letter to the Governor of the NBB. The guide says the file itself can follow by secure file transfer or USB stick, shortly after the letter.
  5. The NBB checks completeness. If something is missing it asks for it within a reasonable time.
  6. Once the NBB confirms the file is complete, it decides within three months, and no later than twelve months after you submit (Law Art 169).
  7. If granted, you appear in the register on the NBB site.

The time you spend preparing the file does not count toward these limits.

How much does an EMI license in Belgium cost?

Annual supervision fee EUR 2,500 as the base; EUR 10,000 if prudential own funds exceed EUR 1 million; EUR 15,000 above EUR 5 million; EUR 25,000 above EUR 10 million NBB application guide, section 3.3

These amounts come from the Royal Decree of 21 December 2013, which the NBB guide also cites. The decree refers to the earlier Law of 21 December 2009, and we found no later version, so confirm the current amounts with the NBB before you budget.

Other costs, for which we give no amounts here, include company formation, staff and managers in Belgium, audit, a safeguarding account, professional indemnity insurance where your services call for it, compliance tooling and advisers.

After you get the EMI license

  • The NBB lists you in its register of payment and e-money institutions.
  • You report to the NBB and pay the annual supervision fee shown above.
  • To serve other EEA states you notify the NBB, which passes the notice on. The steps are one month, one month and three months (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Material changes need notice. A change in qualifying holders needs the regulator's assessment.
  • A limited EMI that outgrows EUR 1,500,000 average outstanding e-money has to move to a full licence.

Belgium EMI license vs other countries

Belgium publishes an English application guide and a fee table, and it requires the capital to be paid up when the licence is granted. Compare the EMI licence in Lithuania, Malta and Ireland. EMI license in Belgium FAQs

How much does an EMI license cost in Belgium?

Capital is EUR 350,000 paid up (Law Art 173). The Royal Decree of 21 December 2013 sets a EUR 1,500 fee on application, plus EUR 1,500 per authorised payment service capped at EUR 4,500, and an annual supervision fee starting at EUR 2,500. The decree predates the current Law, so confirm the amounts. Running costs come on top.

What are the Belgium EMI license requirements?

Paid-up capital of EUR 350,000, two effective managers, suitable directors and shareholders, three lines of defence with written charters, safeguarding of customer funds, AML controls and ICT arrangements under DORA.

How long does an EMI license take in Belgium?

The NBB decides within three months of a complete file and no later than twelve months after submission (Law Art 169). Preparing the file and answering questions sits outside that clock.

Can I apply in English?

The NBB application guide is published in English and does not state a filing language. Agree the language of the file and the application letter at the intake meeting.

Is there a small EMI option in Belgium?

Yes. A limited EMI is registered for Belgian-law legal persons with average outstanding e-money up to EUR 1,500,000 (Law Art 199). It cannot passport.

Can a Belgian EMI passport to other EU countries?

A fully licensed EMI can, by notifying the NBB (PSD2 Art 28). A limited EMI cannot.

Does Belgium accept an EMI licensed elsewhere in the EEA?

Yes. An EMI licensed in another EEA state can operate in Belgium through a branch, an agent or cross-border services (NBB guide, section 1).

Do I need a separate licence for e-money tokens?

Only EMIs and credit institutions may issue e-money tokens (MiCA Art 48(1)). An EMI notifies its regulator under MiCA Art 60(4) to custody or transfer its own tokens.
