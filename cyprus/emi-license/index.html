EMI License in Cyprus

A Cypriot EMI licence lets you issue electronic money and provide payment services from Cyprus, then passport both across the EU and EEA. The Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) grants it. Since 3 August 2026 every applicant starts with a paid pre-application exploratory stage, so you begin with a document and a EUR 5,000 fee before any full application.

Who needs an EMI licence in Cyprus?

You need CBC authorisation to issue electronic money in Cyprus, unless you hold an authorisation from another EU state and use your passport rights (CBC EMI application page). Prepaid cards, wallets with a spendable balance and similar products usually fall inside the e-money definition of EMD2 Art 2(2).

When another route fits better:

  • You only move money and do not hold a balance. A payment institution licence in Cyprus needs less capital.
  • You want to issue e-money tokens. An EMI may do so after a white paper notification (MiCA Art 48). Other crypto services need MiCA authorisation from CySEC. An Art 60 notification covers only custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues.
  • You hold another EEA licence. Use the passport instead of a second authorisation. Cyprus lists such incoming EMIs on its register.
  • You expect to stay small. EMD2 Art 9 allows a waiver below EUR 5,000,000 of average outstanding e-money. The Cypriot law lets the CBC set a limited regime by directive (s 5(3)), but we found no such directive, so do not plan around it.
  • You are a crypto firm that moves or holds e-money tokens for clients. The CBC says that activity can be a payment service needing its own authorisation (see our payment institution page).

Requirements for an EMI licence in Cyprus

Capital and own funds

At least EUR 350,000 of initial capital at the time of authorisation (EMI Law s 12(1); EMD2 Art 4). Own funds then stay at the higher of that amount and the calculated figure, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)). The CBC sets the composition by directive (s 12(2)).

Corporate form and substance

The CBC authorises only a legal person incorporated in Cyprus, with its registered office and head office in Cyprus, which carries out at least part of its e-money business or its unrelated payment services there (CBC EMI application page).

Fit and proper management

The CBC will not authorise if it is not convinced that all board and management members are fit and proper (EMI Law s 6(7)). It applies its Directive on the Assessment of the Suitability of members of the board or management of EMIs of 2025, and a personal questionnaire and a time commitment table go with the file. Shareholders with qualifying holdings submit the QHL or QHN form.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money issued (EMD2 Art 7(1)). PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886, lets you segregate funds at a credit institution or central bank, hold secure low-risk assets, or use insurance or a guarantee from outside the group. The CBC also issued a 2026 directive defining secure, liquid and low-risk assets, published in Greek only.

Governance, ICT and AML

The CBC adopts the EBA guidelines on application content (EBA/GL/2017/09). The file covers a business plan, governance, ICT under DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554), incident and continuity procedures and AML controls (PSD2 Art 5(1)). Internal governance requirements sit in the 2026 EMI Internal Organisation and Governance Directive, again in Greek only. See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for an EMI licence in Cyprus

The CBC runs three stages since 3 August 2026.

  1. Pre-application document. Complete the CBC's Pre-Application Document and email it to the Licensing Section, stating that you intend to apply for an EMI or PI licence, with proof of payment of the non-refundable EUR 5,000 fee. The CBC says the fee goes to its "Application Fees" account.
  2. Preliminary assessment. The CBC assesses the reputation of the ultimate beneficial owners and group, and the risk of the business model. It may ask for an enhanced due diligence report from an independent third party, which you supply or which the CBC obtains at your cost. This stage must finish within 30 working days, or 50 working days if a report is requested.
  3. Initial meeting. The CBC invites the beneficial owners and your representatives, gives feedback and flags concerns. Completing this stage is not approval.
  4. Application. Submit the EMI application form, the QHL and QHN forms for shareholders and the MMB/KFH questionnaires for managers through the CBC e-platform, with the EUR 10,000 fee receipt. The CBC publishes a list of common errors seen in EMI applications to check against.
  5. Assessment. The CBC verifies the information and keeps in touch, meeting your representatives where needed. If you miss a deadline it gives, it warns you and then treats the application as withdrawn.
  6. Decision. EU law requires a decision within 3 months of a complete application, with reasons for a refusal (PSD2 Art 12, applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)).

Preparation time and question rounds come on top of the statutory clock.

After authorisation

  • Register: the CBC lists authorised EMIs, their branches, distributors and agents, and the passport details in a public register. The file we downloaded on 23 November 2026 lists 33 EMI licence numbers.
  • Qualifying holdings: anyone who plans to reach or cross 20%, 30% or 50% of capital or voting rights, or to fall below them, tells the CBC in advance, using the QHL or QHN forms.
  • Management changes: proposed appointments and removals of assessed persons are notified to the CBC.
  • Extension of services: you apply on the application form and ask the CBC about the fee.
  • Distributors and agents: a form covers the appointment of distributors and agents in Cyprus or abroad.
  • Passport: authorisation is valid across the EU (PSD2 Art 11(9), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). You notify the CBC of the states, services, agents and branches. The home regulator forwards the notification within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Material changes: the CBC expects advance notice of other changes that affect the accuracy of your application.

Cyprus vs other countries

Cyprus asks for a Cypriot company and a staged CBC process. Compare the EMI licence in Malta, where the MFSA has a three-month statutory clock and a EUR 10,000 application fee, and the EMI licence in Lithuania or Ireland. In Cyprus, the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP authorisation sit alongside this one. The general rules are in our EMI licence guide.

EMI in Cyprus FAQs

How much capital do you need for an EMI licence in Cyprus?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMI Law s 12(1); EMD2 Art 4). Your own funds then stay above the higher of that figure and 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)). Older sources quoting EUR 250,000 do not match the current law text.

What is the new pre-application stage for Cyprus EMIs?

From 3 August 2026 the CBC requires a Pre-Application Document and a non-refundable EUR 5,000 fee before any formal application. The CBC assesses the beneficial owners' reputation and the business risk, may ask for an enhanced due diligence report, and holds an initial meeting. Completing it is not approval.

How much is the CBC application fee for an EMI?

EUR 10,000, paid with the application. The EUR 5,000 pre-application fee is paid earlier and is separate.

How long does the pre-application stage take?

The CBC's assessment must finish within 30 working days of the document, or 50 working days if it requests an enhanced due diligence report. The initial meeting follows.

How long does an EMI licence take in Cyprus?

The EU rule is a decision within 3 months of a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). The CBC pages do not state a longer period. Pre-application time, file preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a timeline.

Do you need an office in Cyprus?

Yes. You must be a Cypriot company with registered and head office in Cyprus and carry out at least part of your business there (CBC EMI application page).

Can a Cypriot EMI serve customers across the EU?

Yes. You notify the CBC of the states and services you want to add (PSD2 Art 28), and the CBC register records your passporting.

What happens to a slow application?

If you miss a deadline the CBC sets for comments or queries, it warns you and, if you still do not respond, classifies the application as withdrawn and stops assessing it.
