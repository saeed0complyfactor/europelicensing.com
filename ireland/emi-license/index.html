EMI License in Ireland

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence from the Central Bank of Ireland lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Ireland, then passport them across the EEA. It suits fintechs that want an Irish subsidiary with a full-scope licence.

The Central Bank charges no application fee, but its own 2025 report puts the average assessment at 763 calendar days.

Who needs an EMI licence in Ireland?

You need authorisation if you issue e-money: prepaid cards, wallets with a stored balance, or similar products. An EMI may also provide payment services and the other activities listed in reg 28 of the Irish regulations.

Three other routes can fit better:

  • Small EMI registration under reg 33 suits a domestic business. The average outstanding e-money must not exceed EUR 1 million, and payment transactions must average no more than EUR 3 million a month (reg 33(1)). The registration is valid only in Ireland (reg 33(3)), and you must apply for full authorisation within 30 calendar days of ceasing to qualify (reg 35(1)). The Central Bank asks small EMI applicants to contact the Payments Authorisation Team.
  • A payment institution licence, if you will not hold e-money. See the payment institution licence in Ireland. The Central Bank states that no small payment institution regime is in place in Ireland.
  • An EMI authorised elsewhere in the EEA can serve Irish customers through a passport, so an Irish licence is only needed if you want Ireland as your home state.

Requirements for an EMI licence in Ireland

Capital and own funds

Initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 (reg 13(1)). Only the own-funds elements named in reg 13(2) count. Ongoing own funds are the higher of that amount and Method D, at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (regs 14 and 15). If you also provide unrelated payment services, the Bank directs you to Method A, B or C for those (reg 14(1)(a)). In the authorisation letter the Central Bank states the proposed capital for your firm.

Legal form and substance

The applicant must be a legal person with its head office and registered office in the State (reg 11(1)(a)). The Central Bank says it expects decision-making at Board and Executive level to take place within the State, and it assesses the adequacy of local resources.

Management and fitness

Individuals in pre-approval controlled function (PCF) roles can be interviewed as part of the fitness and probity assessment. The Bank refuses authorisation if it is not satisfied with the suitability of anyone holding a qualifying holding (reg 11(5)). Governance must include a clear organisational structure, risk procedures and internal controls (reg 11(3)).

Safeguarding

You safeguard users' funds under reg 29, either by segregation in a credit institution account or secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a comparable guarantee. The Central Bank required a specific safeguarding audit in its Dear CEO letter of 20 January 2023, with a Board response deadline of 31 October 2023.

AML and ICT

Applicants complete an AML/CFT pre-authorisation questionnaire. The file also covers ICT and operational resilience under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA). Our AML and compliance programme covers both.

How to apply for an EMI licence in Ireland

The Central Bank runs three stages.

  1. Exploratory stage. Email applications@centralbank.ie to arrange an initial meeting. Complete the pre-application key facts document (KFD) first, about 15 to 20 pages in Word, and send it at least 5 working days before the meeting. The Central Bank then checks that the key information is present and either moves you to assessment with an assigned case manager or tells you what blocks progress.
  2. Assessment stage. The Bank requests more information, holds meetings and may interview PCF candidates. A positive outcome is a "Minded to Authorise" letter setting out pre-authorisation requirements, post-authorisation conditions and your proposed capital. A negative outcome is a "Minded to Refuse" letter with reasons and time for written submissions.
  3. Decision. The authorisation letter issues once you meet the pre-authorisation requirements and accept the conditions.

Documents go through Kiteworks; ask for access at the same address. PCF questionnaires use the Central Bank Portal, which opens after you reach assessment. The Central Bank's page lists the EMI application form as under development and publishes qualifying shareholder forms and the AML/CFT questionnaire. Authorisation process on centralbank.ie.

The clocks

Reg 12 gives the Bank 3 months from a complete application (or from receipt of all information needed). The Central Bank also publishes service standards: 95% of key information checks within 10 business days and 90% of assessments within 90 business days of assessment starting. The 90-day clock pauses while an information request is open. A firm with no adequate response within 50 working days gets a "Minded to Deem Withdrawn" letter and then 10 working days to respond.

In practice the elapsed time is much longer. The Central Bank's 2025 Authorisations and Gatekeeping Report gives an average of 763 calendar days from initial application to authorisation for payment and e-money firms, and 497 days for the shortest. It attributes the gap to applicants who were not prepared, slow replies to its queries and business model changes mid-application. Nine applications arrived in 2025 and four firms were authorised.

Other cost lines, which we do not put a figure on here: the Irish legal entity, Irish-based directors and staff, a statutory auditor, a safeguarding account or insurance, professional indemnity insurance where relevant, premises, ICT and outsourcing, and advisers.

Our fee starts at EUR 60,000.

After authorisation

Each year you pay the funding levy and submit online returns, which the levy calculation draws on. Reg 25 requires records for at least 5 years. Conditions in the authorisation letter and the safeguarding audit regime continue.

To serve another EEA state, you notify the Central Bank at least one month before starting (reg 26). Distribution through distributors is allowed (reg 20), and agents must be registered (reg 21). Reg 37 defines a qualifying holding, and the Bank assesses shareholders' suitability, so plan any investor change as a regulated event.

Ireland compared with other EMI jurisdictions

Ireland has full-scope EMI authorisation and a published process, but its assessments run long. For faster-moving applicants, compare the EMI licence in Lithuania and the EMI licence in Malta. In Ireland itself, see the payment institution licence and the crypto (MiCA) licence. An EMI that issues e-money tokens can use the MiCA Article 60 notification for limited custody and transfer services.

EMI in Ireland FAQs

How much does an EMI licence in Ireland cost?

The Central Bank charges no application fee. You need EUR 350,000 initial capital (reg 13(1)) and pay an annual levy with a minimum of EUR 6,625 in the 2026 guide, plus a variable part. Your own set-up and adviser costs come on top.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Ireland?

The statutory decision period is 3 months from a complete application (reg 12). The Central Bank's service standard is 90 business days for assessment. Its 2025 report records an average of 763 calendar days overall for payment and e-money firms.

How do I register an EMI licence in Ireland?

Authorisation, not registration, applies to a full EMI. Contact applications@centralbank.ie, complete the KFD and meet the team. Registration applies to small EMIs under reg 33 and to account information service providers.

Does Ireland have a small EMI?

Yes. Reg 33 lets the Central Bank register a small EMI with average outstanding e-money up to EUR 1 million and payment transactions up to EUR 3 million a month. It is valid only in Ireland.

Can I run an Irish EMI from outside Ireland?

No. The firm needs its head office and registered office in the State (reg 11(1)(a)), and the Central Bank expects Board and Executive decision-making there.

Can an Irish EMI passport into other EU countries?

Yes. Notify the Central Bank in writing at least one month before starting (reg 26). The small EMI registration cannot be passported.

What does the key facts document cover?

The April 2024 template asks for your background and legal status, business and operating model, outsourcing, safeguarding and planned start date. It runs about 15 to 20 pages.

Is an EMI licence in Ireland worth it compared with a payment institution?

Choose an EMI if you will hold stored value. Choose a payment institution if you only move money. Ireland has no small payment institution tier, so a PI always needs full authorisation. An EMI can register as a small EMI only within the reg 33 limits, and only for Ireland.
