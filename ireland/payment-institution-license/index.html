Payment Institution License in Ireland

A payment institution (PI) licence from the Central Bank of Ireland lets you provide payment services such as money remittance, acquiring or payment initiation from Ireland and passport them across the EEA. It is the right licence if you move money but do not hold stored value.

Ireland has no small payment institution tier, so every PI applicant goes through full authorisation. The Central Bank charges no application fee.

Who needs a payment institution licence in Ireland?

You need authorisation to provide any of the payment services in the Schedule to S.I. 6/2018, which follows PSD2 Annex I: cash deposits and withdrawals, payment transactions, card issuing and acquiring, money remittance, payment initiation and account information. Other routes can fit better:

  • Account information services only. You are registered, not authorised. Capital and conditions do not apply (reg 42), the Central Bank still assesses the suitability of qualifying holders, and its AISP registration form is available as a Word document. The AML/CFT questionnaire applies only after registration, and only if you are a designated person under the Criminal Justice Act 2010.
  • An EMI, if you will hold customer balances as e-money. See the EMI licence in Ireland.
  • A passport from a licence granted in another EEA state, if you do not need Ireland as your home state.
  • A MiCA authorisation, if your business is crypto services. A CASP that transfers e-money tokens may also need a PI licence or a partnership with an authorised payment provider after the EBA's June 2025 No Action Letter, and the Central Bank reports five CASPs applied for PI authorisation in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Requirements for a payment institution licence in Ireland

Capital and own funds

Initial capital of EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the services (reg 8(1)). Ongoing own funds can never fall below that amount, and the Bank directs you to Method A, B or C (regs 9 and 11 to 14). The Central Bank states your proposed capital in the "Minded to Authorise" letter.

Substance and governance

The Central Bank expects decision-making at Board and Executive level to take place within the State and assesses whether your local resources are adequate. Reg 18 requires governance arrangements that include a clear organisational structure, risk procedures and internal controls. The Bank refuses if it is not satisfied with qualifying shareholders.

Management and fitness

PCF role holders can be interviewed during the fitness and probity assessment. The assessment looks at your business strategy, programme of operations and financial projections for the first three years.

Safeguarding

Reg 17 sets the safeguarding rules for users' funds. The Central Bank's 2023 Dear CEO letter required firms to commission a safeguarding audit.

AML and ICT

Applicants complete an AML/CFT pre-authorisation questionnaire. ICT arrangements follow Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA). Our AML and compliance programme covers both.

How to apply for a payment institution licence in Ireland

  1. Email applications@centralbank.ie to arrange an initial meeting.
  2. Write the pre-application key facts document (KFD), about 15 to 20 pages, and send it at least 5 working days before the meeting. The Central Bank's template asks for your background and legal status, business and operating model, outsourcing, safeguarding and planned start date.
  3. The Bank does a key information check and assigns a case manager if you pass.
  4. In assessment, answer information requests, attend meetings and put PCF candidates forward. The Bank issues a "Minded to Authorise" or "Minded to Refuse" letter.
  5. Meet the pre-authorisation requirements and accept any conditions and the stated capital. The authorisation letter follows.

Documents go through Kiteworks, and PCF questionnaires through the Central Bank Portal after you reach assessment. The Central Bank's page lists the PI application form as under development. Authorisation process on centralbank.ie.

The clocks

Reg 23(1) gives the Bank 3 months from a complete application or from receipt of all required information. Published service standards: 95% of key information checks in 10 business days, 90% of assessments in 90 business days, and a final decision within 10 business days of a satisfactory reply to the "Minded to Authorise" letter. The 90 days pause while information requests are open, and the Bank warns it can take over 12 months for firms to supply everything needed.

The Central Bank's 2025 Authorisations and Gatekeeping Report records an average of 763 calendar days from initial application to authorisation for payment and e-money firms, with 497 days the shortest. Thirteen firms were in pre-application at the end of 2025.

After authorisation

You pay the annual levy and submit online returns, and the authorisation letter's conditions apply. To serve other EEA states, reg 37(1) requires you to send the Central Bank details of the states, services, agents and branches not less than 3 months before you start. The Bank decides within 3 months of that information (reg 37(9)). PSD2 Art 28 sets shorter steps, so confirm the current Irish timing with the Central Bank before planning a launch date. Anyone planning to acquire a qualifying holding in a PI must notify the Bank first (reg 48), and the Bank assesses the proposal (regs 51 to 53).

Ireland compared with other PI jurisdictions

Ireland has no small PI tier. Some other states do, as PSD2 Art 32 allows. Compare the payment institution licence in Lithuania and the payment institution licence in Malta. In Ireland itself, the EMI licence suits stored value and the crypto (MiCA) licence suits crypto services.

Payment institution in Ireland FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Ireland?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for services such as payment accounts, transfers and acquiring (reg 8(1)).

Is there a small payment institution licence in Ireland?

No. S.I. 6/2018 reg 41 contains an exemption power with a EUR 3,000,000 monthly ceiling, but the Central Bank states that "there is no small payment institution regime in place in Ireland", so firms apply for full authorisation.

How long does a payment institution licence take in Ireland?

The statutory period is 3 months from a complete application (reg 23(1)). The Central Bank's 2025 report gives an average of 763 calendar days for payment and e-money applications.

Does the Central Bank of Ireland charge an application fee?

No. Authorised firms pay an annual Industry Funding levy, with a minimum of EUR 6,625 in the 2026 guide, plus a variable part.

Do account information providers need a licence in Ireland?

They register instead of seeking authorisation (reg 42). Capital and conditions do not apply.

Can I passport an Irish payment institution licence?

Yes, by notifying the Central Bank under reg 37. Check the notice period with the Bank before planning.

What is the key facts document?

A 15 to 20 page pre-application document you send at least 5 working days before the first meeting.

Can a crypto firm hold a PI licence in Ireland?

Yes, and some need one. The Central Bank adopted a PI process that relies on the CASP application where firms transfer e-money tokens.
