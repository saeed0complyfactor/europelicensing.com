EMI License in Croatia

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence from the Croatian National Bank (Hrvatska narodna banka, HNB) lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Croatia, then passport them across the EEA. It is for wallets, prepaid and card programmes and neobanks that hold customer balances. A full EMI needs EUR 350,000 of capital. Croatia also has a small EMI tier with lower capital, but it works in Croatia only.

Who needs an EMI license in Croatia?

You need it if you issue electronic money from Croatia: a stored balance that customers hold with you and spend with third parties (EMD2 Art 2(2); Art 10). Prepaid cards, wallets and accounts that hold a spendable balance usually fall in scope. An EMI can also provide the payment services in PSD2 Annex I (EMD2 Art 6(1)).

Other routes can fit better:

  • If you only move money and never hold a balance for later spending, a payment institution licence in Croatia needs less capital.
  • If your business is small and local, the small EMI tier asks for EUR 119,000 of capital and caps average outstanding e-money at EUR 265,000. It works only in Croatia and has no passport.
  • If you already hold an EMI licence in another EEA state, you passport into Croatia by notification and need no Croatian licence.
  • If you also want to custody or transfer your own e-money tokens, an EMI can notify under MiCA Art 60(4). Wider crypto services need a Croatian CASP authorisation from HANFA.

EMI license requirements in Croatia

Capital and own funds

A full EMI holds at least EUR 350,000 of initial capital (Electronic Money Act Art 14(1); EMD2 Art 4). For a company in formation, the shareholders pay that amount in cash (Art 14(2)). Own funds then stay at or above the initial capital and at or above the calculated amount, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (Art 34; EMD2 Art 5(3)). The HNB's Decision on own funds sets the forms and projections.

Management and fit and proper

Board members or executive directors need a good reputation and appropriate knowledge and experience (Art 16(1)(2)). The HNB also tests qualifying shareholders for suitability (Art 16(2); PSD2 Art 11(6) as applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)).

Substance

The activities must be carried out from the territory of Croatia, and you must intend to provide at least part of your e-money issuance and payment services there (Art 16(1)(5)). The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). The HNB page states no minimum number of local staff, so ask at the pre-application stage.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money (Art 35; EMD2 Art 7(1)) by segregating them in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). The HNB's Decision on safeguarding (Official Gazette 13/2025) sets the detail.

ICT and DORA

The HNB asks for ICT service rules, incident reporting, business continuity and security controls under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA) and the EBA guidelines on ICT and security risk management (Art 17(3)(9), (10), (12), (14)).

How to get an EMI license in Croatia

  1. Map your product to the e-money and payment services you need, and decide between a full and a small EMI.
  2. Set up a Croatian legal entity and pay in the capital in cash.
  3. Prepare the file from the HNB EMI page. It lists the Article 17 documents: financial statements on form IEN-RK where applicable, a business plan with three-year projections, safeguarding measures, DORA documents, professional indemnity cover for payment initiation or account information, the organisational structure and a signed personal data form.
  4. Follow the EBA guidelines on the information to be provided for authorisation (PSD2 Art 5(5)), which the HNB page cites.
  5. Pay the application processing fee to the HNB (Art 17(11)).
  6. Submit to the HNB, Trg hrvatskih velikana 3, Zagreb. The page does not state a language or a pre-application meeting, so ask.
  7. The HNB decides in an administrative procedure and must adopt and deliver its decision within three months of a complete application (Art 99).
  8. After the decision, enter the activity in the companies register. You may issue e-money only after registration.

The three-month limit is statutory. Preparing the file and answering questions takes extra time, and we give you a dated plan after scoping.

After you get the EMI license

  • The HNB enters you in its register of electronic money institutions (Art 28).
  • You report to the HNB and keep own funds above the required level.
  • To issue e-money or provide payment services in another EEA state, you notify the HNB in advance. You can use a branch, direct provision or distributors. PSD2 Art 28 sets 1-month steps for the HNB and the host state, and up to 3 months for branches and agents.
  • You may use distributors but not agents to issue e-money (EMD2 Art 3(4), (5)).
  • A small EMI that exceeds its limits must seek full authorisation within 30 calendar days (EMD2 Art 9(6)).
  • A change in a qualifying holding is a change of control and needs the HNB's assessment before it completes.

Croatia EMI license vs other countries

Croatia has a domestic small EMI tier, and its statutory decision period is 3 months. To compare, see the EMI licence in Slovenia, the EMI licence in Lithuania and the EMI licence in Malta. Within Croatia, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence. For the EU rules behind all of them, read our EMI licence in Europe guide.

EMI license in Croatia FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Croatia?

EUR 350,000 for a full EMI (Electronic Money Act Art 14(1); EMD2 Art 4). A small EMI needs EUR 119,000 (Art 58(1)).

What is a small EMI in Croatia?

A domestic tier for e-money issuers whose average outstanding e-money stays at or below EUR 265,000. If it also provides payment services unrelated to e-money, their average monthly value over 12 months stays at or below EUR 995,000. It is entered in the HNB register, works only in Croatia and cannot passport (Art 57, 59).

How much does an EMI licence cost in Croatia?

The HNB charges an application processing fee set in its subordinate legislation, and we could not confirm the amount. Add EUR 350,000 of capital and the running costs of a regulated firm: local people, audit, safeguarding, ICT and advisers. We do not give amounts for those running costs.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Croatia?

The HNB must decide within three months of a complete application (Art 99). Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

Do I need an office or staff in Croatia?

You need a Croatian legal entity, and the activity must be run from Croatian territory (Art 16(1)(5)). A small EMI must have its head office in Croatia (Art 59(1)(3)). The HNB page states no staff minimum, so raise it before you apply.

Can I passport a Croatian EMI licence?

A full EMI can, by notifying the HNB in advance (PSD2 Art 28). A small EMI cannot (Art 57(3); EMD2 Art 9(3)).

Can I file the application in English?

The HNB pages we opened do not say. Ask the HNB before you file, and budget for sworn translations if it asks for Croatian.

Do I need an EMI or a payment institution licence?

Choose an EMI if customers hold a stored balance you issue as e-money. Choose a payment institution if you only execute payments or provide initiation or account information.
