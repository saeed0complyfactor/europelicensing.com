Payment Institution License in Croatia

A payment institution (PI) licence from the Croatian National Bank (Hrvatska narodna banka, HNB) lets you provide PSD2 payment services from Croatia and passport them across the EEA. It is for remitters, payment initiation providers, acquirers and card or transfer processors that do not issue e-money. Capital runs from EUR 20,000 to EUR 125,000 by service, and Croatia also has a small PI tier that works in Croatia only.

Who needs a payment institution license in Croatia?

You need authorisation before you provide a PSD2 payment service from Croatia, unless you already hold a bank, EMI or other PSD2 licence (PSD2 Art 11(1)). The services are cash placement and withdrawal, execution of transfers, direct debits and card payments, issuing or acquiring payment instruments, money remittance, payment initiation and account information.

Pick the route that matches your business:

  • A full licence passports into the EEA and carries the capital tier for your services.
  • A small PI may provide only the services in Art 4 items 3 to 6 of the Payment System Act, only in Croatia. Its average monthly payment transactions over the last 12 months stay at or below EUR 995,000, and its management seat is in Croatia (Art 120, 122).
  • An account-information-only firm registers instead of seeking authorisation, needs professional indemnity insurance and no initial capital (PSD2 Art 33; Art 5(3)).
  • An agent of an authorised PI in another EEA state needs no Croatian licence.
  • If customers keep a balance with you for later spending, that is e-money and you need an EMI licence in Croatia.

Account information only None PSD2 Art 33

For a company in formation, the shareholders pay the initial capital in cash (Art 82(4), 121(3)). Own funds must stay at or above the higher of the initial capital and the amount the HNB's Decision on own funds produces (PSD2 Art 8, 9).

Management and fit and proper

Board members or executive directors need a good reputation and appropriate knowledge and experience for payment services. The HNB also tests qualifying shareholders for suitability (Art 84(1)(1), (2)).

Substance

The business must be run from Croatia, and you must intend to provide at least part of the payment services there (Art 84(1)(5)). The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). A small PI must have its management seat in Croatia (Art 122(1)(3)). The HNB page states no staff minimum, so ask before you apply.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Appoint a named AML compliance officer. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard customer funds under Art 100, either by segregation in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). The HNB's Decision on safeguarding (Official Gazette 13/2025) sets the detail.

ICT and DORA

The file covers ICT arrangements, incident reporting and business continuity under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 and the EBA guidelines on ICT and security risk management (Art 85(2)). Payment initiation and account information providers also hold professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee (Art 101).

How to get a payment institution license in Croatia

  1. Map your services to PSD2 Annex I to find your capital tier and your route: full, small or account information only.
  2. Set up a Croatian legal entity and pay in the capital in cash.
  3. Prepare the file from the HNB PI page, which lists the Article 85 documents and links the EBA guidelines on authorisation information.
  4. Pay the application processing fee to the HNB (Art 85(11)).
  5. Submit to the HNB. The page does not state a language or a pre-application meeting, so ask.
  6. The HNB decides in an administrative procedure, and must adopt and deliver its decision within three months of a complete application (Art 178).
  7. After the decision, enter the payment services in the companies register as a business activity. You may start only after registration.
  8. For a small PI, apply for entry in the HNB register under Art 123, with a report on the last 12 months of transactions or a 12-month projection.

These deadlines are statutory, and preparation time comes on top.

After you get the payment institution license

  • The HNB enters you in its register of payment institutions.
  • You report to the HNB, keep own funds at the required level and safeguard customer funds.
  • To serve another EEA state, a full PI notifies the HNB in advance. The home regulator forwards it within 1 month and the host assesses within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1), (2)). Agents and branches take up to 3 months (Art 28(3)).
  • A small PI that stops meeting the EUR 995,000 condition must apply for full authorisation within 30 days (Art 128(3)).
  • Material changes, including a change of qualifying holder, need notice to the HNB. A new qualifying holding needs the HNB's assessment before it completes.

Croatia payment institution license vs other countries

Croatia has a small PI tier with capital from EUR 6,500, and a statutory decision period of 3 months. Compare the payment institution licence in Slovenia, Lithuania and Malta. In Croatia, the sibling pages are the EMI licence and the MiCA CASP licence. The EU rules sit in our payment institution licence in the EU guide.

Payment institution license in Croatia FAQs

What is a payment institution in Croatia?

A firm the HNB authorises to provide PSD2 payment services under the Payment System Act. It may not issue e-money.

How much capital does a payment institution need in Croatia?

EUR 20,000 for remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5 (Art 82). A small PI needs EUR 6,500 for remittance or EUR 39,500 for services 3 to 5 (Art 121).

What is a small payment institution in Croatia?

A domestic tier for firms providing only the services in Art 4 items 3 to 6, with average monthly transactions over the last 12 months at or below EUR 995,000. It works only in Croatia, is entered in an HNB register and cannot passport (Art 120, 122).

How much does a payment institution licence cost in Croatia?

The HNB charges an application processing fee set in its subordinate legislation, and we could not confirm the amount. Capital and running costs come on top, and we do not give amounts for running costs.

How long does it take to get a payment institution licence in Croatia?

The HNB must decide within three months of a complete application (Art 178). Preparation time is extra, and we do not quote a real-world average.

Can I passport a Croatian payment institution licence?

A full licence can be passported by notifying the HNB in advance (PSD2 Art 28). A small PI cannot (Art 120(2)).

Do I need an EMI or a payment institution licence?

Choose an EMI if customers hold a stored balance you issue as e-money. Choose a payment institution if you only execute payments or provide initiation or account information.

Can I file the application in English?

The HNB pages we opened do not say. Ask the HNB before you file, and budget for sworn translations if it asks for Croatian.
