EMI License in Iceland

An electronic money institution (rafeyrisfyrirtæki) licence in Iceland lets you issue e-money from an Icelandic company. The Central Bank of Iceland (Seðlabanki Íslands), which absorbed the former Financial Supervisory Authority, grants it under Act 17/2013 on the Issuance and Handling of Electronic Money. Iceland is an EEA state, so the licence passports across the EEA.

Who needs an EMI license in Iceland?

The Central Bank says e-money may be issued in Iceland only by issuers under Act 17/2013 that hold authorisation from the Central Bank or from another EEA authority. A balance your customer can hold and spend later is e-money. A product that only moves money for a single payment is a payment service and may fit a payment institution licence instead.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A payment institution licence, if you do not hold stored value.
  • An EMI authorised in another EEA state. The Central Bank says EEA-licensed firms can operate in Iceland, but they cannot start until it has received a notification from their home regulator.
  • A limited licence under Art 16 of Act 17/2013, if you issue no more than ISK 30 million of e-money a calendar year and only in Iceland. This gives no passport.

An Icelandic licence passports across the EEA by notification, like any other EEA licence.

EMI license requirements in Iceland

Capital and own funds. Initial capital is at least the ISK equivalent of EUR 350,000 (Act 17/2013 Art 11; EMD2 Art 4). Own funds are the higher of initial capital and the calculated requirement, with Method D at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (Art 5). The Central Bank's payment institution checklist asks for an auditor's confirmation that capital was paid in full. Expect a similar step.

Management and fit and proper. The Central Bank assesses the board and the managing director under Art 26 of Act 17/2013, with the Act on Financial Undertakings (161/2002). It may also assess shareholders with qualifying holdings under Art 14.

Substance. Act 17/2013 Art 9 requires an Icelandic EMI to be a legal person with its head office in Iceland that carries out at least part of its e-money business there. The Central Bank's payment institution checklist asks for the organisation, outsourcing agreements, staff numbers by area and the location of premises. It requires an application signed by the board.

AML. The checklist lists AML risk assessment, procedures, a named responsible manager and staff training under the Icelandic AML law.

Safeguarding. Safeguarding of customer funds follows EMD2 Art 7. The Central Bank has issued Rules No 322/2014 on safeguarding the funds of e-money institutions, and its payment institution checklist asks for an agreement with a credit institution for customer funds.

ICT and DORA. The checklist asks for IT security, operational risk and incident reporting descriptions. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies through the EEA.

How to get an EMI license in Iceland: process

  1. Choose the route and form an Icelandic company.
  2. Request a meeting with the Central Bank before you file. Its checklist asks for this.
  3. Prepare a written application with the information in chapter III of Act 17/2013. The Central Bank publishes an overview of what an EMI application must include. Its payment institution checklist, which we read, expects a file signed by the board, with the company and service description, articles, organisation and outsourcing, internal controls, safeguarding, ICT, capital and three-year plan, and management information.
  4. File with the Central Bank and pay the fees on its cost page.
  5. The Central Bank notifies its decision no later than 3 months after a satisfactory application (Act 17/2013 Art 21).

We did not find a filing portal or an English application form.

After you get the EMI license

  • The Central Bank supervises e-money institutions and lists them among regulated entities.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee on the levy base.
  • To serve another EEA state, notify the Central Bank first (PSD2 Art 28 via EMD2 Art 3(1)).
  • Material changes to safeguarding are notified in advance (EMD2 Art 3(2)).
  • Check the qualifying holding rules (Act 17/2013 Art 14, with chapter VI of the Act on Financial Undertakings) before any share transfer.

EMI in Iceland FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Iceland?

The ISK equivalent of EUR 350,000 (Act 17/2013 Art 11, following EMD2 Art 4).

How much does the Central Bank charge?

Its cost page lists a one-time processing fee of ISK 1,280,000 and a licence fee of ISK 230,000 for payment and e-money institutions. The page is undated, so confirm the amounts.

How long does a decision take?

Act 17/2013 Art 21 requires the decision no later than 3 months after a satisfactory application. Your preparation time comes on top.

Is Iceland in the EU?

No, but it is in the EEA, and EMD2 and PSD2 apply through the EEA Agreement. A licence from the Central Bank passports across the EEA by notification.

Is there a small EMI licence in Iceland?

Yes. Art 16 of Act 17/2013 allows a limited licence for a legal person with its head office in Iceland, if expected e-money issuance does not exceed ISK 30 million a calendar year. It covers issuance in Iceland only, so there is no passport.

Do I need an Icelandic company?

Yes, for an Icelandic licence. Act 17/2013 Art 9 requires a legal person with its head office in Iceland that carries out at least part of its e-money business there.

Which language do I file in?

The sources we read do not say. The Central Bank's checklist is in Icelandic, so ask before you prepare translations.

Do I need to meet the Central Bank first?

Its payment institution checklist asks applicants to request a meeting before filing.
