Payment Institution License in Iceland

A payment institution (greiðslustofnun) licence in Iceland lets you provide payment services from an Icelandic company. The Central Bank of Iceland (Seðlabanki Íslands) grants it under chapter II of Act 114/2021 on Payment Services, which implements PSD2. The Central Bank asks applicants to request a meeting before they file, and its cost page lists a one-time processing fee of ISK 1,280,000.

Who needs a payment institution license in Iceland?

Only entities authorised under Act 114/2021 may provide payment services in Iceland, according to the Central Bank. E-money institutions may also provide payment services tied to issuing e-money. If you hold stored value, see the EMI licence.

Other routes can fit better:

  • An institution authorised in another EEA state that notifies its home regulator. The Central Bank says such firms cannot start in Iceland until it has received that notification.
  • A limited authorisation or an exemption under Act 114/2021, which the Central Bank lists among permitted providers. We did not read the conditions, so ask the Central Bank whether your model fits.
  • A licence in another EEA state, passported into Iceland.

Payment institution license requirements in Iceland

Capital and own funds. Initial capital is the ISK equivalent of EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the services (Act 114/2021 Art 7, following PSD2 Art 7). The Central Bank's checklist asks for an auditor's signed confirmation that the capital was paid in, and for a three-year plan with a capital plan and stress tests.

Management and fit and proper. The Central Bank assesses the board and the managing director under Art 11 of the Act, with Arts 52 and 52a of the Act on Financial Undertakings, and may check qualifying shareholders under Art 5.

Substance. The checklist asks for a company register certificate, articles, an organisation chart with named managers and staff numbers, premises, outsourcing agreements signed before the licence is granted, and an external auditor under Art 15(2). It requires the application to be written and signed by the board.

AML. The checklist asks for an AML risk assessment, procedures, a named responsible manager, staff training, and sanctions screening.

Safeguarding. Art 10 of the Act sets safeguarding, and the Central Bank's rules 88/2020 apply. The checklist asks for a draft agreement with a credit institution for customer funds and a statement of compliance with Art 10.

Professional indemnity. Payment initiation and account information providers need insurance or a comparable guarantee (PSD2 Art 5(2)-(3)), and the checklist asks for the policy.

ICT and DORA. The checklist asks for IT security, operational risk, strong customer authentication and incident reporting. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies through the EEA.

How to get a payment institution license in Iceland: process

  1. Choose the services and capital band.
  2. Request a meeting with the Central Bank. The checklist asks for one before the application is filed.
  3. Prepare a written application signed by the board, with the contents Art 4 of the Act lists. The Central Bank also recommends the EBA authorisation guidelines of 2017 (EBA/GL/2017/09).
  4. File with the Central Bank and pay the fees on its cost page.
  5. The Central Bank notifies its decision no later than 3 months after a satisfactory application (Act 114/2021 Art 12).

We did not find a filing portal or an English application form.

After you get the payment institution license

  • The Central Bank supervises you and lists you among regulated entities.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee on the levy base.
  • To serve another EEA state, notify the Central Bank first (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Keep own funds and safeguarding in place, and report serious operational or security incidents to the Central Bank.
  • Check the Act for change of control rules before any share transfer. We did not read them for this page.

Payment institution in Iceland FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Iceland?

The ISK equivalent of EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the services (Act 114/2021 Art 7).

How much does the Central Bank charge?

Its cost page lists a one-time processing fee of ISK 1,280,000 and a licence fee of ISK 230,000. The page is undated, so confirm the amounts.

How long does a decision take?

Act 114/2021 Art 12 requires the decision no later than 3 months after a satisfactory application. Your preparation time comes on top.

Do I need to meet the Central Bank first?

Its checklist asks applicants to request a meeting before they file.

Is there a small payment institution or AISP route in Iceland?

The Central Bank lists payment institutions with limited authorisation and persons with an exemption under Act 114/2021 among permitted providers. Its registration list covers other activities, such as currency exchange, so the ISK 340,000 registration fee does not obviously apply. Ask the Central Bank which route and fee fit your model.

Does an Icelandic payment institution licence work in the rest of the EEA?

Yes, by notification (PSD2 Art 28).

Do I need an Icelandic company?

The checklist asks for a company register certificate and articles. PSD2 Art 11 requires the head office in the same state as the registered office.

Which language do I file in?

The sources we read do not say. The checklist is in Icelandic, so ask before you prepare translations.
