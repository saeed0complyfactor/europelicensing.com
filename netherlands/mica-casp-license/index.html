Crypto License in the Netherlands

A crypto licence in the Netherlands is an authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) under MiCA, granted by the Netherlands Authority for the Financial Markets (AFM). It is for exchanges, custodians, brokers and transfer services that want an EU passport from the Netherlands. The Dutch transition ended on 30 June 2025, so the old national registration no longer lets you operate.

Who needs a crypto license in the Netherlands?

You need CASP authorisation to provide any of the ten MiCA crypto-asset services as a business: custody, trading platform, exchange for funds or other crypto-assets, order execution, placing, order reception and transmission, advice, portfolio management and transfer services (MiCA Art 3(1)(16)). The applicant needs a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).

Other routes can fit:

  • If you already hold a bank or investment firm authorisation, you may notify the AFM under MiCA Art 60 instead of applying for the services that authorisation covers. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues (Art 60(4)). The notice goes in at least 40 working days before you start. The AFM charges EUR 200 per hour with an anticipated maximum of EUR 50,000 for a notification. See the EMI licence in the Netherlands.
  • If you are authorised in another member state, you passport into the Netherlands under Art 65 and need no Dutch licence.
  • If you handle e-money tokens in ways that count as payment services, you may also need payment institution authorisation. The EBA advised national authorities in June 2025 to require it from 2 March 2026. See the payment institution licence in the Netherlands.

Netherlands crypto license requirements

Capital by class

Class Minimum capital Services
Class 1 EUR 50,000 Order reception and transmission, advice, execution, placing, transfer, portfolio management
Class 2 EUR 125,000 Class 1 plus custody and exchange services
Class 3 EUR 150,000 Class 2 plus operating a trading platform

You hold the higher of the class minimum and one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads, using projected overheads in your first year (MiCA Art 67(1), (2)). Safeguards can be CET1 own funds, insurance or both (Art 67(4)).

Management and fit and proper

Directors need good repute, knowledge, skills, experience and enough time. Qualifying shareholders need good repute (MiCA Art 68(1), (2)). The AFM assesses each management and supervisory board member with its own forms and charges EUR 700 for the propriety assessment and EUR 2,900 for the suitability assessment per person.

Substance

MiCA requires effective management in the EU and an EU-resident director (Art 59(2)). We could not find a written AFM rule on how many Dutch-resident managers or staff it expects, so confirm this at the pre-scan.

AML officer

Art 68(8) requires AML/CFT controls. Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a business-wide risk assessment. See our AML and compliance programme.

Client assets and custody

Client funds go to a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day, in separate accounts (MiCA Art 70). Custody needs a client agreement, a custody policy, a position register and statements at least quarterly (Art 75).

ICT and DORA

Your ICT systems must meet Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, with continuity and recovery plans (MiCA Art 68(7)).

How to get a crypto license in the Netherlands

  1. Map your services to the ten crypto-asset services to fix your capital class.
  2. Request the optional AFM pre-scan before you apply.
  3. Set up a legal entity with the capital and an EU-resident director.
  4. Build the file from the AFM's licence application form and checklist, plus the fit and proper forms for each board member.
  5. Send it to the AFM by email through Cryptshare, following the AFM's naming and referencing instructions.
  6. The AFM acknowledges receipt within 5 working days and has a 25 working day completeness period, with 5 to 20 working days for you to supply missing information.
  7. It then has 40 working days from the notice that the file is complete. Information requests are limited to a maximum of 20 working days, and the AFM notifies its decision within 5 working days.
  8. Within 2 working days of a grant, ESMA is informed for its register (MiCA Art 63).

The AFM says the statutory total is a maximum of around 5 months (105 working days) and often longer. It says an application takes several months, at least five in the best case, and expects about one to three supervisory meetings. Preparation time comes on top of these AFM figures.

After you get the crypto license

  • The AFM enters you in its register, and ESMA lists you in its MiCA register.
  • You meet the ongoing MiCA rules on capital, governance, custody, complaints and conflicts of interest, and pay supervision fees.
  • To serve other EU states, you notify the AFM, which forwards the notice within 10 working days. You may start on receipt of that notice or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (MiCA Art 65).
  • Adding services later needs an extension of authorisation (Art 59(8)).
  • A change in qualifying holders needs regulator approval.

Is the Dutch crypto transition over?

Yes. The AFM announced on 22 April 2024 that firms operating under the national regime before 30 December 2024 had to hold a MiCAR licence or notification by 30 June 2025. ESMA's list of national grandfathering periods gives the Netherlands 6 months under MiCA Art 143(3). A firm without a MiCA licence or notification has had no right to provide crypto-asset services from the Netherlands since then. EU-wide, the MiCA transition ended no later than 1 July 2026 (Art 143(3)).

On 23 November 2026, the ESMA interim MiCA register lists 29 Dutch entities: 22 under Art 63 CASP authorisation and 7 under Art 60 notification. The register is updated weekly, and its latest Dutch update was dated 17 November 2026.

Netherlands crypto license vs other countries

The Netherlands has one of the longer lists of authorised firms and an AFM that publishes its own process length. See the MiCA licence in Lithuania, Malta and Luxembourg. In the Netherlands, the sibling pages are the EMI licence and the payment institution licence. The EU-wide rules sit in our MiCA CASP licence guide.

Crypto license in the Netherlands FAQs

How much does a crypto license cost in the Netherlands?

The AFM charges EUR 200 per hour for a licence application, with a maximum of EUR 100,000, plus EUR 700 and EUR 2,900 per person for the propriety and suitability assessments. Capital is EUR 50,000, 125,000 or 150,000 by class. Running costs come on top.

What are the Netherlands MiCA requirements?

A registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU, an EU-resident director, minimum capital for your class, fit and proper directors and shareholders, AML/CFT controls, DORA-compliant ICT, custody and complaints policies (MiCA Art 59, 62, 67, 68).

How long does a MiCA license take in the Netherlands?

The AFM puts the statutory total at a maximum of about 5 months (105 working days) and says applications often take longer, at least five months in the best case.

Is there a pre-application step?

Yes. The AFM offers an optional pre-scan before you submit.

Is the Dutch MiCA transition over?

Yes. It ended on 30 June 2025 for firms operating under the Dutch national regime.

Can I apply in English?

The AFM publishes its forms in English and accepts applications by email. We did not find a written language rule, so confirm it at the pre-scan.

Can I passport a Dutch crypto licence?

Yes. You notify the AFM, which forwards the notice (MiCA Art 65). The AFM charges nothing for the outgoing notification.

Can I use my EMI licence instead?

An EMI may notify the AFM under Art 60 for custody and transfer of its own e-money tokens. Wider services need a CASP licence.
