EMI License in Norway

An electronic money institution (e-pengeforetak) licence in Norway lets you issue e-money from a Norwegian company. The Financial Supervisory Authority of Norway (Finanstilsynet) grants it under the Financial Institutions Act (finansforetaksloven), the fee is NOK 30,000, and the application and all attachments must be in Norwegian. Norway is in the EEA but outside the EU, so the EU rules reach it through the EEA Agreement.

Who needs an EMI license in Norway?

Issuing e-money in Norway requires a licence as an e-pengeforetak. A balance your customer can hold and spend later is e-money. A product that only moves money for a single payment is a payment service and may fit a payment institution licence instead.

Finanstilsynet's page lists two exemptions: e-money used in limited networks (finansforetaksforskriften 1-7 k) and e-money as an add-on to telecom subscriptions (1-7 l). Some of these need a notification under 1-8.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A payment institution licence, if you do not hold stored value.
  • An EMI authorised in another EEA state, serving Norway by notification through the home regulator.
  • An authorisation elsewhere in the EEA with a Norwegian passport, if you want to avoid a Norwegian-language file.

EMI license requirements in Norway

Capital and own funds. EMD2 Art 4 sets initial capital at EUR 350,000. Your application explains how you will meet initial capital and own funds, and the method you use to calculate minimum own funds. Method D sets at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5).

Management and fit and proper. Board members, the managing director, key function holders and others in actual management need a suitability assessment on form KRT-1157, plus a CV, a police certificate, diplomas and the company's own assessment of the board as a whole. Owners with qualifying holdings above 10% are assessed too.

Board and substance. Finanstilsynet's page says the board must have at least five members (finansforetaksloven 8-4), the managing director cannot sit on the board, and the chair and at least two-thirds of the board must not be employed by the company or its group. The application must show that the company was formed under chapter 7 of the Financial Institutions Act.

AML. The application carries a risk assessment and anti-money laundering routines under the Money Laundering Act (hvitvaskingsloven).

Safeguarding. You describe routines for protecting customer funds and for redeeming e-money. EMD2 Art 7 sets the method options.

ICT and DORA. Governance and control descriptions are aligned with DORA where relevant, and Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies through the EEA.

How to get an EMI license in Norway: process

  1. Choose the route and form the company under chapter 7 of the Financial Institutions Act.
  2. Pay the NOK 30,000 fee and keep the receipt.
  3. Prepare the file in Norwegian: articles, a three-year organisation and business plan, owners and suitability forms (KRT-1157), cover form KRT-1060, the form for finansforetaksforskriften 3-2, and the insurance or guarantee documents if you also offer payment initiation or account information.
  4. File in Altinn, or by email to post@finanstilsynet.no.
  5. Finanstilsynet's normal processing time is about three months from payment of the fee. If the application is incomplete, the clock starts when Finanstilsynet has what it needs (finansforetaksloven 3-2 fourth paragraph).

The page does not mention a pre-application meeting, so ask Finanstilsynet whether one is available.

How much does an EMI license cost in Norway?

Official amounts:

Item Amount Source
Minimum initial capital EUR 350,000, in NOK EMD2 Art 4
Application fee NOK 30,000 Finanstilsynet
Annual supervision fee Not found. Ask Finanstilsynet n/a

The fee page is undated, so confirm the amount with Finanstilsynet before you pay.

Other cost lines, without amounts: your company, a board that meets the five-member rule and independence rules, local staff, an AML officer, a safeguarding account or guarantee, audit, ICT security, Norwegian translation, and legal work. Our fee depends on scope: from EUR 60,000.

After you get the EMI license

  • Finanstilsynet supervises you as a finansforetak and keeps the register.
  • Changes to the board or key functions need suitability assessment, as the application forms show.
  • To serve another EEA state, notify Finanstilsynet first (PSD2 Art 28 via EMD2 Art 3(1)).
  • Material changes to safeguarding are notified in advance (EMD2 Art 3(2)).
  • Check the Financial Institutions Act for change of control rules before any share transfer. We did not read them for this page.

EMI in Norway FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Norway?

EUR 350,000, in NOK (EMD2 Art 4). Finanstilsynet's e-money page asks how you will meet initial capital and own funds.

How long does Finanstilsynet take to decide on an EMI licence?

Finanstilsynet gives a normal processing time of about three months from payment of the fee. The clock starts once the application is complete.

What is the application fee?

NOK 30,000, set by forskrift til finanstilsynsloven 6-13. The fee page is undated, so confirm it.

Can I file in English?

No. Finanstilsynet states that the application and all attachments must be in Norwegian.

Does a Norwegian EMI licence work in the rest of the EEA?

Yes, by notification to Finanstilsynet (PSD2 Art 28 via EMD2 Art 3(1)).

Is there a small EMI licence in Norway?

Finanstilsynet's e-money page describes none. EMD2 Art 9 allows a waiver below EUR 5,000,000 of average e-money, with no passport. Ask Finanstilsynet whether Norway applies it.

Does the board need five members?

Finanstilsynet's page says so, with the managing director outside the board and at least two-thirds of the board independent of the company and its group.

Which form do I use to file?

Altinn, or email to post@finanstilsynet.no. The forms include KRT-1060 and KRT-1157.

Is Norway in the EU, and do the EU e-money rules apply?

Norway is in the EEA, not the EU. Finanstilsynet applies the e-money rules through the Financial Institutions Act, which follows EMD2 and PSD2 through the EEA Agreement. An EMI authorised in Norway can notify its way into EU member states, and EU EMIs can notify into Norway.

What does the application cover?

Finanstilsynet's page lists the articles of association, a three-year organisation and business plan, owners and managers, governance and control systems, capital and own funds, three-year forecasts, the services and money flows, AML routines, and customer fund safeguarding and redemption routines. Each item has to be in Norwegian.

Does the NOK 30,000 fee depend on the outcome?

Finanstilsynet's fee page does not say. It asks for the payment receipt to be attached to the application, so plan to pay when you file.
