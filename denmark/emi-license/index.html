EMI License in Denmark

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence from Finanstilsynet, the Danish Financial Supervisory Authority, lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Denmark and passport them across the EEA. It is for wallets, prepaid and card programmes and neobanks that hold customer balances. A full licence needs EUR 350,000 of capital. Denmark also grants a limited licence for issuers whose outstanding e-money never passes EUR 5 million, and Finanstilsynet charges no fee to apply.

Who needs an EMI license in Denmark?

You need it if you issue electronic money from Denmark: a stored balance that customers hold with you and spend with third parties (EMD2 Art 2(2); Art 10). Prepaid cards, wallets and accounts that hold a spendable balance usually fall in scope. A Danish EMI can also provide the payment services in PSD2 Annex I (EMD2 Art 6(1)).

Another route can fit better:

  • If you only move money and never hold a balance for later spending, a payment institution licence in Denmark needs less capital.
  • If your outstanding e-money will never pass EUR 5 million, you can apply for the limited licence under section 50 of the Payments Act. The licence lapses if the total goes over that figure. You then have 30 days to file for a full EMI licence, and you may keep trading while Finanstilsynet reviews that application.
  • If you already hold an EMI licence in another EEA state, you passport into Denmark by notification and need no Danish licence.
  • If you also want to custody or transfer your own e-money tokens, an EMI can notify under MiCA Art 60(4). Wider crypto services need a Danish CASP authorisation.

EMI license requirements in Denmark

Capital and own funds

A full EMI needs EUR 350,000 of starting capital when it applies, and you show proof of it in the file (Payments Act section 12(2); EMD2 Art 4). Afterwards own funds must stay at or above the higher of that figure and the calculated amount, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(1) and (3)). Finanstilsynet asks you to submit the calculation under all three methods in the Act's annex with a recommendation on which to use.

Legal form, management and fit and proper

The applicant must be an aktieselskab (A/S), anpartsselskab (ApS), andelsselskab med begrænset ansvar or forening med begrænset ansvar, with a board and a management. Board and management must meet fit and proper criteria for competence, reputation and integrity. Owners of qualifying holdings must meet the criteria in section 23 of the Payments Act, and the company must have no close links that would impede supervision.

Substance

Head office and registered seat must be in Denmark, and at least part of the activity must be carried out there (Payments Act section 10; PSD2 Art 11(3)). Finanstilsynet's pages do not set a minimum headcount, so raise your planned local team with the authority before you file.

AML officer and controls

The file needs adequate procedures under the Danish anti-money laundering rules and Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money (EMD2 Art 7(1)) either by segregating them in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). Card-funded money must be safeguarded within five business days of issuance (EMD2 Art 7(1)).

ICT and DORA

The file describes the security policy, risk assessment, incident handling and continuity plans under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e), (f), (h), (j)).

How to get an EMI license in Denmark

  1. Contact the payments team at Finanstilsynet by email at finanstilsynet@ftnet.dk with procedure questions. Its pages do not describe a formal pre-application meeting.
  2. Set up a Danish company in one of the permitted forms and pay in the capital.
  3. Prepare the file using the application form on the Finanstilsynet forms page and the EBA authorisation guidelines it refers to (EBA/GL/2017/09).
  4. Submit through virk.dk with all attachments. The qualifying owners and the fit and proper assessments have their own forms.
  5. Finanstilsynet does not begin processing until it has every required attachment, and it will not approve the application until your procedures are adequate.
  6. It tells you whether the application can be granted within 3 months of receiving it, or within 3 months of receiving the missing information if the file was incomplete (Payments Act section 15).

These are statutory limits. Preparation time and question rounds come on top, and we give you a dated plan after scoping.

How much does an EMI license in Denmark cost?

Item Amount Source
Application fee, full or limited licence None Finanstilsynet
Initial capital, full EMI EUR 350,000, held as equity Payments Act section 12(2); EMD2 Art 4
Annual fee, full EMI, smaller band DKK 25,000 where e-money plus payment volume is under DKK 250 million Finanstilsynet chapter 22
Annual fee, full EMI, middle band DKK 80,000 where volume is DKK 250 million to DKK 100 billion Finanstilsynet chapter 22
Annual fee, limited licence DKK 11,100 Finanstilsynet chapter 22

The annual fee bands use the sum of average outstanding e-money and payment transactions over the last two half-years. A top band of DKK 500,000 applies above DKK 100 billion. The chapter we read is undated, so confirm the current amounts with Finanstilsynet.

The capital stays in your company as equity. Other costs, for which we give no amounts here, include company formation, local directors and staff, audit, a safeguarding account, ICT security, AML tooling, office and professional advisers. Professional indemnity insurance applies where you also provide payment initiation or account information services.

Our fee starts at EUR 60,000.

After you get the EMI license

  • Finanstilsynet lists you in its company register (virksomhedsregister).
  • You report under the Danish prudential and payment-services rules and keep own funds above the required level.
  • You pay the annual fee, invoiced in early December with payment due at year end (Financial Business Act section 369).
  • To serve another EEA state, you notify Finanstilsynet, which forwards the notice within 1 month. The host state assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1) and (2)). You may use distributors abroad but not agents to issue e-money (EMD2 Art 3(4) and (5)).
  • Material changes to safeguarding need advance notice (EMD2 Art 3(2)). A change of qualifying holding is a change of control and needs the regulator's consent before it completes.

Denmark EMI license vs other countries

Denmark has no application fee and a limited tier capped at EUR 5 million of outstanding e-money. To compare, see the EMI licence in Lithuania, the EMI licence in Ireland and the EMI licence in Malta. Within Denmark, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence. For the EU rules behind all of them, read our EMI licence in Europe guide.

EMI license in Denmark FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Denmark?

EUR 350,000 for a full EMI (Payments Act section 12(2); EMD2 Art 4). Finanstilsynet states no minimum capital for the limited e-money licence, which is capped at EUR 5 million of outstanding e-money.

How much does an EMI licence cost in Denmark?

Finanstilsynet charges nothing to apply. Once licensed you pay an annual fee, which for a full EMI starts at DKK 25,000. Add EUR 350,000 of capital and the running costs of a regulated firm: local people, audit, safeguarding, ICT and advisers.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Denmark?

Finanstilsynet has 3 months from a complete application to say whether it can be granted (Payments Act section 15). It does not start the clock until it has all attachments. Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

What is the limited e-money licence in Denmark?

A begrænset tilladelse under section 50 of the Payments Act. It covers issuers whose outstanding e-money never exceeds EUR 5 million. The licence lapses above that figure, and you must then apply for a full EMI licence.

Do I need an office or staff in Denmark?

Your head office and registered seat must be in Denmark, and part of the activity must be carried out there. Finanstilsynet's pages set no minimum staff numbers, so agree your local set-up with it before filing.

Can I file in English?

We could not find a language rule on Finanstilsynet's application pages, and its forms are in Danish. Ask the payments team before you draft.

Can I passport a Danish EMI licence?

A full EMI can, by notifying Finanstilsynet (PSD2 Art 28). The limited licence does not carry a passport (EMD2 Art 9(3)).

Can I buy a Danish EMI instead of applying?

Yes, but the buyer needs the regulator's consent to acquire a qualifying holding. We do not list firms for sale.
