Payment Institution License in Germany

A payment institution (Zahlungsinstitut) licence from BaFin lets a German company provide payment services such as money remittance, acquiring, payment initiation and account servicing, and passport them across the EU and EEA. The licence comes under section 10 of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Initial capital is EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 depending on the services, and we found no small payment institution tier in the ZAG.

Who needs a payment institution license in Germany?

You need one if you provide payment services in Germany on a commercial basis, or on a scale that needs a commercially organised business, and you are not already a credit institution or an EMI (section 10(1) ZAG). The licence can also cover ancillary services such as foreign exchange and data storage, and the operation of payment systems.

Other routes can fit better.

  • Your product holds a balance for later spending. That is e-money and needs the EMI licence in Germany.
  • You only provide account information. That is a registration under section 34 ZAG with a shorter file. BaFin publishes its own tabular overview for it.
  • You are already a payment institution in another EEA state. Passport into Germany by notification.
  • You act for a licensed firm. A payment institution can use agents (section 25 ZAG), so an agent model may avoid your own licence, but the principal remains responsible.
  • You expected a small payment institution route. PSD2 Art 32 lets Member States offer one, and we found none in the ZAG. Confirm with BaFin.

Payment institution license requirements in Germany

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only, and EUR 125,000 for the other payment services (section 12 Nr. 3 ZAG; PSD2 Art 7). If you are also a credit or securities institution, the higher amount applies. Ongoing own funds follow the PSD2 Art 9 methods. Payment initiation and account information providers hold liability cover instead (section 12 Nr. 9 ZAG; sections 16 and 36 ZAG).

Legal form, seat and management

The applicant must be a legal person or a commercial partnership (section 12 Nr. 1 ZAG). BaFin must refuse if the head office is not in Germany or no part of the payment services is provided in Germany (section 12 Nr. 8). The Act requires at least two managing directors, with a limited exception for small companies, and they must be reliable and professionally qualified (section 10(2) ZAG). BaFin also publishes a guidance note on managing directors.

Owners

Qualifying holders must meet the standards of sound and prudent management, and section 2c KWG applies (section 14 ZAG).

Safeguarding

You safeguard customer funds under sections 17 and 18 ZAG, and BaFin refuses a licence if you do not show compliance (section 12 Nr. 10 ZAG; PSD2 Art 10).

AML, governance and ICT

The file covers internal controls, risk management and accounting procedures (section 12 Nr. 6 ZAG), AML controls (PSD2 Art 5(1)), IT continuity and incident procedures (section 10(2) ZAG). BaFin publishes DORA documentation requirements that apply to applicants. After authorisation the ZAG-MaRisk requirements apply (BaFin Circular 07/2024). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for a payment institution license in Germany

  1. Decide which payment services you will provide. BaFin may limit the licence to specific services (section 10(4) ZAG), and your capital tier follows the list.
  2. Contact BaFin before you file. It can answer questions on documents, application fees and the annual levy.
  3. Build the file against BaFin's tabular overview for section 10 ZAG and the DORA documentation requirements. The business plan needs a three-year budget (section 10(2) ZAG).
  4. File in writing. Send two paper copies to BaFin in Bonn (Directorate ZK), or one copy each to BaFin and the Deutsche Bundesbank. Email the application and all attachments to licensing@bafin.de as well.
  5. Answer information requests. BaFin has 3 months from receipt of a complete application. If it asks for missing information and the file is still insufficient 12 months after receipt, it must reject the application (section 10(3) ZAG).
  6. On a grant, BaFin publishes the licence in the Bundesanzeiger and registers you in its ZAG register (sections 10(6) and 10(7) ZAG).

German is the official language of the proceedings. BaFin should request a translation of foreign-language documents and can require a certified one in justified cases. A deadline on BaFin starts only when it has the translation (section 23 VwVfG). Ask BaFin in the pre-application contact which annexes it takes in English.

Other cost lines, with no amounts here: the German company and commercial register entry, local managing directors and staff, certified German translations, audit, the safeguarding account or insurance, professional indemnity or liability cover for payment initiation and account information, AML and ICT systems, and advisers. After BaFin grants the license

  • Register and publication. BaFin publishes the grant in the Bundesanzeiger. You appear in BaFin's register and the EBA register.
  • Reporting and supervision. BaFin and the Bundesbank supervise together. Monthly returns apply under section 29(1) ZAG, and ZAG-MaRisk applies to risk management.
  • Changes. You must tell BaFin without delay about material changes that affect the accuracy of the application (section 10(5) ZAG).
  • Passport and agents. You notify BaFin first, in German or English, to use a branch, agents or cross-border services (PSD2 Art 28; sections 38 to 42 ZAG).
  • Change of control. Qualifying holders must meet the suitability standard of section 2c KWG (section 14 ZAG). Confirm the notification route with BaFin before any share transfer.

Germany compared with other countries

If your product stores value, see the EMI licence in Germany. For crypto-asset services, see the MiCA crypto licence in Germany. The payment institution licence in Austria is the nearest German-language alternative and also has no small tier. For the same licence elsewhere, compare Lithuania, Ireland and Spain, where a low-volume route exists. The EU baseline is on the payment institution licence page.

Payment institution license in Germany FAQs

How much does a payment institution license cost in Germany?

BaFin charges by time spent for a single service. For several or all services the annex shows 13,523 next to "Zeitaufwand", and BaFin should confirm how that applies to you (FinDAGebV annex no. 11.1.1). You also need EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 of capital. our fee range for payment institution licence in Germany

How much capital does a payment institution need in Germany?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only and EUR 125,000 for the other payment services (section 12 Nr. 3 ZAG).

How long does BaFin take to decide?

BaFin has 3 months from receipt of a complete application. If the file is incomplete, the 3 months run from the date it is complete (section 10(3) ZAG). Preparation and your answers to questions come on top.

What is the ZAG Erlaubnis?

It is the written or electronic permission BaFin grants under section 10(1) ZAG to provide payment services. The licence can also cover ancillary services and payment systems.

Can I register as an account information service instead?

Yes, if you only provide account information. Section 34 ZAG sets a registration, not a licence. BaFin publishes a tabular overview for the registration, and the fee is by time spent (FinDAGebV annex no. 11.10).

Is there a small payment institution licence in Germany?

We found no small-institution section in the ZAG. PSD2 Art 32 leaves the option to each Member State. Confirm with BaFin before you plan around a lighter route.

Does the application have to be in German?

German is the official language of administrative proceedings, and BaFin can require translations (section 23 VwVfG). Ask BaFin early which annexes it takes in English.

Can a German payment institution serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes, by notification to BaFin under PSD2 Art 28 (sections 38 to 42 ZAG).
