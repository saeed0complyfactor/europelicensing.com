EMI License in Germany

An electronic money institution (E-Geld-Institut) licence from BaFin lets a German company issue e-money and provide payment services, and passport both across the EU and EEA. The licence comes under section 11 of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), the initial capital is EUR 350,000, and BaFin charges its fee by time spent, with no fixed amount. The language of the proceedings is German.

Who needs an EMI license in Germany?

You need one if you issue electronic money in Germany on a commercial basis, or on a scale that needs a commercially organised business. Wallets, prepaid cards and accounts with a stored balance that the customer can spend later are the usual cases. The licence also covers payment services, so an EMI does not need a separate Zahlungsinstitut licence (section 11(1) ZAG).

Other routes can fit better.

  • You only move money and hold no stored value. The payment institution licence in Germany needs EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 of capital instead of EUR 350,000.
  • You only provide account information. That is a registration under section 34 ZAG, not a licence, and BaFin charges its time.
  • You are already an EMI in another EEA state. Passport into Germany by notification instead of applying to BaFin.
  • You want to start small. We found no small-institution tier in the ZAG. EMD2 Art 9 leaves that option to each Member State, so plan for a full licence and confirm with BaFin before you assume otherwise.
  • You are a crypto firm that also needs e-money tokens. That is a separate MiCA crypto licence in Germany.

EMI license requirements in Germany

Capital and own funds

Initial capital for an e-money institution is EUR 350,000. If you are also a credit institution or securities institution, the higher of the applicable amounts applies (section 12 Nr. 3 ZAG; EMD2 Art 4). Ongoing own funds follow EMD2 Art 5(3), which sets 2% of average outstanding e-money. Your file must also show that the funds are available in Germany (section 12 Nr. 3 ZAG).

Legal form, seat and management

BaFin must refuse the licence if the applicant is not a legal person or a commercial partnership, or if its head office is not in Germany or it carries on no part of its business in Germany (section 12 Nr. 1 and 8 ZAG). The Act requires at least two managing directors, with a limited exception for small companies, and each must be reliable and professionally qualified (sections 11(2) and 10(2) ZAG). BaFin also publishes a guidance note on managing directors that applies to ZAG institutions.

Owners

Holders of qualifying holdings must meet the standards of sound and prudent management, and section 2c KWG applies (section 14 ZAG). Expect BaFin to look through to the ultimate owners in your structure chart.

Safeguarding

You safeguard customer funds under sections 17 and 18 ZAG, and BaFin refuses a licence if you do not demonstrate compliance (section 12 Nr. 10 ZAG; EMD2 Art 7). Methods are segregation, or insurance or a comparable guarantee.

AML, governance and ICT

The file covers internal controls, risk management and accounting procedures (section 12 Nr. 6 ZAG), AML controls (PSD2 Art 5(1)) and ICT arrangements. BaFin publishes DORA documentation requirements that applicants must meet. After authorisation, the ZAG-MaRisk minimum risk management requirements apply (BaFin Circular 07/2024). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for an EMI license in Germany

  1. Define the business model and the payment services you will add. The licence covers only what the file describes (section 11(3) ZAG).
  2. Contact BaFin before you file. BaFin says it can answer questions on documents, application fees and the annual levy.
  3. Build the file against BaFin's tabular overview for section 11 ZAG, which tells you to structure the application along the Act. Add the DORA documentation. The business plan needs a budget for three years (section 10(2) ZAG).
  4. File in writing. BaFin asks for two paper copies to its Bonn office (Directorate ZK), or one copy each to BaFin and the Deutsche Bundesbank. Send the application and every attachment by email to licensing@bafin.de too. Other secure transmission is possible if BaFin agrees first.
  5. Answer information requests. The clock is 3 months from a complete application (section 10(3) ZAG). If BaFin asks for missing information and the file is still insufficient 12 months after receipt, BaFin must reject it.
  6. On a grant, BaFin publishes the licence in the Bundesanzeiger and adds you to its register of ZAG institutions. Commercial register entries need proof of the licence (sections 10(6), 10(7) and 11(5) ZAG).

German is the official language of the proceedings. BaFin should request a translation of foreign-language documents, can require a certified one in justified cases, and a deadline on BaFin starts only when it has the translation (section 23 VwVfG). Ask BaFin in the pre-application contact which annexes it accepts in English.

EMI license cost in Germany

BaFin's fee follows the annex to the FinDAGebV (the fee ordinance that replaced the older FinDAGKostV). Entry no. 11.1.2 covers the section 11 licence and says the fee is charged by time spent, with no fixed amount.

After BaFin grants the license

  • Register and publication. BaFin publishes the grant in the Bundesanzeiger, and the institution appears in BaFin's ZAG register and the EBA register.
  • Reporting and supervision. BaFin and the Bundesbank supervise together. Monthly returns apply under section 29(1) ZAG, and the ZAG-MaRisk requirements apply to your risk management.
  • Changes. You must tell BaFin without delay about material changes that affect the accuracy of the application (section 11(4) ZAG).
  • Passport. You notify BaFin first, in German or English, and BaFin forwards the notice (PSD2 Art 28; sections 38 to 42 ZAG). E-money distribution abroad goes through distributors, not agents (EMD2 Art 3).
  • Change of control. Qualifying holders must meet the suitability standard of section 2c KWG (section 14 ZAG). Confirm the notification route with BaFin before any share transfer.

Germany compared with other countries

If you only provide payments, see the payment institution licence in Germany. For stablecoins and other crypto services, BaFin also issues the MiCA crypto licence in Germany. Austria is the nearest German-language alternative, and its EMI licence in Austria also needs a local company and a German-speaking manager. For the same licence elsewhere, compare the EMI licence in Lithuania, Ireland and Spain, where the Banco de España charges no application fee. The EU baseline is on the EMI licence page.

EMI license in Germany FAQs

How much does an EMI license cost in Germany?

BaFin charges by time spent under no. 11.1.2 of the FinDAGebV annex, so there is no fixed fee to quote. You also need EUR 350,000 of initial capital and a German company with local management. Our fee starts at EUR 60,000.

How much capital do you need for an EMI license in Germany?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (section 12 Nr. 3 ZAG; EMD2 Art 4). Own funds then follow the 2% rule of EMD2 Art 5(3).

How long does BaFin take to decide?

BaFin has 3 months from receipt of a complete application. If the file is incomplete, the 3 months run from the date it is complete (section 10(3) ZAG). Your preparation time and the time you take to answer questions come on top.

What is the BaFin lizenz or ZAG Erlaubnis?

It is the written or electronic permission BaFin grants under section 11(1) ZAG for e-money business. The licence also covers payment services, certain ancillary services and other permitted business.

Does the application have to be in German?

German is the official language of administrative proceedings. BaFin should ask for a translation of foreign-language documents and in justified cases can require a certified one (section 23 VwVfG). Ask BaFin early which annexes it takes in English.

Can a non-resident founder hold a German EMI?

The ZAG sets no nationality rule in the sections we checked. It requires a legal person with its head office in Germany and part of its business in Germany, funds available in Germany and at least two managing directors (sections 10(2) and 12 ZAG).

Is there a small EMI licence in Germany?

We found no small-institution tier in the ZAG. EMD2 Art 9 leaves the option to each Member State. Confirm with BaFin before you plan around a lighter route.

Can a German EMI serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes, by notification to BaFin under PSD2 Art 28, applied through EMD2 Art 3(1).
