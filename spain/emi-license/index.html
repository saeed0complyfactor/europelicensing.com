EMI License in Spain

An electronic money institution (EMI, or entidad de dinero electrónico) licence from the Banco de España lets a Spanish company issue e-money and provide payment services, and passport both across the EU and EEA. The Banco de España charges no application fee. The application and key legal documents go in Spanish, and if the 3-month clock runs out without a decision, the application counts as refused.

Who needs an EMI license in Spain?

You need one if you issue electronic money in Spain on a professional basis. The Banco de España gives examples: companies that offer payment or e-money accounts used to pay and receive, or that issue credit, debit or prepaid cards (guide 1.2). Wallets and prepaid products with a spendable balance usually fall in scope.

An EMI may also provide payment services, with or without e-money, without a separate payment institution licence. The application must list every payment service you will offer, and the authorisation covers only those (guide section 2).

Other routes can fit better.

  • You only transmit money and hold no stored value. The payment institution licence in Spain has lower capital.
  • You are already an EMI in another EEA state. Passport into Spain.
  • You are small. The guide describes a low-volume registration route for payment services only and none for e-money. EMD2 Art 9 leaves the small EMI tier to each Member State, so plan for full authorisation.
  • You only provide account information. That is a registration, not a licence, and has its own 3-month procedure (guide 1.8).

EMI license requirements in Spain

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is EUR 350,000 when you provide e-money services, whether or not you also provide payment services (guide 2.5; EMD2 Art 4). The Banco de España notes that initial capital is not the same as share capital (guide 2.5). Own funds for e-money are the higher of EUR 350,000 and 2% of average e-money in circulation over the previous six months. If you offer unrelated payment services, you add own funds under the payment services method (guide 2.3).

Substance in Spain

You confirm that the registered office, the address of central administration and the actual management are in Spain (guide 2.1). Once authorised and registered in the Mercantile Register, you must apply for entry in the Banco de España's Special Registry, and you may not start before that entry (guide 1.10).

Fit and proper and owners

The file covers directors, managers and holders of qualifying stakes under RD 778/2012 and RD 736/2019. The Banco de España asks for reports from other supervisors where a controlling entity operates in another Member State (guide 1.7).

Safeguarding

Funds received for e-money issued are segregated or covered by an insurance policy or guarantee (EMD2 Art 7(1); PSD2 Art 10(1)). Card-funded money must be safeguarded within 5 business days of issue (EMD2 Art 7(1)).

AML, governance and ICT

SEPBLAC's Executive Service gives a report on your AML arrangements as part of the procedure (guide 1.7). The file also covers governance, internal control, business continuity, security policy and DORA ICT arrangements (PSD2 Art 5(1)). The AML manual must be in Spanish (guide 1.5). How to apply for an EMI license in Spain

  1. Define the business model precisely. The Banco de España warns that processing tends to run long when the programme of operations is vague or the documents are incomplete (guide 1.3).
  2. Request a meeting before filing, if you want one. You send the pre-application form from the "Tramitación" section of the EMI authorisation procedure on the Banco de España's electronic office, with the documents it lists (guide 1.3). Use the Banco de España EMI authorisation page.
  3. Fill in the EMI authorisation form and annexes. The form follows the EBA Guidelines on authorisation (EBA/GL/2017/09).
  4. Prepare Spanish versions of the application, the draft by-laws, the framework contract and terms, the customer ombudsman rules and the AML manual. Other documents may go in English, though the Banco de España can ask for translations (guide 1.5).
  5. File through the Electronic Registry, in person at calle Alcalá 48 in Madrid, at a branch, or by post. Electronic filing is recommended (guide 1.4).
  6. Answer requests for information. The 3-month clock starts when the application is complete. It can be suspended while you correct gaps, and for mandatory reports for up to 3 months (guide 1.8). If it ends with no decision, the application is deemed denied.
  7. If approved, satisfy any conditions, complete the Mercantile Register steps and apply for entry in the Special Registry before you start (guide 1.9 and 1.10).

The authorisation can be revoked if you do not use it within 12 months (guide 1.10).

EMI license cost in Spain

The application and the procedure are free of charge, and no deposit is required (guide 1.4).

After the Banco de España grants the license

  • Special Registry. You cannot start until you are entered in it (guide 1.10).
  • Reporting and supervision. The Banco de España supervises you as an EMI. You notify it of material changes to the information you filed, without delay (guide 1.5).
  • Passport. You notify the Banco de España of the states and services. It forwards the notice within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(2)). E-money distribution abroad goes through distributors, not agents (EMD2 Art 3(4), (5)).
  • Change of control. Changes in qualifying holdings go to the Banco de España. Confirm the notification route with it before any share transfer, because the guide we used does not set it out.

Spain compared with other countries

If you only provide payments, see the payment institution licence in Spain. For crypto services you go to the CNMV instead, covered in the MiCA crypto licence in Spain. For the same licence elsewhere, compare the EMI licence in Lithuania, Ireland and Bulgaria, where the regulator charges a fee and requires Bulgarian-language documents. The EU baseline is on the EMI licence page.

EMI license in Spain FAQs

How much does an EMI license cost in Spain?

The Banco de España charges no application fee (guide 1.4). You need EUR 350,000 of initial capital and a Spanish entity with local management. our fee range for EMI licence in Spain

How much capital do you need for an EMI license in Spain?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMD2 Art 4; guide 2.5). Own funds then stay at or above the higher of EUR 350,000 and 2% of average e-money in circulation over the previous six months (guide 2.3).

How long does the Banco de España take to decide?

The deadline is 3 months from a complete application. It can be suspended for corrections and for mandatory reports, up to 3 months for the reports. If it passes without an express decision, the application is deemed denied (guide 1.8). Your preparation time comes before the clock starts.

How do I get an EMI license in Spain?

You set up a Spanish company, fill in the Banco de España's EMI authorisation form with a programme of operations and the annexes, file it electronically or in person, then register in the Special Registry before starting (guide 1.4 to 1.10).

Can a non-resident founder hold a Spanish EMI?

The guide requires the registered office, central administration and actual management in Spain (guide 2.1). It does not set a nationality rule for shareholders in the text we checked.

Does the application have to be in Spanish?

The application, by-laws, framework contract and terms, ombudsman rules and AML manual must be in Spanish. Other documents may be in English, with translation possible at the Banco de España's request (guide 1.5).

Can a Spanish EMI serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes, by notification under PSD2 Art 28, applied by EMD2 Art 3(1).

Is there a small EMI licence in Spain?

The guide describes none for e-money. It does describe a low-volume registration for payment services only, covered on the payment institution page.
