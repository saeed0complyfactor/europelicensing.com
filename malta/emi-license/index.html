EMI License in Malta

A Maltese EMI licence lets you issue electronic money and provide payment services from Malta, then passport both across the EU and EEA. The Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA) grants it under the Financial Institutions Act. Plan for substance first: the law requires your head office and registered office in Malta and at least two individuals directing the business from there.

Who needs an EMI licence in Malta?

You need one if you issue electronic money in or from Malta on a regular basis. The Financial Institutions Act lists the issuing of electronic money as activity 10 in its First Schedule and requires a licence before you start (Art 4(1)). Prepaid cards, wallets that hold a spendable balance and similar products usually fall inside it.

A licence is the wrong fit in some cases:

  • You only move money and never hold a balance for later spending. A payment institution licence in Malta has lower capital.
  • You want to offer e-money tokens as well as e-money. MiCA lets an EMI issue e-money tokens after a white paper notification (MiCA Art 48), but other crypto services need a MiCA CASP authorisation. An Art 60 notification covers only custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues.
  • Another EEA regulator has already authorised you. A licensee from another Member State may use its European rights in Malta instead of applying (FIA Art 4(1), proviso). You notify your home regulator, not the MFSA.
  • You are small. EMD2 Art 9 allows Member States to waive full authorisation for firms with average outstanding e-money up to EUR 5,000,000. We could not confirm an official Maltese small-EMI tier, so do not plan around one.

An authorised EMI may also provide payment services, appoint distributors to distribute and redeem e-money, and use agents for payment services (FIA Art 8A).

Requirements for an EMI licence in Malta

Capital and own funds

EMD2 Art 4 sets initial capital of at least EUR 350,000. The FIA says the amount is set in the MFSA's rules and adds that own funds must never fall below initial capital or the calculated own-funds amount, whichever is higher (FIA Art 5(1)(a), Art 5A). For e-money the calculated amount is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)), measured over the preceding six months.

Management and fit and proper

At least two individuals must effectively direct the business from Malta (FIA Art 5(1)(b)). The MFSA must be satisfied that qualifying shareholders, controllers and the people directing the business are suitable (Art 5(1)(d)) and that governance is sound, with clear lines of responsibility and internal controls proportionate to the business (Art 5(1)(c)).

Substance

A licensed financial institution must have its head office and registered office in Malta and carry out at least part of its licensable activity there (FIA Art 5(7)). The MFSA may require a separate entity if other business you run puts the licensed business at risk (Art 5(6)).

Safeguarding

An EMI safeguards all funds received in exchange for e-money (FIA Art 10B(1); EMD2 Art 7). The MFSA's Financial Institutions Rules set the detail (Art 10B(2)). It must redeem e-money at par on request and may not pay interest (FIA Art 8F; EMD2 Art 12).

ICT, DORA and AML

Under FIA Art 11A you build a framework to manage operational and security risk, with incident management procedures, alongside DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. The application file also covers internal AML controls under PSD2 Art 5(1)(k), applied to EMIs by EMD2 Art 3(1). Our AML and compliance programme covers the documents.

Audit and returns

You appoint an approved statutory auditor each year (FIA Art 18(1)) and submit periodic and ad hoc information in the form the MFSA prescribes (Art 14).

How to apply for an EMI licence in Malta

  1. Map your product to FIA First Schedule item 10 and, if relevant, item 4, so you know which application fee and own-funds method apply.
  2. Set up a Maltese company with a registered office and a head office in Malta, and recruit at least two individuals who will direct it from there.
  3. Pay in the initial capital and build the application file to the MFSA's Financial Institutions Rules. FIA Art 4(2) requires the form, information and conditions the Rules prescribe. The MFSA publishes them as the Financial Institutions Rulebook, with FIR01 covering application procedures. See the MFSA financial institutions page.
  4. Pay the application fee on submission (S.L. 376.03 reg 3). It is not refundable (reg 6).
  5. Answer the MFSA's questions. The MFSA can require any information it considers necessary to decide (FIA Art 4(3)).
  6. Receive the decision. The MFSA has 3 months from receipt, or from the date you supply the missing information or comply with the form requirements, whichever is later (Art 5(2)). It must give written reasons for a refusal (Art 5(3)). If it does not decide in time, the law treats that as a refusal (Art 5(5)).
  7. On grant, pay the first annual supervisory fee, open safeguarding arrangements and file any passport notifications.

The statutory clock runs from a complete application. Time spent preparing the file and answering questions comes on top.

How much does an EMI licence cost in Malta?

Regulator fees

Item Amount Source
Application fee, e-money only EUR 10,000 S.L. 376.03 reg 3(1)(a)
Application fee, e-money and payment services EUR 15,000 S.L. 376.03 reg 3(1)(b)
First annual supervisory fee, e-money only EUR 25,000, prorated to the end of the calendar year of grant, paid on grant reg 4(3)-(4), reg 5(1)
Later annual supervisory fee, e-money only EUR 25,000 plus the higher of 0.02% of total assets or 0.01% of average daily outstanding e-money, with that second part capped at EUR 250,000 reg 4(3)
Later annual fee, e-money and payment services EUR 35,000 plus the higher of 0.02% of total assets or 0.0003% of payment volume plus 0.01% of average daily outstanding e-money, with that second part capped at EUR 250,000 reg 4(5)
Payment dates Two instalments, 1 January (50% of last year's fee) and 30 July (the balance) reg 5(2)

These regulations came into force on 1 January 2025 (L.N. 366 of 2024). Fees are not refundable and are not prorated, apart from the first-year proration above (reg 6). Third-party figures that quote a lower application fee predate this change or describe another licence category.

Capital

Initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4), plus ongoing own funds under the method the MFSA applies.

These vary by firm, and we do not quote amounts here:

  • Local directors and staff, including the two individuals directing the business from Malta

  • Professional indemnity insurance, where your services require it
  • Technology, ICT and DORA compliance
  • AML tools and compliance resources

Our fee starts at EUR 60,000.

After authorisation

  • Register entry: the MFSA enters you in its public register of financial institutions, which also lists agents and branches (FIA Art 8D).
  • Reporting: you submit periodic and ad hoc information as the MFSA prescribes, and an annual audit (Art 14, Art 18).
  • The supervision fee is payable in two instalments each year, as in the cost table.
  • Your Maltese licence is valid in all Member States (FIA Art 4(6)). To serve a state, notify your home regulator of the states, services and any agents or branches (PSD2 Art 28(1), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). The home regulator forwards the notification within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(2)). E-money may be distributed abroad through distributors, but not issued through agents (EMD2 Art 3(4)-(5)).
  • For agents and distributors in Malta, you tell the MFSA the agent's or distributor's details and AML controls before use (FIA Art 8A).
  • Tell the MFSA at once of changes to the facts in your application (FIA Art 5(1), final proviso). The MFSA may add or vary licence conditions at any time (Art 5(4)).

Malta vs other countries

If you want a lower-substance route with a long track record, compare the EMI licence in Lithuania and the EMI licence in Ireland. For the Mediterranean alternative, see the EMI licence in Cyprus, where the Central Bank runs a two-stage process. In Malta, the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP authorisation sit alongside this one. The general rules are in our EMI licence guide.

EMI in Malta FAQs

How much capital do you need for an EMI licence in Malta?

At least EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4). The Financial Institutions Act leaves the exact amount to the MFSA's rules (Art 5(1)(a)), and EU law sets the floor. After authorisation, your own funds must cover at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

How much is the MFSA application fee for an EMI?

EUR 10,000 for e-money alone and EUR 15,000 if you apply for e-money and payment services together (S.L. 376.03 reg 3, in force since 1 January 2025). The fee is not refundable.

How long does an EMI licence take in Malta?

The MFSA must decide within 3 months of receiving an application that complies with the prescribed form, or of receiving the missing information if that is later (FIA Art 5(2)). Preparing the file and answering questions comes on top, and we do not quote a timeline.

Do you need an office in Malta?

Yes. The head office and registered office must be in Malta, you must carry out at least part of the licensed activity there (FIA Art 5(7)), and at least two individuals must direct the business from Malta (Art 5(1)(b)).

Can a Maltese EMI serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes. The licence is valid in all Member States (FIA Art 4(6)). You file passport notifications through the MFSA before serving a new state.

Is there a small EMI licence in Malta?

EMD2 Art 9 lets each Member State waive full authorisation below EUR 5,000,000 of average outstanding e-money. We could not confirm an official Maltese small-EMI regime, so check with the MFSA before planning on one.

What happens if the MFSA does not decide in time?

The Act treats a failure to decide within the 3-month period as a refusal (FIA Art 5(5)). A refusal must come with written reasons when the MFSA does decide (Art 5(3)).

Can an EMI in Malta also issue stablecoins?

An EMI may offer e-money tokens after notifying a crypto-asset white paper (MiCA Art 48(1)). Other crypto services need a separate MiCA authorisation.
