Crypto License in Malta

A Maltese crypto licence today means a MiCA authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP), granted by the Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA) under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Act. The old Virtual Financial Assets (VFA) route has closed to new entrants and the transition for existing VFA licensees ended on 1 July 2026. A Maltese CASP authorisation can be passported to every EU and EEA state.

Who needs a crypto licence in Malta?

You need one if you provide crypto-asset services in or from Malta, such as custody, an exchange, a trading platform, order execution, transfers, advice or portfolio management. The Act bars anyone from providing those services in Malta unless they are authorised under MiCA Art 63 or are an entity allowed to provide them under MiCA Art 60 (Cap. 647 Art 26(1)).

Other routes can fit better:

  • Notification under MiCA Art 60. Credit institutions, investment firms, market operators, UCITS management companies and AIF managers can notify the MFSA at least 40 working days before starting. An EMI can notify for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues. See the EMI licence in Malta.
  • Authorisation in another member state. A CASP authorised elsewhere can serve Malta through a passport notification (Cap. 647 Art 26(2)).
  • Services outside MiCA. Some products stay under other regimes, so confirm the classification before you apply.
  • A former VFA licensee who has not been authorised under MiCA. The Maltese transition has ended, so you cannot continue as before.

Requirements for a MiCA licence in Malta

Legal form and presence

You must be a legal person with a registered office in a Member State, with the place of effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (MiCA Art 59(2)). The application goes to the MFSA when Malta is your home state (Cap. 647 Art 28).

Capital and prudential safeguards

Minimum capital is EUR 50,000 for class 1 services, EUR 125,000 for class 2 (custody, exchange) and EUR 150,000 for class 3 (trading platform). You hold the higher of that figure and a quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads (MiCA Art 67(1)). A firm under a year old uses projected overheads (Art 67(2)).

Management and fit and proper

Management body members need good repute, knowledge, skills, experience and enough time. Qualifying shareholders need good repute (MiCA Art 68).

Governance, ICT and AML

You need ICT and business continuity arrangements under DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, an AML and counter-terrorist financing framework, and records kept for five years (MiCA Art 68). Conflict-of-interest, complaints, outsourcing and custody rules apply under Art 71 to 75.

Client assets

Client crypto-assets are kept separate from your own and not used for your own account. Client funds go to a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day (MiCA Art 70).

How to apply for a MiCA licence in Malta

  1. Map your services to the MiCA classes, so you know your capital and fee.
  2. Set up a company with an EU place of effective management and at least one EU-resident director.
  3. Prepare the MiCA Art 62 file, covering the business plan, governance, ICT, AML, custody and complaints policies, and the people and shareholders.
  4. Submit it to the MFSA under Cap. 647 Art 28 and pay the application fee on submission (S.L. 647.01 reg 6). The fee is not refundable (reg 7).
  5. The MFSA acknowledges receipt within 5 working days and checks completeness within 25 working days (MiCA Art 63(1)-(2)).
  6. It decides within 40 working days of a complete application. It can ask for more information by the 20th working day, and the clock pauses for up to 20 working days while you reply (Art 63(9) and 63(12)).
  7. On authorisation, pay the first supervisory fee, then file any passport notifications.

The simplified procedure for former VFA licensees applied only to applications lodged between 30 December 2024 and 1 July 2026 (Cap. 647 Art 58(4)). New applicants use the full procedure.

After authorisation

  • Register: authorised CASPs appear in ESMA's interim MiCA register and on the MFSA's own register.
  • Reporting and fees: the MFSA collects the annual supervisory fee on 30 July each year (S.L. 647.01 reg 6(7)).
  • Passport: you send the MFSA the member states, services and start date. It forwards the notification within 10 working days, and you may start on receipt of that communication or by the 15th calendar day (MiCA Art 65; Cap. 647 Art 32).
  • Changes: management body changes and proposed acquisitions of a CASP are notified to the MFSA (Cap. 647 Art 33 sets out the acquisition assessment).
  • Extension: to add services, you apply to extend your authorisation and pay the reduced fee in reg 6(3).

Status of the Maltese regime on 23 November 2026

The Maltese Act let VFA licensees continue under the VFA Act until 1 July 2026 or until their MiCA application was granted or refused, whichever came first (Cap. 647 Art 58(3)). Any VFA licence was cancelled automatically on 2 July 2026 or on the MiCA decision, if earlier (VFA Act Art 66(2), inserted by Cap. 647 Art 63). ESMA's public statement of 23 June 2026 says unauthorised CASPs must stop onboarding EU clients and wind down.

ESMA's interim MiCA register lists 24 distinct entities with Malta as home Member State, in the file downloaded on 23 November 2026. They include Maltese entities of OKX, Gemini, Gate and Blockchain.com. Check the live register before you quote a number. This page does not cover MFSA statements on its supervisory approach.

Malta vs other countries

The same MiCA rules apply everywhere, but fees, practice and substance expectations differ. Compare the crypto licence in Cyprus, where CySEC is the authority, and the CASP authorisation in Lithuania or Ireland. In Malta, the EMI licence and payment institution licence sit alongside this one. The general framework is in our MiCA CASP licence guide.

Crypto licence in Malta FAQs

Which authority grants a crypto licence in Malta?

The MFSA is the competent authority under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Act (Cap. 647 Art 2 and 4). It authorises CASPs under MiCA Art 63.

What is the MFSA application fee for a CASP?

EUR 10,000 for class 1 services, EUR 20,000 for class 2 and EUR 25,000 for a trading platform. If you offer services from several classes, only the highest fee applies (S.L. 647.01 reg 6(1)). Fees are not refundable.

How much capital does a Maltese CASP need?

EUR 50,000 for class 1, EUR 125,000 for class 2 and EUR 150,000 for class 3, or a quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads if higher (MiCA Art 67).

How long does MiCA authorisation take in Malta?

The MFSA has 25 working days to check completeness and then 40 working days to decide (MiCA Art 63). Information requests pause the clock for up to 20 working days. Preparation time comes on top, and we do not quote a timeline.

Is the VFA licence still valid in Malta?

No. VFA licences were cancelled automatically on 2 July 2026 or on the MiCA decision, whichever came first (VFA Act Art 66(2), inserted by Cap. 647 Art 63). Former VFA holders need a MiCA authorisation.

Can a Maltese CASP serve other EU countries?

Yes. MiCA authorisation is valid across the EU. You notify the MFSA, which informs host authorities within 10 working days (MiCA Art 65).

Do you need a physical office in Malta?

MiCA needs a registered office in a Member State, effective management in the EU and an EU-resident director (Art 59(2)). Authorisation does not require a physical presence in host states. We have not confirmed any MFSA substance expectation beyond those rules.

Do I need a separate licence for e-money tokens?

Only credit institutions and EMIs can issue e-money tokens (MiCA Art 48). A CASP that custodies or trades them may also need a payment licence, so check your product against PSD2.
