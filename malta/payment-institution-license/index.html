Payment Institution License in Malta

A Maltese payment institution (PI) licence lets you provide PSD2 payment services such as transfers, card acquiring, remittance or payment initiation from Malta, and passport them across the EU and EEA. The Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA) grants it under the Financial Institutions Act. Plan for a real Maltese presence: head office and registered office in Malta, and at least two people directing the business from there.

Who needs a payment institution licence in Malta?

You need a licence before you regularly provide payment services in or from Malta. The Act lists payment services as activity 4 of its First Schedule, and the Second Schedule sets out the services themselves (FIA Art 4(1)). The usual cases:

  • Executing transfers, direct debits or card payments for customers
  • Acquiring card payments or issuing payment instruments
  • Money remittance
  • Payment initiation

Other routes can fit better:

  • Account information only. You register instead of applying for a licence (FIA Art 4(1A)), with a EUR 1,000 application fee and a EUR 5,000 annual registration fee (S.L. 376.03 reg 5A).
  • You hold customer balances for later spending. That is e-money and needs an EMI licence in Malta.
  • You already hold a licence from another EEA state. You may use your European rights in Malta without a second authorisation (FIA Art 4(1), proviso).
  • You sit under a regulated firm as its agent. A payment institution may use agents after telling the MFSA (FIA Art 8A).
  • Small payment institution. PSD2 Art 32 lets states exempt firms with a monthly average up to EUR 3 million, with no passport. We could not confirm an official Maltese regime.

Requirements for a payment institution licence in Malta

Capital and own funds

PSD2 Art 7 sets initial capital by service: EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money remittance. The MFSA's rules fix the Maltese figure within that framework (FIA Art 5(1)(a)). Own funds must never fall below the higher of initial capital and the amount calculated under Method A, B or C (FIA Art 5A; PSD2 Art 9).

Management and fit and proper

At least two individuals must effectively direct the business from Malta (FIA Art 5(1)(b)). Qualifying shareholders, controllers and those directing the business must be suitable (Art 5(1)(d)), and governance must be sound and proportionate (Art 5(1)(c)).

Substance

The head office and registered office must be in Malta, and you carry out at least part of the licensed activity there (FIA Art 5(7)). The MFSA may ask for a separate entity if other business you run could harm the payment business (Art 5(6)).

Insurance for payment initiation and account information

Payment initiation and account information providers must hold professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee covering the territories in which they offer services, recalculated at least annually (FIA Art 5(1B), 5(1C)).

Safeguarding

For payment services 2(a) to (f) of the Second Schedule, you safeguard all funds received from customers or through another provider (FIA Art 10B(1)). PSD2 Art 10(1) allows segregation in a credit institution or central bank account, secure liquid low-risk assets, or insurance or a guarantee from outside the group. The MFSA's rules set the detail (Art 10B(2)).

ICT, DORA and AML

You maintain a framework to manage operational and security risk with incident procedures (FIA Art 11A), alongside DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. The file also describes AML internal controls (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for a payment institution licence in Malta

  1. Map each product feature to the payment services list, so you know your capital tier, own-funds method and fee.
  2. Set up a Maltese company with a registered and head office in Malta, and recruit the people who will direct it from there.
  3. Pay in the capital and build the file to the MFSA's Financial Institutions Rules, which prescribe the form and information (FIA Art 4(2)). The MFSA publishes them as the Financial Institutions Rulebook. See the MFSA financial institutions page.
  4. Pay the application fee on submission. It is not refundable (S.L. 376.03 reg 3 and 6).
  5. Answer MFSA questions. The MFSA may ask for any information it needs (FIA Art 4(3)).
  6. Receive the decision within 3 months of receipt or of the missing information, whichever is later (Art 5(2)). A refusal comes with written reasons (Art 5(3)). Silence counts as a refusal (Art 5(5)).
  7. Pay the first annual supervisory fee on grant, put safeguarding in place and file passport notifications.

The clock starts when the application meets the prescribed form. Preparation and question rounds come on top.

After authorisation

  • Register entry: the MFSA lists you, your agents and any branches in its public register (FIA Art 8D).
  • Reporting: you file periodic and ad hoc returns in the form the MFSA prescribes (Art 14) and appoint an approved statutory auditor each year (Art 18).
  • Supervision fee: due in two instalments each year, as in the cost table.
  • Passport: your licence is valid in all Member States (Art 4(6)). You notify your home regulator of the states, services and any agents or branches. The home regulator forwards the notice within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1)-(2)). For an agent or branch the home regulator decides within 3 months (Art 28(3)).
  • Agents in Malta: you give the MFSA the agent's details and AML controls before use (FIA Art 8A).
  • Changes: report changes to the facts behind your application without delay (FIA Art 5(1), final proviso). The MFSA may vary licence conditions (Art 5(4)).

Malta vs other countries

For a payment institution you can also compare Lithuania and Ireland. In the same region, see the payment institution licence in Cyprus. If you also hold balances, the EMI licence in Malta covers both e-money and payment services, and the MiCA CASP authorisation covers crypto services. The general rules are in our payment institution licence guide.

Payment institution in Malta FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Malta?

It depends on the services: EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money remittance only (PSD2 Art 7). Account information providers register and hold no initial capital.

What is the MFSA application fee for a payment institution?

EUR 10,000 for payment services, EUR 15,000 if you also apply for e-money (S.L. 376.03 reg 3, in force from 1 January 2025). Registering as an account information provider costs EUR 1,000. All fees are non-refundable.

How long does a payment institution licence take in Malta?

The MFSA must decide within 3 months of receiving an application that meets the prescribed form, or of the missing information if later (FIA Art 5(2)). Preparing the file comes on top, and we do not quote a timeline.

Do you need an office in Malta for a payment institution?

Yes. The head office and registered office must be in Malta, you must carry out part of the licensed activity there (FIA Art 5(7)), and at least two individuals must direct the business from Malta (Art 5(1)(b)).

Can I register as an account information provider instead?

If you only provide account information services, you apply for registration, not a licence (FIA Art 4(1A)). Registration costs EUR 1,000 to apply and EUR 5,000 a year (reg 5A), and you hold professional indemnity insurance (Art 5(1B)).

Can a Maltese payment institution serve other EU countries?

Yes. The licence is valid in all Member States (FIA Art 4(6)), after you notify the MFSA of the states and services (PSD2 Art 28).

What happens if the MFSA does not decide in time?

The Act treats a missed 3-month deadline as a refusal (FIA Art 5(5)). A decision to refuse must give written reasons (Art 5(3)).
