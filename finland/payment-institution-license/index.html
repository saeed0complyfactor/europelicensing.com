Payment Institution License in Finland

A payment institution (maksulaitos) licence in Finland lets you provide payment services such as money remittance, payment initiation and card issuing or acquiring from a Finnish company. The Financial Supervisory Authority (FIN-FSA, Finanssivalvonta) grants it under the Act on Payment Institutions. FIN-FSA ties the need for authorisation to EUR 3 million of monthly payment volume, to payment initiation services, and to any plan to operate in another EEA state.

Who needs a payment institution license in Finland?

Providing payment services in Finland needs FIN-FSA authorisation in three situations, according to FIN-FSA's page: completed transactions that average more than EUR 3 million per month, a payment initiation service, and an intention to operate in another EEA state.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A smaller operation below the EUR 3 million average, where FIN-FSA's page does not require authorisation. This does not allow payment initiation and does not give you a passport. Read the Act, and ask FIN-FSA how to register or notify.
  • An institution authorised in another EEA state that notifies FIN-FSA to serve Finland. FIN-FSA's schedule lists EUR 585 for registering an agent in the EEA and EUR 585 for a free provision of services notification, and EUR 2,400 for a branch.
  • An EMI licence if you issue stored value.

Payment institution license requirements in Finland

Capital and own funds. Initial capital depends on the services: EUR 125,000 for a full payment institution, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation, EUR 20,000 for money transmission only (PSD2 Art 7, listed on FIN-FSA's page). Own funds must be at least the higher of initial capital and the calculated amount (PSD2 Art 8 and 9).

Management and fit and proper. FIN-FSA asks for evidence that management and the board are fit and proper, plus information on major shareholders.

Substance. The Finnish sources we read do not set a number of local directors or an office size. PSD2 Art 11(3) requires the head office to be in the same state as the registered office. FIN-FSA says an authorisation application always involves at least one authorisation meeting, as a rule at the applicant's premises.

AML. FIN-FSA lists customer identification, due diligence and anti-money laundering procedures in the application.

Safeguarding. For services 1 to 6, customer funds are segregated, covered by an insurance policy or comparable guarantee, or held under the other options in PSD2 Art 10 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886. FIN-FSA asks you to describe how funds are handled and protected.

Professional indemnity. Payment initiation providers need indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee, as FIN-FSA's page notes (PSD2 Art 5(2)).

ICT and DORA. FIN-FSA asks for the management of operational and security risks, and incident and fraud reporting. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly.

How to get a payment institution license in Finland: process

  1. Choose the services and the capital band: EUR 125,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 20,000.
  2. Download FIN-FSA's application form, the workbook for payment institutions and the instructions. The workbook covers part of the financial information. The business plan and the capital adequacy description go in separate documents.
  3. Prepare the file under Decree 1040/2017 and chapter 5 of the Act. FIN-FSA also recommends the EBA authorisation guidelines.
  4. File with FIN-FSA's registry with the workbook for your authorisation type.
  5. Meet FIN-FSA. There is always at least one authorisation meeting, as a rule at the applicant's premises.
  6. Wait for the decision. FIN-FSA processes the application within 3 months of receiving complete documentation, and the same page says the full process averages six to twelve months.

After you get the payment institution license

  • FIN-FSA keeps the register of payment institutions.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee under the Act on Supervision Fees.
  • To serve another EEA state, notify FIN-FSA first (PSD2 Art 28). The fee schedule lists EUR 2,400 for a branch and EUR 585 for an agent or a services notification.
  • Keep own funds and safeguarding at the required level, and report incidents and fraud as the application described.
  • Check the Act for the rules on changes of control before any share transfer.

Payment institution in Finland FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Finland?

EUR 125,000 for a full payment institution, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money transmission only (PSD2 Art 7, listed on FIN-FSA's page).

How long does FIN-FSA take to decide?

3 months from complete documentation. FIN-FSA also says the full process averages six to twelve months, depending mostly on application quality.

How much does FIN-FSA charge?

EUR 6,550 for authorising a payment institution in the 2026 schedule, plus EUR 133 per hour above 50 hours. It is charged for refusals too.

Can I operate without authorisation in Finland?

FIN-FSA's page ties authorisation to EUR 3 million average monthly transactions, payment initiation and cross-border activity. Below that level you may not need it, and the schedule lists a EUR 1,850 line for payment services without authorisation. Confirm the conditions with FIN-FSA first.

Does a Finnish payment institution licence work in the rest of the EU?

Yes. Notify FIN-FSA before you use a branch, an agent or cross-border services (PSD2 Art 28).

Do I need a Finnish company?

PSD2 Art 11 requires a legal person with its head office in the same state as the registered office. The Finnish sources we read do not give further substance rules, so ask FIN-FSA.

Which language do I file in?

FIN-FSA's authorisation page does not say. Ask before you prepare translations.
