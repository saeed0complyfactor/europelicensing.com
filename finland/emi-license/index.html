EMI License in Finland

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence in Finland lets you issue e-money and provide linked payment services from a Finnish company. The Finnish Financial Supervisory Authority (FIN-FSA, Finanssivalvonta) grants it under the Act on Payment Institutions, and FIN-FSA lists EUR 350,000 as the minimum capital. The law gives FIN-FSA 3 months to decide on a complete file, and FIN-FSA says the whole process takes six to twelve months on average.

Who needs an EMI license in Finland?

FIN-FSA says that issuing e-money, or providing payment services, generally requires authorisation. For e-money, the page names two triggers: issued e-money above EUR 5 million, and any plan to operate in another EEA state. If your product holds a customer balance that the customer can spend later, you are issuing e-money. A product that only moves money for a single payment is a payment service and may fit a payment institution licence instead.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A payment institution licence, if you do not hold stored value.
  • An EMI authorised in another EEA state that notifies FIN-FSA to serve Finland. FIN-FSA's schedule lists EUR 585 for registering an agent in the EEA and EUR 585 for a free-provision-of-services notification, which gives a sense of what the passport notifications cost.
  • A smaller operation below the EUR 5 million trigger, which FIN-FSA handles as a registration by notification instead of an authorisation. It gives no passport, so check the conditions in the Act with FIN-FSA first.

EMI license requirements in Finland

Capital and own funds. Initial capital is at least EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4, repeated on FIN-FSA's page). Own funds must be the higher of that amount and the calculated requirement. For e-money, Method D sets at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5). FIN-FSA's 2026 workbook asks for your financial capacity information in a set format.

Management and fit and proper. FIN-FSA's page asks for evidence that management and the board are fit and proper, and for information on major shareholders. Decree 1040/2017 sets the appendices.

Substance. The Finnish sources we read do not give a number of local directors or a minimum office size. FIN-FSA says an authorisation application always involves at least one authorisation meeting, as a rule at the applicant's premises, so expect to show your staff and systems in person. Ask FIN-FSA before you fix your board.

AML. FIN-FSA lists customer identification, due diligence and anti-money laundering procedures as part of the application.

Safeguarding. The application must describe how customer funds are handled and protected. EMD2 Art 7 sets the method options, including segregation or an insurance guarantee.

ICT and DORA. The application describes operational and security risk management and incident and fraud reporting. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA) applies directly, so build your ICT risk framework and incident procedures before you file.

How to get an EMI license in Finland: process

  1. Choose the route: EMI, payment institution, or authorisation elsewhere with a Finnish passport.
  2. Download FIN-FSA's application form, the workbook for electronic money institutions and the instructions. The workbook covers part of the financial information. The business plan and the capital adequacy description go in separate documents.
  3. Prepare the file under Decree 1040/2017. FIN-FSA also recommends the EBA authorisation guidelines.
  4. File with FIN-FSA. The application goes to its registry, with the workbook chosen for the authorisation type.
  5. Meet FIN-FSA. There is always at least one authorisation meeting, as a rule at the applicant's premises.
  6. Wait for the decision. FIN-FSA processes the application within 3 months of receiving complete documentation. The same page says the full process averages six to twelve months and depends mostly on application quality.

A processing fee applies to approvals and refusals alike. If you withdraw, FIN-FSA still charges for the costs already incurred.

After you get the EMI license

  • FIN-FSA keeps the register of payment institutions and e-money institutions.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee under the Act on Supervision Fees.
  • Material changes to safeguarding must be notified in advance (EMD2 Art 3(2)). Check the Act for the notification rules on changes of control and qualifying holdings.
  • To serve another EEA state, notify FIN-FSA first (PSD2 Art 28 via EMD2 Art 3(1)). FIN-FSA's fee schedule lists EUR 2,400 for a branch and EUR 585 for an agent or a services notification.
  • Keep your own funds and safeguarding at the required level on an ongoing basis.

EMI in Finland compared with other countries

For comparison, see Lithuania and Ireland, and the Nordic and EEA neighbours Norway and Iceland. In Finland itself, a payment institution licence needs less capital, and the crypto page covers MiCA. For the EU rules, read the EMI licence guide.

EMI in Finland FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Finland?

EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4). FIN-FSA's authorisation page lists the same figure.

How long does FIN-FSA take to decide on an EMI licence?

The statutory period is 3 months from complete documentation. FIN-FSA also states that the whole process averages six to twelve months, depending mostly on the quality of the application.

Can I get a small EMI licence in Finland?

FIN-FSA's page ties authorisation to issued e-money above EUR 5 million or a wish to operate in another EEA state, which follows EMD2 Art 9. Below that level FIN-FSA describes a registration by notification, processed within 1 month, and a registered firm has no passport. Check the Act's conditions with FIN-FSA before you rely on this.

Does a Finnish EMI licence work in the rest of the EU?

Yes. Notify FIN-FSA of your intention to use a branch, an agent or cross-border services (PSD2 Art 28 via EMD2 Art 3(1)).

What does FIN-FSA charge to process an application?

The 2026 schedule lists EUR 6,550 for authorising a payment institution, with an hourly rate of EUR 133 above 50 hours. We did not find an e-money line, so ask FIN-FSA. Fees apply to refusals too.

Do I need a Finnish company?

The sources we read do not state this for e-money. Under EMD2 and PSD2 Art 11 the head office and registered office must be in the same member state, and the application is made to the home regulator.

Which language do I file in?

FIN-FSA's authorisation page does not say. Ask FIN-FSA before you prepare translations.
