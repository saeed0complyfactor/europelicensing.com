Crypto License in Austria

A crypto-asset service provider (Kryptowerte-Dienstleister) in Austria needs authorisation from the Austrian Financial Market Authority (FMA) under MiCA and the MiCA-Verordnung-Vollzugsgesetz (MiCA-VVG). The old Austrian virtual currency registration ran until 31 December 2025 at the latest. The FMA's fee depends on your capital class, from EUR 3,750 to EUR 12,500.

Who needs a crypto license in Austria?

You need FMA authorisation if you provide crypto-asset services in Austria as a legal person or undertaking established in the EU. That covers custody and administration, exchange, operating a trading platform, execution and placing, transfer services, reception and transmission of orders, advice and portfolio management (MiCA Art 59 and Annex IV).

Other routes can fit better.

  • You already hold a licence as a credit institution, investment firm, EMI or similar. MiCA Art 60 lets you notify at least 40 working days before starting, instead of applying. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues. The FMA charges for notifications too, for example EUR 2,800, 7,500 or 9,375 for an investment firm depending on the service (FMA-GebV annex 1, III.O.19).
  • You are authorised in another EU state. Passport into Austria by notification under Art 65.
  • You are outside the EU. You need an EU entity with a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).
  • You also move fiat money. Payment services need the payment institution licence in Austria or an EMI licence.
  • You issue e-money tokens. The FMA fee for that authorisation is EUR 11,250 (III.O.7), alongside the EMI rules.

Crypto license requirements in Austria

Capital and prudential safeguards

The minimum is EUR 50,000 for class 1 services, EUR 125,000 for class 2 (adds custody and exchange) and EUR 150,000 for class 3 (adds operating a trading platform). You hold the higher of that minimum or a quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads, and a new firm uses projected overheads. The safeguards can be CET1 own funds, an insurance policy or comparable guarantee, or both (MiCA Art 67, Annex IV).

Management and owners

Directors need good repute, knowledge, skills, experience and enough time. Qualifying shareholders need good repute (Art 68).

Substance

You need a registered office in a member state where you provide part of your services, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)). Authorisation is valid throughout the EU, with no presence needed in host states (Art 59(7)).

Custody and client money

You may not use clients' crypto-assets for your own account. Custody needs an agreement, a policy, a position register and segregation. Client funds go to a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day, unless you are also an EMI, payment institution or credit institution (Art 70, 75).

ICT, AML and governance

DORA applies (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, MiCA Art 68). AML/CFT controls and five-year record keeping apply (Art 68). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for a crypto license in Austria

  1. Map each planned service to MiCA and your capital class. The class also sets the FMA fee.
  2. Make first contact. The FMA's CASP roadmap, as it appears in search results, asks you to make first contact by email, at the CASP address on that page, and to fill in its questionnaire as far as you can. It expects a self-assessment of which services you provide and a gap analysis against MiCA, and it advises against filing an incomplete application.
  3. Prepare the file under MiCA Art 62. The FMA's form and information letter are in German, per the same search-result text, and the EU content rules are in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/305 and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/306.
  4. File through the FMA's intake platform. It sends access details by email beforehand.
  5. The FMA acknowledges within 5 working days, checks completeness within 25 working days and decides within 40 working days of a complete application. Questions raised by the 20th working day can suspend the clock for up to 20 working days (MiCA Art 63).
  6. The procedure is subject to a fee and follows the general administrative procedure act (AVG), per the FMA roadmap. On a grant, your details go to ESMA for its register.

Preparation comes before the clock starts. We could not read the FMA pages directly, so treat steps 2 to 4 as the FMA's position as reported in search results, and confirm each one at first contact.

Crypto license cost in Austria

We found no public FMA cost guide for CASPs, so these numbers come from the FMA fee ordinance and MiCA. The fee depends on your capital class.

Item Class 1 Class 2 Class 3 Basis
FMA authorisation fee EUR 3,750 EUR 10,000 EUR 12,500 FMA-GebV annex 1, III.O.14
FMA fee to extend an authorisation EUR 2,800 EUR 7,500 EUR 9,375 III.O.15
Minimum capital EUR 50,000 EUR 125,000 EUR 150,000 MiCA Art 67, Annex IV

Other FMA fee lines: EUR 375 for passport documents sent to other member states (III.O.16) and EUR 625 per acquirer for a non-objection to a qualifying holding (III.O.17).

The FMA also assesses annual supervision costs on CASPs, ART issuers and EMT issuers as a shared group by decision, and flat rates are allowed. The detailed rules sit in an FMA regulation that we did not read (section 22 MiCA-VVG). Ask the FMA what you will pay each year.

Other cost lines, with no amounts here: the Austrian or other EU entity, EU-resident directors and staff, ICT security and DORA work, audit, custody infrastructure, insurance where used for safeguards, AML tooling including travel rule systems, and advisers. Our own fee: from EUR 60,000.

After the FMA grants the license

  • Register. ESMA lists the authorisation in the interim MiCA register, and the FMA publishes its own information.
  • Supervision. The FMA supervises you as the competent authority (section 1 MiCA-VVG) and has powers to act on breaches (sections 4 and 5 MiCA-VVG).
  • Passport. You notify the FMA of the target states, services and start date. The FMA forwards it within 10 working days, and you can start on receipt or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (MiCA Art 65). The FMA fee for the documents is EUR 375.
  • Adding services. An extension carries its own fee (III.O.15).
  • Change of control. Qualifying holders must keep meeting the Art 68 repute test, and the FMA charges EUR 625 per acquirer for the non-objection procedure. Confirm the notification route with the FMA before any share transfer.

31 December 2025 Latest end of the Austrian transition, or the earlier Art 63 decision MiCA-VVG section 23

Austria allowed less time than the EU maximum. Section 23 MiCA-VVG lets registered providers continue until 31 December 2025 or until the FMA grants or refuses authorisation, whichever comes first. The FMA could also revoke a registration earlier, which ended the right to continue (section 43a FM-GwG). The MiCA-VVG text we read contains no simplified procedure.

On 23 November 2026 the ESMA interim MiCA register file listed 13 entries with Austria as home member state, counted from an automated read of the file. All 13 entries listed Art 63 authorisation as their legal basis. Recheck the figure on the register before you quote it.

Austria compared with other countries

Its crypto licence in Germany charges BaFin's time instead of a class-based fee. For the same licence elsewhere, compare Lithuania, Malta and Poland, where no authority was designated at the last check. The EU baseline is on the MiCA CASP licence page.

Crypto license in Austria FAQs

How much does a MiCA license cost in Austria?

The FMA fee is EUR 3,750 for class 1, EUR 10,000 for class 2 and EUR 12,500 for class 3 (FMA-GebV annex 1, III.O.14). Capital is EUR 50,000, 125,000 or 150,000 by class, or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher (MiCA Art 67). Our fee starts at EUR 60,000.

Who needs a MiCA license in Austria?

Anyone providing crypto-asset services as a business in the EU needs authorisation or, for existing financial entities, an Art 60 notification. An Austrian firm applies to the FMA.

How long does the FMA take for a MiCA license?

The statutory clock is 25 working days for completeness and 40 working days to decide from a complete application. It can be suspended for up to 20 working days for questions (MiCA Art 63). Your preparation time comes before it starts.

When did the Austrian VASP transition end?

Section 23 MiCA-VVG lets registered providers continue until 31 December 2025, or until a grant or refusal under Art 63 if that comes first. The EU-wide limit was 1 July 2026 (Art 143(3)).

Does Austria have a simplified procedure for existing providers?

The MiCA-VVG section on transitional provisions that we read contains none. MiCA Art 143(6) allows member states to apply a simplified procedure for applications made in a set window, and the FMA's own pages would state whether it used one.

How do I start an application with the FMA?

The FMA's CASP roadmap, as shown in search results, asks you to make first contact by email and complete a questionnaire. We could not confirm the address on an FMA page. Confirm the current route directly with the FMA.

Can an Austrian firm get a crypto license in another country?

Yes. You apply where your registered office and effective management are, then passport. Your registered office must be in a member state where you provide part of your services (Art 59(2)).

Can I use an existing EMI licence for crypto services?

An EMI can notify under Art 60, but only for custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues. Anything wider needs a full authorisation.
