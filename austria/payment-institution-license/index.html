Payment Institution License in Austria

A payment institution (Zahlungsinstitut) licence from the Austrian Financial Market Authority (FMA) lets an Austrian company provide payment services and passport them across the EU and EEA. The law is the Payment Services Act 2018 (ZaDiG 2018). Capital is EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 of core equity depending on the services, and the FMA's licence fee is EUR 10,000.

Who needs a payment institution license in Austria?

You need one if you provide payment services in Austria as a business and you are not a credit institution or an EMI (section 3(2) ZaDiG 2018 excludes both from the licence chapter). The licence covers the services in section 1(2), and the FMA can limit it to specific services.

Other routes can fit better.

  • Your product holds a balance for later spending. That is e-money and needs the EMI licence in Austria.
  • You only provide account information. Section 15 ZaDiG 2018 sets a registration, not a licence, and the FMA fee is EUR 5,000.
  • You are already a payment institution in another EEA state. Passport into Austria by notification.
  • You rely on an exemption. Section 3 lists excluded activities, including limited networks and some telecom-billed payments. Limited-network providers must notify the FMA once payment volume over twelve months exceeds EUR 1 million.
  • You expected a small payment institution route. PSD2 Art 32 lets Member States offer one. Section 3 ZaDiG 2018 contains none.

Payment institution license requirements in Austria

Capital and own funds

Your common equity tier 1 capital may never fall below EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only, or EUR 125,000 for any of the payment services in section 1(2) Z 1 to 5. In ongoing operation it may not fall below those amounts or the own funds calculation under section 17, whichever is higher (section 16 ZaDiG 2018; PSD2 Art 7). The capital must be freely available to the managers in Austria without restriction or encumbrance (section 9 ZaDiG 2018). Payment initiation services also need professional indemnity cover (section 8).

Legal form, seat and management

You must be a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) or cooperative, with the registered seat and head administration in Austria and at least part of the services provided there. At least one manager has the centre of life interests in Austria, and at least one has no other main occupation outside payment services, e-money or banking. Professional fitness is presumed where a manager shows at least three years of leading experience at a comparable company, and at least one manager must command German (section 10 ZaDiG 2018).

Owners

The application names qualifying shareholders and gives the information the FMA needs to assess their reliability (section 9 ZaDiG 2018). Section 19 governs qualifying holdings during operation.

Safeguarding

Section 18 ZaDiG 2018 covers safeguarding of customer funds (PSD2 Art 10), and the application must describe how you protect users' funds.

AML, governance and ICT

The application describes governance, internal controls, risk management, accounting, incident procedures, sensitive payment data handling, business continuity, statistical data collection and a security strategy. It describes the internal AML control system and explains how you reach high digital operational resilience under DORA, including third-party ICT (section 9 ZaDiG 2018). How to apply for a payment institution license in Austria

  1. Decide which payment services you will provide. Your capital tier follows the list.
  2. Form the Austrian company, place its seat and head administration in Austria, and fund the capital so it is freely available to the managers.
  3. Contact the FMA before you file. This is our recommendation. We could not read the FMA's payment institution pages because the site blocks automated access.
  4. Assemble the file against section 9 ZaDiG 2018, which lists 17 points, including the three-year business plan and the DORA explanation.
  5. File with the FMA. The Act names no official form, so confirm the filing route when you make contact.
  6. The FMA has 3 months from receipt. If the file is incomplete, the 3 months run from when all information is in (section 9(4) ZaDiG 2018). It consults the Oesterreichische Nationalbank before it grants (section 10).
  7. The FMA decides by formal decision (Bescheid). A grant must be in writing or it is void, and the FMA enters you in the Zahlungsinstitutsregister at the same time (section 10).

The Act does not state the language of the application. German is Austria's official language, and one manager must command German. Ask the FMA which documents it takes in English.

After the FMA grants the license

  • Register. The FMA enters you in the Zahlungsinstitutsregister, and section 13 covers the Firmenbuch entry.
  • Changes. Changes to the facts behind the licence go to the FMA (section 14 ZaDiG 2018).
  • Reporting and supervision. Section 26 covers reporting, and section 89 allocates the FMA's supervision costs.
  • Passport and agents. Sections 27 to 29 cover freedom of establishment and services (PSD2 Art 28), and section 22 covers agents.
  • Change of control. Section 19 governs qualifying holdings. Confirm the notification route with the FMA before any share transfer.

Payment institution license in Austria FAQs

How much does a payment institution license cost in Austria?

The FMA fee is EUR 10,000 for the licence and EUR 5,000 for AISP registration (FMA-GebV annex 1). You also need EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 of CET1 capital and an Austrian company with local management. our fee range for payment institution licence in Austria

How much capital does a payment institution need in Austria?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only and EUR 125,000 for the other payment services, as CET1 capital (section 16 ZaDiG 2018).

How long does the FMA take to decide?

The deadline is 3 months from receipt. If the file is incomplete, the 3 months run from when all information is submitted (section 9(4) ZaDiG 2018). Preparation and your answers to questions come on top.

How do I get a payment institution license in Austria?

You form an Austrian corporation seated in Austria, fund the capital, prepare the section 9 ZaDiG file, apply to the FMA and answer its questions. The FMA decides by formal decision after consulting the Oesterreichische Nationalbank.

Can I register as an account information service instead?

Yes, if you only provide account information. Section 15 ZaDiG 2018 sets the registration and the FMA fee is EUR 5,000.

Is there a small payment institution licence in Austria?

No. Section 3 ZaDiG 2018 has no small payment institution exemption, although PSD2 Art 32 would allow one.

Does the application have to be in German?

The Act does not state the language of the application. At least one manager must command German. Ask the FMA which documents it takes in English.

Can an Austrian payment institution serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes, by notification under PSD2 Art 28 and sections 27 to 29 ZaDiG 2018.
