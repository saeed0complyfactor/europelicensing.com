Payment Institution License in the EU

A payment institution licence under PSD2 lets you run payment accounts, execute transfers, acquire card payments, send remittances or initiate payments from one EU or EEA country, then passport those services across the Union. Europe Licensing prepares the application, sizes your capital and safeguarding, and writes the compliance documents your regulator will review.

Who needs a payment institution licence?

You need authorisation before you provide payment services, unless you are already another PSD2 provider such as a credit institution or EMI, or use the small PI or account information exemptions (PSD2 Art 11(1); Art 1(1); Art 32; Art 33). Only a legal person established in a Member State can be authorised (Art 11(1)).

PSD2 Annex I lists eight payment services:

  1. Placing cash on a payment account and operating it.
  2. Cash withdrawals from a payment account and operating it.
  3. Executing payment transactions: direct debits, card payments, credit transfers.
  4. The same, where funds are covered by a credit line.
  5. Issuing payment instruments or acquiring payment transactions.
  6. Money remittance.
  7. Payment initiation services.
  8. Account information services.

A fully licensed firm is an "authorised payment institution" (Art 28). If your product holds a balance for later spending, that is e-money and needs an EMI licence.

Payment initiation and account information providers must also hold professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee (Art 5(2) and (3)).

Own funds: methods A, B and C

Own funds must never fall below the higher of your initial capital and the Art 9 amount (PSD2 Art 8(1)). The regulator picks the method under national law (Art 9(1)):

  • Method A: 10% of the preceding year's fixed overheads, or projected overheads in your first year.
  • Method B: slices of monthly payment volume (one twelfth of the preceding year's total), from 4.0% of the first EUR 5 million down to 0.25% above EUR 250 million, multiplied by k.
  • Method C: an income-based indicator multiplied by sliding factors from 10% down to 1.5%, then by k.

k is 0.5 for money remittance only and 1 for any of services 1 to 5 (Art 9(2)). The regulator may require up to 20% more or allow up to 20% less (Art 9(3)).

Safeguarding customer funds

For services 1 to 6, you safeguard all funds received for payment transactions in one of two ways (PSD2 Art 10(1)). You can segregate them, placing funds still held at the end of the next business day in a separate account at a credit institution or, where the central bank agrees, at a central bank, or in secure, liquid, low-risk assets, protected from other creditors in insolvency. Or you can cover them with insurance or a guarantee from an insurer or credit institution outside your group. Where funds are mixed with non-payment money, the payment portion is safeguarded too (Art 10(2)).

Governance, fit and proper and AML requirements

The PSD2 Art 5(1) file includes a programme of operations, a business plan with a 3-year forecast budget, governance and ICT arrangements under DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, an incident procedure, business continuity plans, a security policy and AML controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (Art 5(1)(a), (b), (e) to (k)). It also identifies qualifying holders with evidence of suitability, and directors of good repute with appropriate knowledge and experience (Art 5(1)(m), (n)).

The regulator authorises you only if your governance is sound and proportionate (Art 11(4)) and it is satisfied with your shareholders (Art 11(6)). Your head office is in the same state as your registered office (Art 11(3)), and the regulator may require a separate entity if other business puts the payment business at risk (Art 11(5)). See our AML and compliance programme.

Small payment institution and AISP registration

A Member State may exempt firms providing services 1 to 6 from all or part of the regime (PSD2 Art 32(1)). The monthly average of the preceding 12 months' transactions must stay within a national limit of at most EUR 3 million, and no manager may have convictions for money laundering, terrorist financing or other financial crimes. A small PI must have its head office where it actually operates (Art 32(2)), cannot passport (Art 32(3)) and has 30 calendar days to seek authorisation if it outgrows the conditions (Art 32(5)). AML law applies in full (Art 32(6)).

Account-information-only firms register instead (Art 33(1)). The file is shorter, and most conduct rules in Titles III and IV do not apply (Art 33(2)).

How to apply for a payment institution licence

  1. Map each product feature to Annex I to fix your capital tier, own-funds method and safeguarding scope.
  2. Set up a legal person in your chosen home Member State (PSD2 Art 11(1)).
  3. Prepare the Art 5(1) file, plus insurance under Art 5(2) or (3) where needed.
  4. Pay in the initial capital and include the evidence (Art 5(1)(c)).
  5. Submit and answer the regulator's questions until the file is complete.
  6. After authorisation, put safeguarding in place and file passport notifications.

How long does a payment institution licence take?

The regulator must decide within 3 months of receiving the application or, if it is incomplete, all the information needed, and must give reasons for a refusal (PSD2 Art 12). Preparation and question rounds come on top of that clock.

Passporting a payment institution licence

An authorisation is valid in all Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9)). Before serving a new state, you notify your home regulator of the states, services and any agents or branches (Art 28(1)). The home regulator forwards this within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (Art 28(2)). For an agent or branch, the home regulator decides within 3 months (Art 28(3)). The UK is outside the EU passport. Under the Payment Services Regulations 2017, the FCA uses the same EUR 20,000, 50,000 and 125,000 tiers, with none for account information (Sch 3 Part 1). A UK small PI is capped at a EUR 3 million monthly average and cannot offer account information or payment initiation (reg 14). The FCA must decide a complete application within 3 months and any application within 12 months (reg 9). See the UK payment institution licence and UK FCA authorisation.

Payment institution licence FAQs

What is a payment institution?

A firm authorised under PSD2 to provide one or more Annex I payment services (PSD2 Art 1(1)(d); Art 11). It may not issue e-money.

Is a PSD2 licence the same as a payment institution licence?

In everyday use, yes: "PSD2 licence" usually means authorisation as a payment institution under Directive (EU) 2015/2366. PSD2 also governs AISP registration (Art 33).

What is the difference between an authorised and a small payment institution?

An authorised payment institution meets the full regime and can passport (Art 11(9)). A small payment institution uses a national exemption, with a monthly average of at most EUR 3 million, and cannot passport (Art 32).

How much capital does a payment institution need?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5 (PSD2 Art 7). Own funds then stay at or above the higher of that figure and your Method A, B or C result (Art 8(1)).

Do payment initiation and account information providers need a licence?

Payment initiation needs authorisation, EUR 50,000 of capital and professional indemnity insurance (Art 7(b); Art 5(2)). Account information needs registration and the same type of insurance (Art 33; Art 5(3)).

What is the status of PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation?

They are not yet law. On 23 November 2026, the European Parliament's Legislative Observatory lists both procedures as awaiting Parliament's second reading: PSD3 (2023/0209(COD)) and the Payment Services Regulation (2023/0210(COD)). The Council adopted its first-reading positions on 29 October 2026, and the Commission issued its communications on them on 3 November 2026. Neither has been published in the Official Journal. The PSD3 proposal would repeal PSD2 and EMD2. Until new rules apply, you apply under the national laws implementing PSD2.
