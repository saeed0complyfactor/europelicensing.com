EMI License in Europe

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from one EU or EEA country, then passport both across the Union. Europe Licensing prepares your application, your capital and safeguarding model, and the compliance documents your regulator will test.

Who needs an EMI licence?

You need one if you issue electronic money: monetary value stored electronically as a claim on the issuer, issued on receipt of funds and accepted by someone other than the issuer (EMD2 Art 2(2)). Only authorised e-money issuers may issue it (EMD2 Art 10). Prepaid cards and wallets that hold a spendable balance usually fall in scope.

Alongside e-money, an EMI may provide any PSD2 Annex I payment service, grant credit linked to certain payment services (never from e-money funds), provide ancillary services, operate payment systems and run other lawful business (EMD2 Art 6(1)). It may not take deposits (Art 6(2)) or pay interest on balances (Art 12), and must redeem e-money at par on request (Art 11(2)).

The small EMI regime is optional: each Member State decides whether to offer it (EMD2 Art 9(1)). Managers must have no convictions for money laundering, terrorist financing or other financial crimes (Art 9(1)(b)), AML rules apply in full (Art 9(8)), and a firm that outgrows the conditions has 30 calendar days to apply for full authorisation (Art 9(6)). If you never hold stored value, compare the payment institution licence.

EU requirements for an EMI licence

Initial capital

At least EUR 350,000 of eligible capital at authorisation (EMD2 Art 4).

Own funds

Own funds must stay at or above the higher of the initial capital and the calculated requirement (EMD2 Art 5(1)). For e-money you hold at least 2% of average outstanding e-money, known as Method D (Art 5(3)), measured over the preceding six months (Art 2(4)). For payment services not linked to e-money, the regulator adds Method A, B or C from PSD2 Art 9 (EMD2 Art 5(2)). It may then require up to 20% more or allow up to 20% less (Art 5(5)).

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money issued (EMD2 Art 7(1)) in one of two ways (PSD2 Art 10(1)). You can segregate them, placing funds still held at the end of the next business day in a separate account at a credit institution or, where the central bank agrees, at a central bank, or in secure, liquid, low-risk assets, protected from other creditors in insolvency. Or you can cover them with insurance or a guarantee from an insurer or credit institution outside your group. Card-funded money must be safeguarded within five business days of issuance (EMD2 Art 7(1)), and material changes need advance notice to the regulator (Art 3(2)).

Fit and proper

Directors and managers must be of good repute with appropriate knowledge and experience (PSD2 Art 5(1)(n)). Qualifying holders must be shown to be suitable (Art 5(1)(m)), and the regulator must refuse if it is not satisfied with them (Art 11(6)). EMD2 Art 3(1) applies these rules to EMIs.

Governance and ICT

You need clear lines of responsibility, risk procedures and internal controls proportionate to the business (PSD2 Art 11(4)). The file describes governance and ICT arrangements under DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (Art 5(1)(e)), plus incident handling, business continuity and a security policy (Art 5(1)(f), (h), (j)). Your head office is in the same state as your registered office (Art 11(3)).

AML

The file describes your internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 and Regulation (EU) 2015/847 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for an EMI licence

  1. Map your product to EMD2 Art 6(1) and PSD2 Annex I to fix your own-funds method and safeguarding scope.
  2. Set up a legal person in your chosen home Member State (PSD2 Art 11(1)).
  3. Build the PSD2 Art 5(1) file, including a business plan with a 3-year forecast budget.
  4. Pay in the initial capital and include the evidence (Art 5(1)(c)).
  5. Submit and answer the regulator's questions until the file is complete.
  6. After authorisation, open safeguarding arrangements and file passport notifications.

How long does an EMI licence take?

The statutory decision period is 3 months. EMD2 Art 3(1) applies PSD2 Articles 5 and 11 to 17 to EMIs, and PSD2 Art 12 requires the regulator to decide within 3 months of receiving the application or, if it is incomplete, all the information needed. A refusal must give reasons. Preparing the file and answering questions comes on top of that clock.

Passporting an EMI licence across the EEA

Your authorisation is valid in all Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). Before serving a new state, you notify your home regulator of the states, services and any agents or branches (PSD2 Art 28(1)). The home regulator forwards this within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (Art 28(2)). For an agent or branch, the home regulator decides within 3 months (Art 28(3)). You may distribute e-money abroad through distributors (EMD2 Art 3(4)) but may not issue it through agents (Art 3(5)). Small EMIs cannot passport (Art 9(3)). The UK is outside the EU passport. The FCA authorises UK EMIs under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, with initial capital of EUR 350,000 (Sch 2 para 2) and part of the business carried on in the UK (reg 6(4A)). It must decide a complete application within 3 months and any application within 12 months (reg 9). See the UK EMI licence and UK FCA authorisation.

EMI licence FAQs

What is an EMI licence?

It is the authorisation under Title II of EMD2 that lets a legal person issue electronic money in the EU or EEA (EMD2 Art 2(1)). It also covers PSD2 payment services (Art 6(1)(a)) and can be passported.

How much capital do you need for an EMI licence?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMD2 Art 4). Afterwards your own funds must cover at least 2% of average outstanding e-money plus any requirement for unrelated payment services, and never drop below EUR 350,000 (Art 5).

How long does it take to get an EMI licence?

The regulator must decide within 3 months of a complete application (PSD2 Art 12, applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). Preparation time comes on top.

Can an EMI pay interest on customer balances?

No. EMD2 Art 12 prohibits interest or any benefit linked to how long a customer holds e-money.

Can an EMI issue stablecoins?

An EMI may offer e-money tokens once it has notified and published a crypto-asset white paper (MiCA Art 48(1)), and those tokens count as e-money (Art 48(2)). Custody or transfer of its own tokens needs 40 working days' notice to the regulator (Art 60(4)). Other crypto services need MiCA CASP authorisation.

Do I need a separate EMI licence for the UK?

Yes. An EEA authorisation is valid only in the Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9)), so UK customers need FCA authorisation or registration under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011.
