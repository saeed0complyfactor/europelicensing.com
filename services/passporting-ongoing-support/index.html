EMI passporting and post-license support

Once you are authorised as an EMI, payment institution or CASP, you can serve other Member States from your home licence. You do this through a notification to your home regulator (PSD2 Art 28, MiCA Art 65). For as long as you hold the licence, shareholder changes and periodic reporting also need filings.

Europe Licensing prepares your passport notifications and tracks the deadlines for your post-licence filings.

These periods bind the regulator. Your own preparation time comes on top.

How to passport a payment institution license in the EU

PSD2 Article 28(1) requires you to send your home regulator:

  • your name, address and authorisation number
  • the Member States where you plan to operate
  • the payment services you will provide there
  • for agents, the details in Article 19(1)
  • for a branch, its business plan, governance and internal controls, organisational structure and managers

Tell your home regulator too if you will outsource operational functions in the host state.

Within one month of receiving complete information, the home regulator sends it to the host regulator. The host has one month to assess it and flag concerns, in particular about money laundering or terrorist financing linked to an agent or branch. The home regulator communicates its decision within three months (Art 28(3)). An agent or branch can start once it is entered in the home register, and you must tell the home regulator the date you start.

Any later change, such as a new agent, branch or outsourcing provider, goes through the same procedure (Art 28(4)).

Passporting an EMI

EMD2 Article 3(1) applies PSD2 Articles 11 to 17, 19(5) and (6) and 20 to 31 to EMIs, so the Article 28 procedure covers EMI passports. You can distribute and redeem e-money through distributors, and distributing in another Member State follows PSD2 Articles 27 to 31 (EMD2 Art 3(4)). You cannot issue e-money through agents, though agents can provide payment services for you under PSD2 Article 19 (EMD2 Art 3(5)).

Agents and branches

To register an agent, you file its name and address, its AML/CFT internal controls, the identity and fitness of its managers and the services it is mandated for (PSD2 Art 19(1)). The regulator decides within two months (Art 19(2)). An agent or branch in another Member State follows Article 28 (Art 19(5)). Host regulators can ask for periodic reports on activity in their territory (Art 29(2)), and a host Member State may require a central contact point when you operate there through agents (Art 29(4)).

MiCA passporting for CASPs

MiCA Article 65(1) asks for the Member States you will serve, the services you will provide there, your start date and any activities outside MiCA. Your home regulator passes this to the host single points of contact, ESMA and EBA within 10 working days and tells you when it has done so. You may start from the date you receive that communication, or at the latest from the 15th calendar day after you submitted the information (Art 65(4)).

Change of control in an EMI, PI or CASP

Under PSD2 Article 6(1), anyone who decides to acquire or increase a qualifying holding in a payment institution must notify the regulator in writing in advance if it would cross 20%, 30% or 50% or make the institution a subsidiary. Selling below those levels also needs advance notice. The regulator can oppose an acquirer whose influence would harm sound and prudent management. If shares are acquired despite opposition, the voting rights can be suspended or the votes annulled (Art 6(3), (4)). EMD2 Article 3(3) applies the same rule to EMIs. PSD2 Article 6 sets no assessment period of its own, so the timing comes from your home country's law.

For CASPs, MiCA Article 83 sets the timetable. The regulator acknowledges your notice within two working days and assesses it within 60 working days. It can request more information until the 50th working day, which suspends the clock for up to 20 working days. The regulator may extend that suspension by up to 30 working days for an acquirer outside the EU or regulated under third-country law. If it does not oppose by the deadline, the acquisition is deemed approved (Art 83(8)). Article 84(1) lists the criteria: the acquirer's reputation and financial soundness, the people who will run the business, continued compliance and ML/TF risk.

Ongoing obligations after authorisation

  • Report fraud statistics to your regulator at least once a year (PSD2 Art 96(6)).
  • Report major ICT-related incidents under DORA Article 19, which has covered PIs, EMIs and CASPs since 17 January 2025.
  • Notify changes to agents, branches and outsourcing without undue delay (PSD2 Arts 19(8), 28(4)).
  • For EMIs, give advance notice of any material change to safeguarding (EMD2 Art 3(2)).

Your home regulator sets other returns and their format. Our AML and compliance service covers the AML side.

Passporting and change of control FAQs

What does passporting mean for an EMI?

It means serving clients in another Member State on your home authorisation. You notify your home regulator under PSD2 Article 28, which EMD2 Article 3(1) applies to EMIs.

How long does it take to passport a payment institution license?

The law gives the home regulator one month to forward your complete notification and the host one month to respond. The home regulator must communicate its decision within three months (PSD2 Art 28(2), (3)).

Do I need to passport to use agents in another country?

Yes. Article 19(5) sends agents and branches in another Member State through the Article 28 procedure, and the agent can start once entered in the register.

How does a CASP passport under MiCA?

You file the notice in MiCA Article 65(1). You can begin when your home regulator tells you it has passed the notice on, and no later than the 15th calendar day after you submitted it.

Do I need approval for a change of control of an EMI?

You must notify the regulator in advance when a holding crosses 20%, 30% or 50% (EMD2 Art 3(3)), and the regulator can oppose. Member States may waive this for EMIs that also run other businesses.

How long does a CASP change of control assessment take?

60 working days from the regulator's acknowledgement, which itself comes within two working days. An information request can pause the clock for up to 20 working days, and the regulator may extend that by up to 30 working days for non-EU acquirers (MiCA Art 83(6)).
