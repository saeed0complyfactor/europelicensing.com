MiCA License for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASP)

Since 1 July 2026, a firm that provides crypto-asset services in the EU needs authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), Regulation (EU) 2023/1114. One authorisation from your home regulator covers every EU member state. We prepare the application file, write the policies the regulator reviews and run the process with you up to the decision.

Jan Hagen, regulatory compliance expert. Jan has supported authorisations with the FCA and DNB and has held senior compliance and MLRO roles.

Who needs a MiCA licence?

You need CASP authorisation if your business provides any of these services to clients on a professional basis (MiCA Art 3(1)(15) and (16)): custody and administration of crypto-assets; operating a trading platform; exchanging crypto-assets for funds or for other crypto-assets; executing orders; placing; receiving and transmitting orders; advice; portfolio management; and transfer services.

The applicant needs a registered office in a member state where it carries out part of its services, its place of effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)). ESMA has said firms outside the EU cannot serve or solicit EU clients, including business clients, except under the narrow reverse solicitation regime.

VASP licence or CASP licence?

"VASP" (virtual asset service provider) is the term many member states used for crypto firms registered under national anti-money laundering rules before MiCA. A VASP registration is not a MiCA authorisation. The licence you now need is CASP authorisation from a national competent authority.

Has the MiCA transitional period ended?

Yes. Article 143(3) let firms operating under national law before 30 December 2024 continue until 1 July 2026, or until their MiCA application was decided, whichever came first. Member states could shorten the period or skip it, but none could extend it past 1 July 2026.

An old national VASP registration therefore no longer allows you to provide crypto-asset services anywhere in the EU. ESMA's statement of 23 June 2026 says unauthorised CASPs must stop onboarding new EU clients, stop marketing and wind down in an orderly way. The simplified procedure in Article 143(6) only covered applications submitted by 1 July 2026, so new applicants go through the full process.

MiCA licence requirements

Minimum capital by CASP class

Class Minimum capital Services covered
Class 1 EUR 50,000 Reception and transmission of orders, advice, execution of orders, placing, transfer services, portfolio management
Class 2 EUR 125,000 Class 1 plus custody and administration, and exchange for funds or other crypto-assets
Class 3 EUR 150,000 Class 2 plus operating a trading platform

The class minimum is a floor: you must hold the higher of that amount or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads, reviewed yearly; a new firm uses its projected first-year overheads (Art 67(1) and (2)). The safeguards can be Common Equity Tier 1 own funds, an insurance policy or comparable guarantee covering the EU, or both (Art 67(4)).

Governance and fit and proper

Directors must be of sufficiently good repute, have the right knowledge, skills and experience, and commit enough time (Art 68(1)). Qualifying shareholders must also be of good repute (Art 68(2)), and staff need the skills their duties require (Art 68(5)).

Client assets and custody

You may not use clients' crypto-assets for your own account (Art 70(1)). Client funds must go to a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day, in separate accounts, unless you are also an EMI, payment institution or credit institution (Art 70(2), (3) and (5)). Custody requires a client agreement, a custody policy, a register of positions, segregation from your own estate and statements at least every three months and on request. Your liability for losses you cause is capped at the market value of the lost crypto-asset, and any sub-custodian must be an authorised CASP (Art 75).

Complaints, conflicts and outsourcing

Complaints handling must be free for clients, published and fair (Art 71). Your conflicts of interest policy must be disclosed on your website and reviewed at least yearly (Art 72). Outsourcing needs a written agreement, and you stay fully responsible (Art 73).

ICT security and DORA

Your ICT systems must meet Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), with business continuity and recovery plans (Art 68(7)). You also need AML/CFT controls (Art 68(8)) and records kept for five years, or up to seven if the regulator asks (Art 68(9)).

How to get a MiCA licence: process and timeline

  1. Map your product to the ten crypto-asset services to set your capital class.
  2. Choose your home member state.
  3. Build the file listed in Article 62(2): programme of operations, proof of capital, governance, fit-and-proper evidence, AML/CFT controls, business continuity, ICT documentation, client asset segregation, complaints, and service-specific policies.
  4. Submit. The regulator acknowledges receipt within 5 working days (Art 63(1)).
  5. Within 25 working days it confirms the file is complete or sets a deadline for missing items (Art 63(2)).
  6. It decides within 40 working days of a complete application (Art 63(9)). Questions raised by the 20th working day pause the clock for up to 20 working days (Art 63(12)).
  7. Within 2 working days of authorisation, your details go to ESMA for its register (Art 63(13)).

Preparation and answering questions sit outside these statutory limits, so we give you a dated plan for your chosen country after scoping. The regulator must refuse if directors or qualifying shareholders fail the Article 68 tests, or if you are likely to fail any MiCA Title V requirement (Art 63(10)).

Passporting a MiCA licence across the EU

Your authorisation is valid throughout the EU, and you need no physical presence in host states (Art 59(7)). You give your home regulator the target states, services, start date and any non-MiCA activities. It forwards them to the host states, ESMA and the EBA within 10 working days, and you can start on receipt of that notice or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (Art 65). Adding services later needs an extension from the same regulator (Art 59(8)).

Already a bank, EMI or investment firm?

Credit institutions, central securities depositories, investment firms, EMIs, UCITS management companies, AIFMs and market operators can provide some crypto-asset services by notifying their regulator at least 40 working days in advance instead of applying (Art 60). The scope follows their existing licence. An EMI, for example, may only offer custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues; anything wider needs CASP authorisation. It also said a CASP authorised elsewhere can serve Polish clients through an Article 65 passport. Check the current status with the UKNF before planning a Polish application. Read our Poland note.

MiCA licence FAQs

How much does a MiCA licence cost?

The cost has three parts: capital of EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class, or a quarter of annual fixed overheads if higher (Art 67, Annex IV); regulator application and supervision fees, set by each member state and listed on our country pages; and the cost of running the firm, including directors, staff, office, ICT security, audit and advisers. Our fee depends on scope: from EUR 60,000.

How long does it take to get a MiCA licence?

The regulator has 25 working days to check completeness and 40 working days to decide, plus up to 20 working days of pause for questions (Art 63). Preparing the file comes on top.

What is the difference between a VASP and a CASP?

A VASP was usually a national anti-money laundering registration. A CASP is authorised under MiCA, with capital, governance, conduct and custody rules and an EU-wide passport.

Can I still operate with my national VASP registration?

No. Every transitional period ended by 1 July 2026 at the latest (Art 143(3)).

Can a non-EU company get a MiCA licence?

Yes, through an EU entity with a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).

Do I need a licence in every EU country?

No. You notify your home regulator and can start in each host state within 15 calendar days at the latest (Art 65).

Where can I check whether a firm is MiCA authorised?

ESMA lists authorised CASPs in its register, first published as the interim MiCA register of csv files updated weekly. See the CASPS file on ESMA's MiCA page.
