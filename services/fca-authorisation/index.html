FCA Authorisation: EMI, Payment Institution and Crypto License in the UK

To issue e-money, provide payment services or run a cryptoasset business in the UK, you need authorisation or registration with the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). An EU licence does not cover the UK, because passporting between the UK and the EEA ended on 31 December 2020. We help you pick the right FCA status, build the application and deal with the FCA's questions until it decides.

Which FCA licence do you need?

Status When it fits Initial capital
Authorised EMI (AEMI) You project average outstanding e-money above EUR 5m EUR 350,000
Small EMI (SEMI) Average outstanding e-money up to EUR 5m, payment transactions up to EUR 3m a month, no AIS or PIS 2% of average outstanding e-money once it reaches EUR 500,000
Authorised payment institution (API) Payment transactions above a EUR 3m monthly average, or you offer payment initiation, which a small PI cannot EUR 20,000 (money remittance), EUR 50,000 (payment initiation), EUR 125,000 (other payment services)
Small payment institution (SPI) Monthly average payment transactions up to EUR 3m over 12 months, no AIS or PIS Not in the Schedule 3 table, which covers APIs
Registered account information service provider (RAISP) Account information services are your only payment service Professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee instead of capital

If you provide several payment services, the highest of the API amounts applies (PSRs Sch 3 para 2(2)). Once authorised, an EMI's own funds for issuing e-money under Method D are 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMRs Sch 2 para 23(2)). A new firm can use projected volumes to test the small EMI and small PI thresholds.

FCA EMI and payment institution requirements

The FCA's application page asks for:

  • complete answers to every question on the form, with reasons where a question does not apply;
  • a main contact who works at the firm, not an outside adviser;
  • policies and procedures written for your business, not generic templates;
  • the correct forms for directors, managers and controllers with qualifying holdings;
  • evidence that the people involved know payment services or e-money;
  • financial forecasts, including balance sheet forecasts and two stress-tested scenarios where relevant;
  • risk information on any unregulated activities;
  • arrangements for capital, safeguarding, wind-down and professional indemnity insurance, where they apply.

Directors and managers must be of good repute and have the right knowledge and experience. You pay a non-refundable application fee, and the FCA rejects an application without assessment if the fee is wrong.

Safeguarding

An authorised PI must protect customer funds either by segregating them, placing them in a separate account with a credit institution or the Bank of England if still held at the end of the next business day, or by covering them with an insurance policy or comparable guarantee (PSRs reg 23). Small PIs can opt in. The FCA's supplementary safeguarding rules for payment and e-money firms, set out in PS25/12, have applied since 7 May 2026.

How long does FCA authorisation take?

  1. Choose your status from the table above and confirm your capital.
  2. Prepare the file: forecasts, policies, safeguarding and wind-down arrangements, and the forms for individuals and controllers.
  3. Submit the application to the FCA and pay the fee.
  4. The FCA allocates a case officer within its published maximum of 20 working days.
  5. The FCA asks questions and you answer them.
  6. The FCA must decide a complete EMI or PI application within 3 months of receiving it. If the application is incomplete, the limit is 12 months from the date the FCA received it (EMRs reg 9, PSRs reg 9).

The 3-month clock starts only once the FCA treats the application as complete. Preparation and question rounds add to the elapsed time, so we give you a dated plan after scoping.

UK and EU licences after Brexit

Passporting between the UK and the EEA ended at the end of the Brexit transition period on 31 December 2020. An EEA firm needs UK authorisation to serve the UK, and a UK-authorised EMI or PI has no right to serve EU clients. If you need both markets, you need two licences: one from the FCA and one from an EU or EEA regulator. Our guides cover the EU EMI licence, the EU payment institution licence and MiCA CASP authorisation, which the UK does not recognise.

Crypto licence UK: MLR registration and the new FCA regime

Today: MLR cryptoasset registration

Under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017, cryptoasset exchange providers and custodian wallet providers must be registered before they act (MLRs reg 56(1)(f) and (g)). The FCA says its deadline for deciding a registration is 3 months from receiving the application form, or from receiving any further information it asked for. It also says firms should not apply for MLR registration after 31 July 2027, because it is unlikely to decide before the new regime starts.

From 25 October 2027: FCA cryptoasset authorisation

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 were made on 4 February 2026. The FCA states the new regime starts on 25 October 2027. From then, firms carrying on the new cryptoasset regulated activities need FSMA authorisation with the matching permissions.

  • The application window opened on 30 September 2026 and closes on 28 February 2027.
  • MLR registrations are not converted automatically, and being registered does not guarantee authorisation.
  • EMIs and payment institutions also need FSMA authorisation for these activities. Firms already authorised under FSMA apply to vary their permissions.
  • A firm that applies in the window can keep providing services under the saving provision while its application is decided.
  • A firm that does not apply must run off its UK cryptoasset business before the regime starts.

The FCA asks firms to run a gap analysis against the FSMA requirements, agree a board plan and apply early in the window. Under FSMA section 55V, the FCA has 6 months to decide a complete Part 4A application and 12 months for an incomplete one. See our UK crypto authorisation guide.

FCA authorisation FAQs

How much does an FCA EMI licence cost?

The main fixed cost is capital: EUR 350,000 for an authorised EMI (EMRs Sch 2 para 2). On top of that come the FCA's non-refundable application fee, your staff, systems and safeguarding costs, and advisers. Our own fee depends on scope: from EUR 60,000.

How long does the FCA take to approve an EMI or payment institution?

By law, 3 months from a complete application and 12 months at most from receipt of an incomplete one (EMRs reg 9, PSRs reg 9). The FCA's published time to allocate a case officer is a maximum of 20 working days.

What is the difference between an authorised and a small EMI?

A small EMI must stay at or below EUR 5m average outstanding e-money and EUR 3m a month in unrelated payment transactions, and cannot offer AIS or PIS (EMRs reg 13). Above EUR 5m you need full authorisation and EUR 350,000 initial capital.

Do I need FCA authorisation for account information services only?

You need registration as a RAISP, which requires professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee (PSRs reg 18).

Does an EU EMI or MiCA licence let me operate in the UK?

No. EEA firms lost passporting rights into the UK on 31 December 2020 and need UK authorisation where their activity requires it.

Do I need FCA registration or authorisation for crypto?

Until the new regime starts on 25 October 2027, cryptoasset exchange providers and custodian wallet providers need MLR registration. From that date, the new cryptoasset activities need FSMA authorisation, and the application window runs from 30 September 2026 to 28 February 2027.

My firm is MLR-registered. What do I need to do?

Apply for FSMA authorisation in the window if you want to continue. Registrations are not converted, and firms that do not apply must run off their UK cryptoasset business before 25 October 2027.

Can an FCA EMI offer crypto services?

Not under its EMI authorisation alone. The FCA states that EMRs and PSRs firms also need FSMA authorisation for the new cryptoasset activities.
