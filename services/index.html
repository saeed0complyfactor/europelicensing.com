Our licensing services

EMI licence

For firms that issue electronic money, such as prepaid cards and stored-value wallets. An EMI needs at least EUR 350,000 initial capital (EMD2 Art 4) and may also provide payment services under the same licence.

EMI licence

Payment institution licence

For firms that run payment accounts, execute transfers, acquire card payments, send remittances or initiate payments without issuing e-money. Initial capital is EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the services you provide (PSD2 Art 7).

Payment institution licence

MiCA CASP authorisation

For exchanges, custodians, brokers, transfer providers and other crypto-asset service providers in the EU. Minimum capital is EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by service class (MiCA Art 67 and Annex IV).

MiCA CASP authorisation

UK FCA authorisation

For firms with UK customers. The FCA authorises or registers EMIs under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and payment institutions under the Payment Services Regulations 2017, and registers cryptoasset exchange and custodian wallet providers under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (reg 56).

UK FCA authorisation

AML and compliance programme

We write the AML and counter-terrorist financing controls your application must describe (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)) for your product and risk profile. We also draft the governance, ICT, incident and security documents the regulator reviews alongside them (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e) to (j)).

Which licence do you need?

Start with what your product does with customer money.

  • You hold a balance that customers spend later, or you want to issue e-money tokens: you need an EMI licence. E-money is monetary value stored electronically as a claim on the issuer, issued on receipt of funds and accepted by people other than the issuer (EMD2 Art 2(2)). Under MiCA, an e-money token offered to the public must be issued by a credit institution or an EMI (MiCA Art 48(1)).
  • You move money without issuing stored value: a payment institution licence covers the eight payment services in PSD2 Annex I. If you only provide account information services, you register instead of seeking full authorisation (PSD2 Art 33).
  • You exchange, hold, transfer or trade crypto-assets for clients: you need CASP authorisation (MiCA Art 59(1)).
  • You serve customers in the UK: you need FCA authorisation or registration, because an EU or EEA authorisation is valid only in the Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9)).

An EMI may also provide payment services (EMD2 Art 6(1)(a)), so one EMI licence covers a product that needs both. If your volumes are small, a national small EMI or small PI regime may apply where the country offers one (EMD2 Art 9; PSD2 Art 32). Firms registered under those regimes cannot passport (EMD2 Art 9(3); PSD2 Art 32(3)).

Licensing FAQs

What is the difference between an EMI and a payment institution?

An EMI issues electronic money and may also provide payment services (EMD2 Art 6(1)). A payment institution provides payment services only and may not issue e-money, which is reserved for e-money issuers (EMD2 Art 10). An EMI needs EUR 350,000 initial capital (EMD2 Art 4). A payment institution needs between EUR 20,000 and EUR 125,000, depending on its services (PSD2 Art 7).

Do I need a licence in every EU country?

No. An authorisation granted in one Member State is valid in all of them (PSD2 Art 11(9)), and the same rule applies to EMIs through EMD2 Art 3(1). Before you start in a new state, you notify your home regulator (PSD2 Art 28). CASPs follow their own notification route under MiCA Art 65.

Can one licence cover both the EU and the UK?

No. The UK is not a Member State, so the EU passport does not reach it (PSD2 Art 11(9)). UK customers need FCA authorisation or registration under the UK regulations, and an FCA licence gives you no rights in the EU. If you need both markets, you plan two applications.

How long does authorisation take?

For EMIs and payment institutions, the regulator must decide within 3 months of receiving a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). For CASPs, it has 25 working days to check completeness and 40 working days to assess a complete file (MiCA Art 63(2) and (9)), and that clock stops for up to 20 working days while it waits for information it has requested (MiCA Art 63(12)). In the UK, the FCA must decide a complete EMI or PI application within 3 months and any application within 12 months (EMRs 2011 reg 9; PSRs 2017 reg 9). Each clock starts only once the file is complete, so your preparation time comes on top.
