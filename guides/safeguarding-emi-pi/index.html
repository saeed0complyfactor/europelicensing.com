Safeguarding for EMIs and Payment Institutions

Safeguarding means keeping customer money apart from your own and protected if you fail. EU law gives two methods, segregation or insurance, in Article 7 of the e-money directive (EMD2) and Article 10 of the payment services directive (PSD2), and since Regulation (EU) 2024/886 the segregated account can also be at a central bank where that central bank agrees.

What does EU law require for safeguarding?

Two provisions apply: EMD2 Article 7 and PSD2 Article 10, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886.

EMD2 Article 7(1) requires an EMI to safeguard funds received in exchange for e-money. If the customer pays by payment instrument, such as a card, you need not safeguard until the funds are credited to you under the payment services execution rules, and in any case no later than five business days after the e-money is issued. Article 7(2) defines secure, low-risk assets as asset items in the categories of Table 1 of point 14 of Annex I to Directive 2006/49/EC whose specific risk capital charge is no higher than 1.6%, and units in UCITS that invest only in such assets. Competent authorities can exclude assets in exceptional circumstances and with justification. Article 7(3) applies the payment services rules to any activities of an EMI that are not linked to issuing e-money.

PSD2 Article 10(1), as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2024/886, Article 3(1), now names both payment institutions and EMIs. It applies to PIs providing services 1 to 6 of Annex I. It sets two methods:

  1. Segregation. Funds are never commingled with the funds of anyone other than the users they are held for. If you still hold them at the end of the business day after receipt, they go into a separate account at a credit institution, into an account at a central bank at the discretion of that central bank, or into secure, liquid, low-risk assets as defined by your home regulator. They must also be insulated under national law from claims of your other creditors, in particular in insolvency.
  2. Insurance or guarantee. An insurer or credit institution outside your group covers an amount equal to what you would have segregated, payable if you cannot meet your financial obligations.

Where a payment institution uses part of the same funds for non-payment services, the portion to be used for payment transactions is also safeguarded. If that portion varies, Article 10(2) lets you apply a representative portion that can be reasonably estimated from historical data to your regulator's satisfaction.

Member States had to apply the Regulation 2024/886 amendments by 9 April 2025 (Article 5).

Can a payment institution or EMI safeguard funds at a central bank?

The text now allows it, with a limit. Article 10(1)(a) allows deposit "in a central bank at the discretion of that central bank". The central bank decides whether to open an account for you, and the law does not oblige it to. Treat a credit institution account as your base case, and ask the central bank of your home state whether it offers accounts to firms like yours before you rely on that route.

What does the regulator ask to see about safeguarding?

PSD2 Article 5(1)(d) requires the application to include a description of the measures taken to safeguard users' funds. The EBA Guidelines on authorisation (EBA/GL/2017/09) list the content in Guideline 7, with a version for payment institutions and one for EMIs.

If you segregate funds, the description contains:

  • the investment policy that keeps assets liquid, secure and low risk, where relevant;
  • the number of people with access to the safeguarding account and their functions;
  • the administration and reconciliation process that insulates users' funds from your other creditors, especially on insolvency;
  • a copy of the draft contract with the credit institution;
  • an explicit declaration that you comply with PSD2 Article 10.

If you use insurance or a guarantee, it contains:

  • confirmation that the provider is outside your group;
  • the reconciliation process that keeps the cover sufficient at all times;
  • the duration and renewal terms;
  • a copy of the draft agreement or guarantee.

After authorisation you must tell your regulator without undue delay of any change that affects the accuracy of what you filed (PSD2 Article 16). Your safeguarding set-up is part of that information.

Who audits safeguarding?

EU law has no safeguarding-specific audit in EMD2 Article 7 or PSD2 Article 10. What it does have is the statutory audit of a payment institution's annual accounts under PSD2 Article 17(2), and each national regulator then decides how much more to ask. Two examples show the range.

  • Ireland. The Central Bank's Dear CEO letter of 20 January 2023 required a specific safeguarding audit.
  • UK. The FCA's PS25/12 requires certain authorised payment institutions and EMIs to arrange an annual audit of their safeguarding compliance by a qualified auditor. A threshold of relevant funds applies below which no audit is required, and the FCA gives guidance on expectations for firms below it.

What is the UK position and CASS 15?

The UK has its own regime, and a UK licence does not cover the EEA (see UK EMI vs EU EMI). The FCA's Supplementary Regime came into force on 7 May 2026 under PS25/12. The rules are in CASS 10A, CASS 15, SUP 3A and SUP 16.14A. It applies to authorised payment institutions other than those that only provide payment initiation or account information services, authorised EMIs and small EMIs. Small payment institutions may opt in.

The main duties the policy statement confirms are:

  • safeguarding reconciliations at least once each reconciliation day, which excludes weekends, bank holidays and days when relevant foreign markets are closed;
  • a resolution pack of documents that would help return relevant funds to customers in an insolvency;
  • an annual safeguarding audit for certain firms, and a new monthly regulatory return;
  • due diligence when you appoint or review a third party that holds relevant funds or provides an insurance policy or guarantee.

The FCA consulted on a later Post-Repeal Regime that would replace the safeguarding rules in the PSRs 2017 and EMRs 2011 with a CASS-style regime and would require relevant funds to be received into a designated safeguarding account at an approved bank or the Bank of England. It needs legislation, and in PS25/12 the FCA said it will not implement those proposals without further consideration and consultation. Read the current CASS text before you design your accounts. See the FCA authorisation guide.

What this means for you

  1. Choose the method early. Segregation needs a bank willing to hold the account. Insurance needs an insurer or bank outside your group, which means a policy price and a contract before you file. See getting a safeguarding bank account.
  2. Ask your home regulator whether it fixes the method (EMD2 Article 7(4)), and whether it expects a safeguarding audit.
  3. Prepare the Guideline 7 package: the account contract draft, the reconciliation procedure, the list of people with access and the declaration of compliance with PSD2 Article 10.
  4. Design the reconciliation so that you can show, any day, that the amount safeguarded equals what you owe users.
  5. If you also operate in the UK, you will need a second set of rules and a second account structure.

Safeguarding FAQs

What is safeguarding for an EMI?

Protecting the funds you receive in exchange for e-money so that users get their money back if you fail. EMD2 Article 7 sets the duty and PSD2 Article 10 sets the methods.

What are the safeguarding methods for a payment institution?

Segregation in a separate account, or in secure, liquid, low-risk assets, insulated from other creditors, or insurance or a comparable guarantee from an insurer or bank outside your group (PSD2 Article 10(1)).

Can an EMI hold safeguarded funds at a central bank?

Article 10(1) now allows a separate account in a central bank at the discretion of that central bank, so access is not guaranteed.

When must card-funded e-money be safeguarded?

Once the funds are credited to you, and no later than five business days after the e-money is issued (EMD2 Article 7(1)).

Does an EMI need a safeguarding audit?

EU law has no safeguarding audit rule in EMD2 Article 7 or PSD2 Article 10. National regulators can require one: Ireland did in 2023 and the UK has done so since 7 May 2026.

What does CASS 15 cover?

CASS 15 is part of the FCA's safeguarding rules for payment and e-money firms, in force since 7 May 2026 with CASS 10A, SUP 3A and SUP 16.14A.
