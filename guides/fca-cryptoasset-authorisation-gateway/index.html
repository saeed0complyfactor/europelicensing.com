UK Crypto License: the FCA Gateway and Deadlines

You can apply to the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) for UK cryptoasset authorisation until 28 February 2027, and the FCA expects the new regime to start on 25 October 2027. A registration under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLRs) does not convert into that authorisation, so every firm that wants to keep serving UK customers has to apply again.

What is the FCA cryptoasset gateway?

The gateway is the period in which the FCA accepts applications for the new regime. It opened on 30 September 2026 and closes on 28 February 2027. You apply through the FCA's Connect system. A pre-application meeting is optional and the FCA does not charge for it.

A firm that applies inside the window can keep providing cryptoasset services under a saving provision until the application is finally determined, including during any Upper Tribunal appeal. You must tell the FCA when you start and stop relying on that provision, and the method will be set out in a later direction.

The FCA says it expects to decide in-window applications before the regime starts. Section 55V of FSMA gives it 6 months for a complete application and 12 months for an incomplete one. As arithmetic, the 6-month period for a complete application filed on the last day of the window runs to late August 2027, about two months before the expected start. The 12-month period for an incomplete one runs to late February 2028, after the start date. So a complete file matters as much as an early one.

If you apply after 28 February 2027 but before commencement, the FCA will accept the application. It will not speed up the assessment to make up for the late filing. A firm that is not yet authorised when the regime starts enters the transitional provision. It can then carry on activity only as needed to perform contracts made before that point, and it cannot enter new contracts with UK customers, whether new or existing.

What MLR-registered firms must do

Today a cryptoasset exchange provider or custodian wallet provider has to be on the FCA register before it acts (MLRs reg 56(1)(f), (g)). That requirement applies today.

For a firm that is not yet registered, the FCA's deadline to decide a registration is 3 months from receiving the application form, and the clock can restart if it asks for further information. Because of that clock, it says an application made after 31 July 2027 is unlikely to be decided before the new regime starts, and firms should not apply after that date. It also advises applying for early registration only if you are confident it can be granted early enough to be worth the effort. Registration is Category 6 on the fee schedule, at GBP 11,260.

For a firm that is already registered, the practical steps are:

  1. Run a gap analysis against the FSMA requirements. The FCA asks for this explicitly.
  2. Agree a plan at board level.
  3. Apply as early in the window as you can.
  4. Keep your MLR registration and AML controls running until commencement.

A refused MLR registration does not stop you from being authorised later if you meet the standards, according to the FCA's page.

What does a UK crypto license cost?

The FCA fee depends on the highest-category activity you apply for. If you apply for several, you pay the highest fee only.

Category Activities in the fee schedule Fee Source

6 Dealing as principal, safeguarding or arranging safeguarding, issuing a qualifying stablecoin, registering as a cryptoasset firm GBP 11,260 FCA fees
7 Operating a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform GBP 28,150 FCA fees

These are only the regulator's application fees. A full budget also has to cover a UK entity and office, senior management and compliance staff, systems and custody arrangements, audit, any prudential resources the FCA's final rules set, insurance, periodic FCA fees and adviser fees. What this means for you

If you are an MLR-registered firm with UK customers, the deadline that matters is 28 February 2027. Applying inside the window puts you under the saving provision. Applying later puts you at risk of the transitional provision, where you can only perform existing contracts.

If you run an EMI or payment institution and want to add crypto, your current authorisation does not cover it. The FCA says firms under the EMRs or PSRs need FSMA authorisation with the relevant permissions. If you already hold FSMA permissions for other activities, you apply to vary them; if not, you apply for authorisation.

If you hold an EU MiCA authorisation and want UK clients, that licence does not reach the UK. For the EU side, see the MiCA CASP licence guide. The UK licence also gives you no right to serve EU clients.

If you are a new firm, you can still apply in the window with no prior UK status. Use the free pre-application meeting if you are unsure how your activities map to the fee categories.

UK crypto license FAQs

How much does a UK crypto license cost?

The FCA application fee is GBP 2,820, GBP 11,260 or GBP 28,150 by the highest-category activity. Staff, systems, audit and adviser costs come on top. Our fee: our fee range for a crypto license in the UK.

What is the deadline to apply for FCA cryptoasset authorisation?

The application window closes on 28 February 2027. You can apply later, but the FCA will not speed up the assessment, and an unauthorised firm can only perform existing contracts once the regime starts.

When does the UK crypto regime start?

The FCA says it is expected to come into force on 25 October 2027.

Is my MLR registration enough?

No. The FCA states there is no automatic conversion, and registration does not make FSMA authorisation certain.

Do I need FCA crypto registration or authorisation today?

Cryptoasset exchange providers and custodian wallet providers need MLR registration now (MLRs reg 56). Any firm carrying on the new regulated activities needs FSMA authorisation once the regime starts.

Does a MiCA license work in the UK?

No. The UK is outside MiCA, and passporting between the UK and the EEA ended on 31 December 2020.

How long does the FCA take to decide?

FSMA section 55V sets 6 months for a complete application and 12 months for an incomplete one. The FCA says it expects to decide in-window applications before the regime commences.
