MiCA in the EEA: Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein

MiCA applies in Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein because EEA Joint Committee Decision 41/2025 incorporated Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 into the EEA Agreement. Each state has its own act and regulator, and a CASP authorised in one EEA state can serve the others by notification.

How did MiCA become law in Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein?

MiCA is an EU regulation, and the three EFTA states in the EEA are not EU members. A regulation reaches them when the EEA Joint Committee incorporates it into the EEA Agreement. EEA Joint Committee Decision 41/2025 did that on 20 February 2025, for Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, in Annex IX point 31r. The EFTA page records publication in the Official Journal on 8 May 2025 and entry into force in the EEA on 24 June 2025. It also records that Norway fulfilled its constitutional requirements on 10 June 2025, and notes none for Iceland or Liechtenstein.

Each state then passed an act to name its authority, set fees and fit MiCA into national law. Their dates differ, as the table below shows.

Capital is the same in all three: EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class, or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher (MiCA Art 67, Annex IV). The clocks are also MiCA's: 25 working days for completeness, then 40 working days from a complete application, with a pause of up to 20 working days for questions (Art 63).

Norway

The Crypto Asset Act has applied since 1 July 2025, and Finanstilsynet is the authority. You apply through Altinn with form KRT-3060. Finanstilsynet's CASP page names no application fee, and the language is not stated on that page. Its payment and e-money pages require Norwegian, so ask before you prepare documents.

Existing financial firms listed in Art 60, such as banks and investment firms, can notify at least 40 working days before launch, for services that match their licence. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues, and a payment institution has no Art 60 route. AK Jensen Norway AS went this way and has been permitted since 2 February 2026.

Iceland

Act 101/2025 was passed on 24 December 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026. Its Art 1 says MiCA applies through Decision 41/2025, and Art 3 makes the Central Bank the competent authority. Art 15 lets the Minister set supervisory fees by regulation, and the Ministry issued Regulation No 420 on 20 April 2026. The Central Bank issued Rules No 714 on 9 June 2026. We read only the headings of those rules.

The ESMA register lists one Icelandic entry on 23 November 2026, Myntkaup ehf., dated 25 June 2026. The file does not mark it as an Art 63 authorisation or an Art 60 notification. We did not confirm what happened to Icelandic virtual asset firms registered under the older AML regime. See the Iceland page.

Liechtenstein

Liechtenstein put MiCA into law first. The EWR-MiCA-DG has been in force since 1 February 2025, before the Joint Committee adopted the decision on 20 February 2025. The FMA says TVTG service providers could continue under the older Blockchain Act until 1 July 2026, and TVTG registrations for MiCA activities expired on 2 July 2026. Items outside MiCA, such as non-fungible tokens, may still fall under the TVTG, and some providers need authorisation under both regimes.

What this means for you

  • An authorisation from Finanstilsynet, the Central Bank of Iceland or the FMA passports across the EEA, and one from an EU state passports into the three states. The firm notifies its home regulator, which forwards the notice within 10 working days. You may start on receipt or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (MiCA Art 65).
  • Registered office, effective management in the EU and one EU-resident director apply as in every state (Art 59(2)). Iceland applies this through the EEA adaptations in Decision 41/2025, which we did not read, so ask the Central Bank how they apply to your structure.
  • Liechtenstein is the busiest of the three, with six full authorisations, and publishes a fee schedule by service type. Norway has a mix of notifications and authorisations. Iceland has one entry.
  • A firm from outside the EEA cannot rely on the passport. It needs an EEA entity.
  • Compare with an EU state before you choose. See the authorised CASPs tracker, the transitional periods tracker and the MiCA CASP licence guide. Our fee for the file: our fee range for a MiCA CASP licence in Norway, Iceland or Liechtenstein.

MiCA in the EEA FAQs

Does MiCA apply in Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein?

Yes. EEA Joint Committee Decision 41/2025 of 20 February 2025 incorporated Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 into the EEA Agreement, and each state has its own implementing act.

What is EEA Joint Committee Decision 41/2025?

It is the decision that put MiCA into Annex IX of the EEA Agreement, point 31r. It was published in OJ L 2025/765 on 8 May 2025 and entered into force in the EEA on 24 June 2025.

Can a MiCA CASP from Germany or Ireland serve clients in Norway?

Yes, by notification under Art 65 through the home regulator. The same applies to Iceland and Liechtenstein.

Which regulator authorises CASPs in each state?

Finanstilsynet in Norway, the Central Bank of Iceland in Iceland and the FMA in Liechtenstein.

How many CASPs are authorised in Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein?

On 23 November 2026 the ESMA register lists 6 Norwegian entries, 1 Icelandic entry and 12 Liechtenstein entries, six of them Art 63 authorisations. See the tracker.

Does the EEA passport work both ways?

Yes. MiCA is part of the EEA Agreement, so an authorisation from one EEA state passports across the EEA. A firm from outside the EEA cannot rely on it.

What happened to old crypto registrations in these states?

Norway extended its transition to 30 June 2026, Liechtenstein's TVTG registrations for MiCA activities expired on 2 July 2026, and we did not confirm Iceland's position. See the VASP register guide.
