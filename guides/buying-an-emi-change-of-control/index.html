Buying an EMI or PI License vs Applying: Change of Control Explained

You cannot buy a licence by itself. A licence belongs to the authorised company, so buying an EMI or payment institution means buying its shares, and the regulator assesses you as the new owner before the deal completes. We do not list or broker firms for sale, and this guide explains the rules and how the buy route compares with applying.

Buying an EMI or PI license in short

  • A buyer who will reach or exceed 20%, 30% or 50% of the capital or voting rights, or make the firm a subsidiary, must tell the regulator in writing in advance (PSD2 Art 6(1); EMD2 Art 3(3)).
  • You supply the size of the holding and the information the directive lists in Article 23(4) of Directive 2013/36/EU (PSD2 Art 6(2)).
  • The regulator can oppose you if your influence is likely to harm sound and prudent management. Votes you acquire despite opposition can be suspended or annulled (PSD2 Art 6(3), (4)).
  • PSD2 sets no assessment period for this notice, so the timing comes from national law.
  • For CASPs, MiCA fixes it: acknowledgement within 2 working days, assessment within 60 working days, deemed approval if the regulator does not oppose in time (MiCA Art 83).
  • Applying for a new licence has a statutory clock of 3 months from a complete application (PSD2 Art 12).

Sources: PSD2, EMD2, MiCA. Checked on 23 November 2026.

Is there an EMI license for sale?

Listings with that title exist on broker sites, and we do not carry them. What is being sold in those cases is a company that already holds the authorisation. The regulator authorised that company, and it will assess you as the buyer separately.

That has three consequences. You inherit the company's history, including its compliance record, its customer contracts and any open issues with the regulator. The company's capital, safeguarding and business plan stay tied to the authorisation. And a deal that the regulator opposes does not complete cleanly, because votes acquired despite opposition can be suspended or annulled (PSD2 Art 6(4)).

If you are weighing a purchase, ask for the register entry, the authorisation decision and the company's recent correspondence with its regulator, and take advice on the buyer notification before you sign. We do not quote prices or timelines for purchases, because the law sets neither.

What PSD2 Article 6 and EMD2 Article 3(3) require

PSD2 Article 6(1) applies to payment institutions. Anyone who has decided to acquire or increase a qualifying holding, and would reach or exceed 20%, 30% or 50% of the capital or voting rights or make the institution a subsidiary, must tell the competent authority in writing in advance. A seller must do the same before disposing of a holding or reducing it below those levels.

The proposed acquirer supplies the size of the intended holding and the information referred to in Article 23(4) of Directive 2013/36/EU (Art 6(2)). If the acquirer's influence is likely to operate to the detriment of prudent and sound management, the authority opposes or takes other measures. These can include injunctions, penalties against directors or managers and suspension of voting rights (Art 6(3)). If a holding is acquired despite opposition, the Member State must provide for suspended votes, nullity of votes cast or the option to annul them (Art 6(4)).

EMD2 Article 3(3) applies the same rule to EMIs. It lets Member States waive all or part of the obligation for EMIs that carry out the other activities listed in Article 6(1)(e), which covers hybrid firms.

Neither article sets a deadline for the regulator. The home country's law and the regulator's guidance supply it, as the national examples below show.

What MiCA Articles 83 and 84 require for a CASP

MiCA fixes the timetable. The rule applies to a person who decides to acquire a qualifying holding in a crypto-asset service provider, or to increase one so that it reaches or exceeds 20%, 30% or 50% or makes the CASP a subsidiary (Art 83(1)).

Step Period Source
Regulator acknowledges the notice in writing Within 2 working days MiCA Art 83(3)
Regulator assesses the acquisition 60 working days from the acknowledgement MiCA Art 83(4)

No opposition by the deadline Acquisition deemed approved MiCA Art 83(8)

A seller also has to notify the regulator before disposing of a qualifying holding, and again when reducing it below 10%, 20%, 30% or 50% (Art 83(2)).

The regulator weighs five points (Art 84(1)): the acquirer's reputation, the reputation, knowledge, skills and experience of whoever will direct the business, the acquirer's financial soundness, whether the CASP can keep complying with MiCA, and whether there are reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing. It can oppose only on those grounds, or where the information is incomplete or false (Art 84(2)). Member States cannot set a minimum holding level or examine the acquisition for the economic needs of the market (Art 84(3)).

Buying vs applying: how the two routes compare

Point Apply for a new licence Buy a licensed company
Legal step Authorisation of your new company Notice or approval of you as acquirer
Statutory clock 3 months from a complete application (PSD2 Art 12) PSD2 sets none. MiCA: 60 working days for a CASP
What the regulator tests Your business plan, capital, governance, safeguarding, AML Your reputation and finances, the people who will run the firm, continued compliance, ML/TF risk (MiCA Art 84 for CASPs)
Capital You fund the full initial capital, for example EUR 350,000 for an EMI (EMD2 Art 4) The target's capital stays in place, and you pay for the shares
Track record None You take on the company's history
Business changes Written into your plan Material changes still go to the regulator, and new services may need a variation

In a purchase, the regulator's attention moves from your business plan to you and your funds, while the company's existing obligations continue.

What this means for you

If you want to start a new payment or e-money business in a given country, applying is the route with a fixed statutory clock and a clean record. See the EMI licence and payment institution licence guides, and use the country pages for capital and fees.

If you want to buy, treat the regulator notification as the critical path. Check the national rule for your target's country, prepare the Article 23(4) information early and expect the regulator to look at the source of your funds and the people you will put in charge. For a CASP, plan around the 60 working day assessment and the pauses MiCA allows.

If you already hold an EMI and plan a group restructure, a transfer of shares between group entities can still cross a threshold. Treat it as a change of control and notify.

Buying an EMI or PI FAQs

Can I buy an EMI license?

You can buy the shares of a company that holds one. The regulator assesses you as the acquirer first when you cross 20%, 30% or 50%, and it can oppose (PSD2 Art 6; EMD2 Art 3(3)).

Do you have an EMI license for sale?

No. We do not list or broker firms for sale. We cover applications and the change of control process.

Do I need approval for a change of control of an EMI?

You must notify the regulator in advance when a holding would reach or exceed 20%, 30% or 50% (EMD2 Art 3(3)). Several national regulators add their own approval or no-objection step. Member States may waive the rule for hybrid EMIs.

How long does a change of control take for a payment institution?

PSD2 Article 6 sets no period, so it depends on the country. The Netherlands lists 62 business days and Romania up to 60 working days.

How long does a CASP change of control take?

MiCA gives the regulator 60 working days from its written acknowledgement, which comes within 2 working days. An information request can pause the clock for up to 20 working days, and by up to 30 more for a non-EU acquirer (MiCA Art 83).

What happens if I buy without approval?

If a holding is acquired despite the regulator's opposition, the votes can be suspended, treated as void or annulled (PSD2 Art 6(4)). Failing to give prior notice can draw similar measures (Art 6(3)).

Is buying faster than applying?

The directives do not say. Applying has a 3 month clock from a complete application. Buying has no fixed clock outside MiCA, and we do not quote a real-world average.
