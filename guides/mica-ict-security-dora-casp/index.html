MiCA ICT Security and DORA Requirements for CASPs

MiCA does not require a stand-alone smart contract audit or security audit from a crypto-asset service provider (CASP). It does require resilient ICT systems under Article 68(7), and since 17 January 2025 the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) has applied to every CASP authorised under MiCA, with binding rules on ICT risk, incident reporting, testing and third-party providers.

Written by Jan Hagen, regulatory compliance expert. Jan has supported authorisations with the FCA and DNB and has held senior compliance and MLRO roles.

What does MiCA require for ICT security?

MiCA sets the principle in four articles and leaves the detail to DORA.

  • Article 62(2)(j) makes the technical documentation of your ICT systems and security arrangements part of the application, with a description in non-technical language.
  • Article 68(7) requires resilient and secure ICT systems as required by DORA and a business continuity policy that includes the ICT continuity, response and recovery plans of DORA Articles 11 and 12.
  • Article 68(8) requires the mechanisms, systems and procedures that DORA requires, together with risk assessment procedures for national AML law, and a regular check that they work. It also requires systems that protect the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data.
  • Article 73 lets you outsource operational functions but keeps full responsibility with you. You need direct access to the outsourced information and a written agreement.

Some services add security duties of their own:

  • A custodian must have a custody policy that minimises the risk of loss from fraud, cyber threats or negligence (Article 75(3)). It is liable for the loss of crypto-assets or access keys from an incident attributable to it, capped at the market value at the time of loss (Article 75(8)).
  • A trading platform operator must have trading systems that are resilient, tested and covered by business continuity arrangements (Article 76(7)).
  • A transfer service agreement must describe the security systems you use (Article 82(1)(c)).

Does MiCA require a smart contract audit?

No. MiCA requires audits of the reserve of assets of token issuers, and "smart contracts" appears once, in recital 96 on market abuse. Nothing in the CASP title (Title V) makes a smart contract audit or a penetration test a stand-alone licence condition.

The application rules do ask about audits. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/305 sets out what you file under Article 62(2)(j). Article 9 asks for:

  1. Technical documentation of your ICT systems, the distributed ledger infrastructure you rely on and your security arrangements, including the people and tools you use to comply with DORA.
  2. A description of how you manage third-party ICT services that support critical or important functions, with copies of the contracts.
  3. A description of your security and incident management procedures.
  4. If available, a description of a cybersecurity audit by an experienced third-party auditor. The regulation lists the tests it would ideally cover: organisational and physical security, vulnerability scans and network assessments, configuration reviews, penetration tests (black, grey and white box) and, where you use or develop smart contracts, a cybersecurity source code review.
  5. A description of any ICT system audits already done, including of the ledger infrastructure you use.

"If available" means the regulation does not strictly require the audit, and a supervisor still reads the application for evidence that your systems have been tested. The regulation does not say how national regulators weigh a missing audit, so ask your regulator at the pre-application meeting where one is offered.

How does DORA apply to a CASP?

DORA lists CASPs "as authorised under" MiCA among its financial entities (Article 2(1)(f)). It also covers payment institutions and electronic money institutions (Article 2(1)(b) and (d)), so a firm with several licences meets one set of rules. The regulation has applied since 17 January 2025 (Article 64).

Three points shape how much work it is for you.

  1. Proportionality. Article 4 applies the ICT risk rules in line with your size, risk profile and the nature, scale and complexity of your services. A small custodian does not run the same programme as a large exchange.
  2. The simplified framework in Article 16 is for small, non-interconnected investment firms, payment institutions and EMIs that use a regulatory exemption, certain exempted credit institutions and small occupational pension funds. A CASP is not on that list, so the full chapters apply to you.
  3. Supervision. The competent authority for a CASP under DORA is the one designated under MiCA (Article 46(d)). In practice that is the same regulator that authorised you.

What are the DORA incident reporting deadlines for crypto firms?

You report major ICT-related incidents to your competent authority (DORA Article 19(1)). The deadlines come from Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301, Article 5.

Report Deadline
Initial notification As early as possible, within 4 hours of classifying the incident as major and no later than 24 hours from the moment you became aware of it
Intermediate report Within 72 hours of the initial notification, even if nothing has changed. Send an updated one without undue delay, and in any case when regular activity has recovered
Final report No later than one month after the intermediate report, or after the latest updated one

If you cannot meet a deadline, you must tell the authority before it expires and explain why. Where an incident affects client financial interests, you must also inform clients without undue delay once you are aware of it, and tell them what you have done to limit the damage (DORA Article 19(3)). You may outsource the reporting task, but you stay fully responsible for it (Article 19(5)).

What testing and audits does DORA require?

Security testing is a legal duty under DORA.

  • Article 24 requires a digital operational resilience testing programme as part of your ICT framework, unless you are a microenterprise. Tests must be done by independent parties, internal or external. If you use internal testers, you must give them enough resources and avoid conflicts of interest. You need a way to prioritise, fix and validate every weakness found.
  • Article 24(6) requires appropriate tests at least yearly on all ICT systems and applications that support critical or important functions.
  • Article 25 lists the kinds of test: vulnerability assessments and scans, open source analyses, network security assessments, gap analyses, physical security reviews, source code reviews where feasible, scenario-based tests, performance and end-to-end tests and penetration testing.
  • Article 26 requires threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) at least every 3 years, but only for entities identified by the authority under that article, and never for the Article 16 simplified-framework firms. The test runs on live production systems that support critical or important functions. The Commission RTS at OJ L 2025/1190 sets the detail.

What does DORA require for third-party ICT providers?

Most CASPs depend on cloud hosting, custody technology, node or data providers and KYC tools. DORA treats that as ICT third-party risk.

  • Article 28(1) leaves you fully responsible for compliance even where a provider runs the system.
  • Article 28(3) requires a register of information on all contractual arrangements with ICT third-party providers, separating those that support critical or important functions. You report at least yearly to the authority on the number of new arrangements, the provider categories, the contract types and the functions supported, and you hand over the register on request. A Commission ITS sets the templates (OJ L 2024/2956).
  • Article 28(4) requires you to assess a contract before signing it, including concentration risk, and to run due diligence on the provider.
  • Article 30(2) lists the minimum contract content: a description of the services, locations where data is processed, data protection, access and return of data on insolvency or termination, service levels, incident assistance, cooperation with authorities, and termination rights.

The MiCA application asks for the same material, including copies of contracts for services that support critical or important functions (Delegated Regulation 2025/305, Article 9). Prepare the register and the contract set before you file.

What this means for you

If you are applying for authorisation, your ICT file is a core part of the application and the regulator reads it against DORA. Build it in this order.

  1. Name the management body member who owns ICT risk and write the ICT risk framework, the continuity plan and the incident procedure. DORA Article 5 puts the approval and review on the management body.
  2. List every ICT provider, mark which ones support critical or important functions, and collect the contracts. Check each against DORA Article 30(2).
  3. Commission an independent security review before you file. A penetration test of the systems that support client assets and, if you run smart contracts, a source code review, give you evidence for the "if available" line in Delegated Regulation 2025/305.
  4. Set up your incident classification and reporting workflow so that someone can file within 4 hours of classification.
  5. Plan the yearly test cycle and the register update as recurring tasks.

If you already hold an EMI or payment institution licence, much of this exists. MiCA Article 62(4) says the authority cannot ask again for information it already holds under those authorisations, provided it is still up to date.

MiCA and DORA security FAQs

Does MiCA require a smart contract audit?

No article of MiCA requires one for a CASP. If you use or develop smart contracts, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/305 Article 9 asks for a source code review description with the application where one is available.

Does DORA apply to crypto-asset service providers?

Yes. DORA Article 2(1)(f) covers CASPs authorised under MiCA, and the regulation has applied since 17 January 2025 (Article 64).

Is a penetration test mandatory for a CASP?

DORA requires a testing programme and yearly tests on systems that support critical or important functions (Article 24(6)). Penetration testing is one of the listed test types (Article 25(1)). Threat-led penetration testing every 3 years applies only to entities identified by the authority (Article 26(1)).

How fast must a CASP report a cyber incident?

Within 4 hours of classifying it as major and no later than 24 hours after becoming aware of it. The intermediate report follows within 72 hours and the final report within one month (Delegated Regulation 2025/301, Article 5).

Who supervises a CASP under DORA?

The competent authority designated under MiCA (DORA Article 46(d)). It receives your incident reports and the register of information.

Does a CASP need a DORA register of information?

Yes. Article 28(3) requires every financial entity to keep a register of its ICT service contracts and to report at least yearly to the authority.
