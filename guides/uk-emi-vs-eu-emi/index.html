UK EMI vs EU EMI: Do You Need Both?

Yes, if you want to issue e-money to customers in both the UK and the EU or EEA. UK-EEA passporting ended on 31 December 2020, so a licence from the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) does not cover the EEA and an EEA licence does not cover the UK. The rules for capital and the decision period are close to identical. The regulators, fees and small-firm tiers differ.

What changed when passporting ended?

Before 31 December 2020, an EMI authorised in the UK could serve EEA customers on a passport, and the reverse held too. The FCA's page on passported regimes says that passporting between the UK and the EEA ended at the end of the transition period on 31 December 2020, and that EEA firms need UK authorisation where necessary.

An EU or EEA EMI authorisation is valid in all Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9), applied to EMIs by EMD2 Art 3(1)), but the UK is not a Member State. A UK authorisation gives no passport rights in the EEA. If you need both markets, you need two licences: one from the FCA and one from an EU or EEA regulator. Whether a particular state allows anything short of a licence for a particular activity is a question for that state's regulator, so ask before you rely on it.

How do capital and the licence tiers compare?

An authorised EMI needs EUR 350,000 of initial capital in the UK and in every EU state (EMRs Sch 2 para 2; EMD2 Art 4). Own funds for the e-money business are 2% of average outstanding e-money in both (EMRs Sch 2 para 23(2); EMD2 Art 5(3)).

The small-firm tiers differ.

UK EU
Small EMI ceiling Average outstanding e-money up to EUR 5,000,000; unrelated payment transactions up to EUR 3,000,000 a month on average; no account information or payment initiation Waiver up to EUR 5,000,000 of average outstanding e-money in EMD2 Art 9, which each member state may or may not offer
Initial capital for a small EMI 2% of average outstanding e-money once it reaches EUR 500,000 Set by each state that offers the waiver
Passport None None. Small EMIs cannot passport (EMD2 Art 9(3))

The UK's small EMI rules are in the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 (reg 13). In the EU, the waiver comes from EMD2 and the national law decides whether it exists. If you plan a small EMI in the EU, check the country page, because some states do not offer it.

A UK EMI must also carry on at least part of its e-money and payment business in the UK (EMRs reg 6(4A)). An EU EMI is authorised as a legal person established in a Member State, with its head office in the same state as its registered office (PSD2 Art 11(1), (3)).

In both systems the 3-month clock starts only when the file is complete, so preparation and question rounds come on top. The UK rules also set an outer limit of 12 months from receipt for an incomplete application (EMRs reg 9).

Do you need both a UK and an EU EMI?

Your plan What you need
UK customers only An FCA EMI (or a small EMI if you stay under the ceilings)
EU or EEA customers only An EMI from one EU or EEA state, passported to the others
Customers in both An FCA licence and an EU or EEA licence
An EU EMI that wants to serve UK customers UK authorisation where necessary (FCA)
A UK EMI that wants to serve EU customers An EU or EEA licence. The UK licence gives no passport

If you need both, which to apply for first depends on your market. A UK application can run as a small EMI at lower fees if your volumes are modest, but a small EMI is limited in volume and cannot offer account information or payment initiation. An EU EMI passports across the EEA by notification (PSD2 Art 28), so one EU licence reaches many states while the UK licence reaches one market.

Two entities usually follow. The FCA requires part of a UK EMI's business to be carried on in the UK, and an EU EMI must be established in a Member State. Group structure, capital for each entity and the safeguarding account at each credit institution are all separate lines. Plan them together.

UK EMI vs EU EMI FAQs

Can a UK EMI passport into the EU?

No. UK-EEA passporting ended on 31 December 2020.

Can an EU EMI serve UK customers?

Not on a passport. The FCA says EEA firms need UK authorisation where necessary.

Is the capital different for a UK EMI?

No. An authorised EMI needs EUR 350,000 in the UK (EMRs Sch 2 para 2) and in the EU (EMD2 Art 4).

What is the difference between a UK small EMI and an EU small EMI?

The UK has a small EMI tier with a EUR 5m ceiling on average outstanding e-money and a EUR 3m monthly ceiling on unrelated payment transactions. The EU waiver in EMD2 Art 9 is optional for each member state, and neither version can passport.

How long does the FCA take compared with EU regulators?

The FCA must decide a complete application within 3 months and an incomplete one within 12 months of receipt (EMRs reg 9). EU regulators have 3 months from a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). Preparation and question rounds come on top in both.

How much does the FCA charge for an EMI application?

GBP 5,640 for an authorised EMI and GBP 1,130 for a small EMI, per the FCA fee page updated on 19 November 2026. The fee is non-refundable.

Does a UK EMI need safeguarding?

Yes. The FCA's supplementary safeguarding regime has applied since 7 May 2026 (PS25/12).
