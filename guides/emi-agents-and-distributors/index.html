Agents and Distributors: Operating Under Another EMI's License

An agent provides payment services on behalf of a licensed payment institution or EMI, and the institution registers it with its regulator under PSD2 Article 19. A distributor distributes and redeems e-money for an EMI. Neither can issue e-money, and the licensed institution stays fully liable for what they do.

What is the difference between an agent and a distributor?

The two roles sit under different rules, and the choice depends on what the person does for the licensed firm.

Point Agent Distributor
Acts for A payment institution or an EMI An EMI
What it does Provides payment services on the institution's behalf Distributes and redeems e-money on the EMI's behalf
Can it issue e-money? No No
Registration Registered by the institution under PSD2 Art 19 Allowed under EMD2 Art 3(4). Cross-border use follows PSD2 Arts 27 to 31, except Art 29(4) and (5)
Who is liable The institution (PSD2 Art 20(2)) The EMI, which issues and redeems the e-money
Typical case Money remittance counter, retailer taking payments Shop or app selling or redeeming prepaid e-money

The article text keeps the roles apart. EMD2 Article 3(5) says EMIs shall not issue e-money through agents, though they may provide payment services through agents on the Article 19 conditions. Article 3(4) separately lets EMIs distribute and redeem e-money through natural or legal persons acting on their behalf.

How is an agent registered under PSD2 Article 19?

The principal, which is the licensed institution, does the filing. The steps are:

  1. The institution sends its home regulator the agent's name and address, a description of the internal controls the agent will use to meet AML and counter-terrorist-financing obligations, the identity of the agent's directors and managers, and the payment services for which the agent is mandated. Where the agent is not itself a payment service provider, the institution also supplies evidence that those people are fit and proper. If the agent has an identification code, that goes in too (Art 19(1)).
  2. If the regulator considers the information incorrect, it takes further action to verify it before listing the agent. If it is still not satisfied, it refuses to list the agent and tells the institution without undue delay (Art 19(3), (4)).
  3. Within 2 months of receiving the information, the regulator tells the institution whether the agent has been entered in the register. On entry, the agent may start (Art 19(2)).
  4. The institution makes sure the agent tells users that it acts for the institution (Art 19(7)).
  5. Any change, including an additional agent, goes to the regulator without undue delay and follows the same procedure (Art 19(8)).

The register is public. PSD2 Article 14 requires each Member State to keep one listing authorised institutions and their agents, and Article 15 sends that data to EBA's central register.

If you are the agent, you do not hold a licence of your own. The institution does the registration, bears the liability and answers to the regulator. Our country pages record this for several states. In Sweden the principal registers the agent with Finansinspektionen, which decides within two months and charges no fee for it. In the Czech Republic the ČNB enters authorised representatives of Czech institutions in its list. In Croatia, Denmark, Lithuania and Slovenia, an agent of an authorised payment institution from another EEA state needs no local licence.

Can an agent work in another EU country?

Yes, through the principal's passport. PSD2 Article 19(5) sends an institution that wants to provide services in another Member State through an agent to the Article 28 procedure.

The home regulator forwards the notification to the host within 1 month of receiving all the information. The host has 1 month to assess it, in particular for money laundering or terrorist financing concerns about the agent. The home regulator communicates its decision within 3 months, and the agent may start once it is entered in the home register. The institution then tells the home regulator the start date (Art 28(2), (3)).

Two host-state rules can apply afterwards. The host may require periodic reports on activity by agents in its territory (Art 29(2)). A Member State may also require an institution that operates there through agents, with its head office elsewhere, to appoint a central contact point (Art 29(4)). The Article 29(4) rule does not apply to e-money distributors, because EMD2 Article 3(4) excludes Article 29(4) and (5).

For the passport steps in full, see our passporting guide.

How do EMI distributors work?

EMD2 Article 3(4) obliges Member States to let EMIs distribute and redeem e-money through natural or legal persons acting on their behalf. The distributor does not issue anything. The EMI issues the e-money and stays responsible for it.

When the EMI wants to distribute in another Member State through a distributor, Articles 27 to 31 of PSD2 apply with the necessary changes, apart from Article 29(4) and (5). So a cross-border distribution plan goes through the same home and host notification steps as an agent, and you do not need to appoint a central contact point for it.

A few country pages record how distributors are handled locally. The Croatian page restates the EMD2 rule that you may use distributors but not agents to issue e-money. Ireland allows distributors under regulation 20 and requires agents to be registered under regulation 21. Malta requires the EMI to tell the MFSA the details and AML controls of any agent or distributor before use (FIA Art 8A). Cyprus has a form for appointing distributors and agents in Cyprus or abroad.

What this means for you

If you want to offer payments in a country without your own licence, becoming an agent of an authorised institution is a route. Your principal registers you, carries the liability and sets your AML controls. Expect to supply the director and manager details listed in Article 19(1), and expect the principal to monitor you closely.

If you run an EMI and want a retail network, use distributors to distribute and redeem e-money, and keep the issuing in your own hands, because an agent cannot issue it.

If you are the licensed firm and want agents abroad, plan for the Article 28 steps: 1 month for your regulator to forward the file, 1 month for the host, and a decision within 3 months. Build in the host's reporting rules and any central contact point.

If your own activity is regulated in a way an agent cannot cover, such as issuing e-money, you need your own licence. See the EMI licence and payment institution licence guides.

Agents and distributors FAQs

Can I operate under another company's EMI license?

You can be its agent for payment services, or its distributor for e-money distribution and redemption. You cannot issue e-money under its licence through an agent arrangement (EMD2 Art 3(5)).

Can an EMI issue e-money through agents?

No. EMD2 Article 3(5) bars it. An EMI can use distributors and can provide payment services through agents.

How long does agent registration take?

The regulator tells the principal within 2 months whether the agent has been entered in the register (PSD2 Art 19(2)). The agent may start on entry.

Does an agent need its own license?

Not under the PSD2 model. The institution registers the agent with its regulator, and several of our country pages record that an agent of an authorised institution needs no separate local licence. Check the national rule for your case.

Can an agent work in another EU country?

Yes, through the principal's passport. PSD2 Article 19(5) sends it through the Article 28 procedure, and the agent starts on entry in the home register.

Who is liable for an agent's mistakes?

The institution. PSD2 Article 20(2) requires Member States to make payment institutions fully liable for acts of their agents, branches and outsourced entities.

Where can I check whether an agent is registered?

In the home regulator's public register, which PSD2 Article 14 requires and which feeds EBA's central register under Article 15.
