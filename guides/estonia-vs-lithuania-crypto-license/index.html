Estonia vs Lithuania Crypto License After MiCA

Estonia and Lithuania now grant the same thing, a MiCA CASP authorisation with an EU passport, on the same capital and the same decision clock. What differs is the regulator's fee, the language of the file, the history of each country's old crypto regime and the number of firms on each register.

What is the same in Estonia and Lithuania

Both countries apply Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 directly, so the core rules are identical.

  • Capital: class 1 covers order reception and transmission, advice, execution, placing, transfer and portfolio management. Class 2 adds custody and exchange. Class 3 adds a trading platform. You hold the higher of the class figure and a quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads (MiCA Art 67).
  • Clock: the regulator acknowledges receipt within 5 working days, checks completeness within 25 working days and decides within 40 working days of a complete file. Questions can pause the clock for up to 20 working days (Art 63).
  • Substance minimums: a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).
  • Passport: after authorisation you notify your home regulator, which forwards the notice within 10 working days. You can start in the host state on receipt of its communication or on the 15th calendar day (Art 65).
  • There is no small tier, and class 1 is the lowest capital tier in both.

A CASP authorised in either country can serve clients in the other, and in the rest of the EU, by notification, so the home state you pick does not shrink your market.

Where Estonia and Lithuania differ

Regulator and fees

FI's page names a EUR 3,000 processing fee for a CASP application and no processing fee for the passport notification. It states no supervision fee. The Bank of Lithuania sets its fee in a government fee resolution, and we could not confirm an official amount, because the Bank's site blocked our fetch tool and we read it through search-engine extracts. Ask the Bank for the current figure, and see our Lithuania CASP page for the state of that check.

Consultancy websites quote other figures for both countries. We do not use them, because they are not regulator fees.

Filing language

Estonia takes the application in Estonian. English documents are allowed if the application says so. Lithuania accepts Lithuanian or English under the Board's authorisation resolution and may ask for Lithuanian translations of English documents. For a team with no Estonian speaker, Lithuania's filing rule is the simpler one on paper. Check what the regulator expects in meetings and follow-up questions, which the written rules do not cover.

How you apply

FI takes crypto licence applications only through its portal, taotlus.fi.ee, from 18 March 2026. FI's page lists 18 items under MiCA Art 62(2) and ESMA's technical standards, and takes manager details through its fit and proper form. For Lithuania, contact the Bank before you apply and read its CASP authorisation page.

Local presence

Neither page states a local office or staff rule beyond Art 59(2). FI's page names no share capital figure beyond MiCA and no local office rule, so ask FI before you set your board. We could not retrieve the Bank of Lithuania's written view on local staff or office, and our Lithuania page tells you to confirm local expectations before you apply. Settle this with both regulators at the pre-application stage.

Old regimes: what happened to Estonian FIU and Lithuanian VASP status

An old licence or registration does not carry over in either country.

Date Estonia Lithuania
Before MiCA FIU virtual currency licences: 641 valid in mid-2021, 36 at the start of 2026 National VASP registration under AML law
End of transition 1 July 2026: FIU cancelled the register data of the licences End of 2025: 12-month transition from 30 December 2024 ran out
Since then Only firms authorised by FI or another EEA supervisor under MiCA may provide crypto-asset services The old VASP registration no longer authorises crypto-asset services

Estonian FIU licences were not converted into MiCA authorisations. FI's notice says firms without authorisation cannot accept new clients, open accounts or actively market to clients in the EEA, and that applicants awaiting a decision must limit their activity until FI decides. Estonia's FIU also set 20 July 2026 as the deadline for firms that provided virtual currency services in the second quarter to report through the Eesti Pank portal.

Lithuania's transition ended earlier, by the ESMA list, though MiCA capped every national period at 1 July 2026 (Art 143(3)). A firm in either country that did not obtain MiCA authorisation by then cannot serve clients on the old basis.

FI's notice of 30 June 2026 names Lightspark Payments Europe AS as the only CASP FI had authorised at that date. We found no later FI statement with a new count. The ESMA register also lists more than 100 entries that name Estonia as a state of service, so many firms authorised elsewhere may serve Estonian clients.

Counts change weekly. Check the ESMA CASPS file and each regulator's own register before you rely on a number. The register does not show how long any firm waited for its decision.

Which one suits which firm?

We give no winner, because the facts above do not rank the two. These are the points we would weigh with you.

Your team does not speak Estonian

Lithuania accepts filings in English. Estonia accepts English documents when the application says so, but the application itself is in Estonian. Plan translation cost for Estonia.

You hold an old Estonian FIU licence or a Lithuanian VASP registration

The old status is gone in both countries, and an application is a fresh file in either. Nothing in the sources we checked gives a preference to a former local licensee. Pick the home state on the forward-looking points.

You want a regulator with more firms on its register

Lithuania lists 7 home-state entries, 4 of them full authorisations, against Estonia's 3. Neither number is large, and neither tells you how a regulator will treat your model. Ask each regulator about your services at the pre-application stage.

Cost is your main concern

FI states EUR 3,000. The Bank of Lithuania's amount is unconfirmed on our pages. Capital is identical. Both countries also bring the same kinds of cost: company, directors, audit, custody, ICT, AML and advisers. We have no official figures to compare their amounts. For a full cost view see the MiCA license cost guide and the cheapest country guide.

You are an EMI or bank in either country

You may notify under MiCA Art 60 at least 40 working days before starting, instead of applying. The Lithuanian list has three Art 60 notifications. FI's notice says AS LHV Pank and the investment firm Lightyear Europe AS can also provide crypto-asset services in Estonia, but it does not name the route. An EMI is limited to custody and transfer of its own e-money tokens. See the Lithuania EMI page and the Estonia EMI page.

You are Polish or Romanian

Poland has no authority designated for CASPs, and Romania has no confirmed route. A firm based in either applies in another state and passports back. The Poland crypto page compares Estonia and Lithuania as possible home states.

What this means for you

  1. Write down your services and fix your class. It sets capital in both countries.
  2. Decide your filing language. It narrows the choice before cost does.
  3. Ask both regulators the questions our pages could not answer: Lithuania's current fee and local-staff expectations, and Estonia's supervision fee and local-office view.
  4. Compare your findings in a table like the one above, and choose on the points that matter to your model.
  5. Plan for time beyond the statutory clock, which starts only when the file is complete.

Estonia vs Lithuania crypto license: FAQs

Is Estonia or Lithuania better for a crypto license?

The MiCA clocks and capital are the same. Fees, filing language and register size differ: FI states EUR 3,000, the Bank of Lithuania's figure is unconfirmed, Lithuania accepts English filings, and the ESMA register lists 7 Lithuanian and 3 Estonian entries. Choose on your product and team.

Which is cheaper, an Estonian or a Lithuanian crypto license?

We cannot say. FI's EUR 3,000 is the only official regulator fee we confirmed for either country, and an unconfirmed Lithuanian fee is not a zero fee. Capital is the same in both.

Is my old Estonian FIU crypto licence still valid?

No. The FIU cancelled the register data on 1 July 2026, and only firms authorised under MiCA may provide crypto-asset services in Estonia.

Does the Lithuanian VASP registration still work?

No. It no longer authorises crypto-asset services, because Lithuania's 12-month transition ran out at the end of 2025 and MiCA capped all national periods at 1 July 2026.

Can I apply in English?

In Lithuania, yes: the Bank accepts Lithuanian or English. In Estonia, the application is in Estonian and English documents are allowed if the application says so.

Can an Estonian CASP serve Lithuania, and the other way round?

Yes. After your home regulator forwards your Art 65 notification, you can start on receipt of its communication or on the 15th calendar day at the latest.

How long does each regulator take?

MiCA Art 63 gives each 25 working days to check completeness and 40 working days to decide on a complete file, with up to 20 working days of pause for questions. Preparation sits outside the clock.
