Crypto License in Poland in 2026: Why There Is No CASP Route Yet

You cannot get a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) licence in Poland, because no Polish authority has been designated to grant one. A crypto act passed the Sejm in May 2026, the President vetoed it, and the Sejm did not pass it again on 4 September 2026. Polish firms apply for MiCA authorisation in another member state and passport into Poland.

Written by Jan Hagen, regulatory compliance expert. Jan has supported authorisations with the FCA and DNB and has held senior compliance and MLRO roles.

Why is there no crypto license in Poland?

MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, applies directly in every member state. It still needs each state to name the authority that receives applications and to give it powers. Poland has not done that for CASPs. The UKNF says so in its statement: no Polish authority is designated as competent for offerors, issuers of asset-referenced tokens or CASPs, and authorisation proceedings can only start once a law designates one.

The transitional period in MiCA Art 143(3) ended on 1 July 2026 at the latest. The UKNF says the date cannot be extended by law or by a KNF decision. Polish entities that relied on the transition lose the right to provide crypto-asset services under MiCA after that date unless they get authorisation.

So a Polish company cannot apply at home, and one that kept trading after 1 July 2026 on the strength of the Katowice register has no MiCA authorisation behind it. The Tax Administration Chamber in Katowice kept the register of virtual currency activities. It remains an anti-money laundering register, and the UKNF lists it only as the pre-MiCA basis for Polish entities.

What do Polish crypto firms do instead?

There are four routes. Which one fits depends on what you already are.

Route Who it fits Basis
Authorisation in another member state, then an Art 65 passport into Poland A new CASP, or a Polish team with a start-up MiCA Arts 59, 62, 63, 65
Art 60 notification in the home state A bank, investment firm or other listed entity. An EMI may use it only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues MiCA Art 60
Serve Polish clients from an EEA CASP you partner with A firm that does not want its own licence yet MiCA Art 65; UKNF statement
E-money token issuance through a Polish EMI An issuer of e-money tokens UKNF statement; see the Poland EMI page

Under MiCA Art 59(2) the applicant needs a registered office in a member state where it carries on part of its services, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director. A Polish founder can form a company in another state to meet that. Whether a Polish e-money institution can use the Art 60 notification when no Polish authority is designated is a question for the UKNF, so ask it before you build a plan on that route.

Which country do Polish firms apply in?

The capital classes and the clock are the same everywhere: EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000, and 25 working days for completeness plus 40 working days to decide (MiCA Art 63). More than 100 entries on the register list Poland as a state where they may provide services (ESMA file). Polish clients already have authorised providers to choose from, and a Polish firm that wants to compete for them needs its own authorisation elsewhere.

What this means for you

If you run a Polish crypto business that relied on the Katowice register, you have no MiCA authorisation and the UKNF says you cannot rely on the transition any more. Stop onboarding new clients on the old basis, plan an orderly wind-down or a partnership with an authorised CASP, and decide which state you will apply in. Ask the UKNF directly where you stand on existing client assets.

If you are starting out, you do not need to wait for the Sejm. Choose the home state, form the company there with the substance Art 59(2) asks for, and file. Your Polish team can stay in Poland as long as effective management sits in the EU and one director lives in the EU. Check the host-state rules for staff and marketing before you hire.

If you are a bank or investment firm, look at Art 60 first. It replaces an application with a notification at least 40 working days before you start. An EMI can use it only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues (Art 60(4)).

If you are waiting for a Polish licence, the record gives you no date to plan around. A future act would also set its own fees and an authority's process, and none of that exists yet.

Crypto license in Poland FAQs

Can I get a crypto license in Poland in 2026?

No. The UKNF says no Polish authority is designated for CASPs and that proceedings can only start after a law designates one. The act passed on 15 May 2026 was vetoed and not passed again on 4 September 2026.

Is the Polish crypto-asset act in force?

We found no act in force on 23 November 2026. The Sejm record shows the veto stands, and the MPs' bill (print 3173) shows receipt but no reading.

Can I still operate with a Polish VASP registration?

No. The register of virtual currency activities kept in Katowice was the pre-MiCA basis. After 1 July 2026 it gives no right to provide MiCA services.

How do Polish crypto firms get licensed?

They apply in another member state and passport into Poland under MiCA Art 65. The UKNF statement confirms that CASPs from other states may serve Poland.

How much does a crypto license cost in Poland?

There is no Polish fee. Capital is EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class under MiCA, plus the fees of the state where you apply.

Can Polish clients use an EU exchange?

Yes, if the exchange is authorised in an EEA state and has completed its Art 65 notification. Check the ESMA register.

Which regulator supervises a Polish-based CASP?

The regulator of the home state where you are authorised. No Polish authority is designated.
