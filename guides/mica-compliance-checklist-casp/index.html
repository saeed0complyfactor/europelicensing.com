MiCA Compliance Checklist for CASPs

A crypto-asset service provider (CASP) has to meet the duties in Title V of MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, which run from Article 59 to Article 85, and the DORA and AML rules that sit beside it. The checklist below follows the articles in order and says what to hold on file for each.

Which MiCA articles cover getting authorised (Arts 59 to 65)?

Article Duty What to hold on file
59 Provide crypto-asset services in the EU only if authorised as a CASP or entitled under Art 60 Authorisation letter, entry on the ESMA register
60 Credit institutions, CSDs (custody only), investment firms, UCITS management companies and AIFMs, and market operators (trading platform only) notify at least 40 working days before starting, instead of applying. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues (Art 60(4)). Payment institutions are not listed, so they need full authorisation The notification and the regulator's acknowledgement
61 A third-country firm is exempt only for services a client requests at its own exclusive initiative. Soliciting EU clients, by any channel, removes the exemption A reverse solicitation policy, if you are outside the EU. ESMA has published guidelines on it (ESMA35-1872330276-1899)
62 Application contents, points (a) to (s), including governance, internal controls, ICT documentation, client asset segregation and complaints procedures. The regulator cannot ask again for information it already holds under EMD2, MiFID II or PSD2, if still current (Art 62(4)) The application file and a change log
63 Assessment and decision by the home authority Dated correspondence with the regulator
64 Withdrawal. Mandatory if you do not use the authorisation within 12 months, provide no services for nine consecutive months, or lack effective AML systems Evidence of live activity, and AML testing records
65 Passport notification to your home authority to serve other Member States List of Member States, services and start date

The transitional period for pre-MiCA firms ended on 1 July 2026, so the route now is full authorisation or the Art 60 notification (ESMA statement of 23 June 2026).

Which MiCA articles set the ongoing duties (Arts 66 to 74)?

Article Duty What to hold on file
66 Act honestly, fairly and professionally in clients' best interests. Provide information that is fair, clear and not misleading, marketing identified as such. Warn clients of risks. Give hyperlinks to white papers when operating a trading platform, exchanging, advising or managing portfolios. Publish your pricing, costs and fees policy and the environmental impact information for each crypto-asset Client terms, marketing sign-off log, website disclosures
67 Prudential safeguards at all times: the higher of the Annex IV minimum and one quarter of the fixed overheads of the preceding year, reviewed annually. Form: CET1 own funds, an insurance policy covering the territories where you serve clients, or a combination Annual overhead calculation, capital evidence, any insurance policy (which must be published on your website)
68 Management body of good repute and with enough knowledge and time. Qualifying shareholders of good repute. Policies to comply with MiCA. Staff with the right skills. Periodic review of policy effectiveness by the management body (Art 68(6)). Resilient ICT and a business continuity policy under DORA. AML systems. Records of all services, orders and transactions Fit and proper files, policy set, board minutes, continuity plan, record retention schedule
69 Tell your authority without delay of any change to the management body, before the new member starts Change notification template
70 Protect clients' ownership rights, especially on insolvency, and do not use their crypto-assets for your own account. Place client funds (other than e-money tokens) with a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day. The funds rule does not apply to CASPs that are EMIs, payment institutions or credit institutions Account agreements, reconciliation records
71 Effective, published complaints procedure. Complaints free of charge. Template for clients and a record of every complaint Complaints register and published procedure
72 Conflicts of interest policy, disclosure on your website in a prominent place, and a review at least annually Policy, disclosure page, annual review record
73 Outsource operational functions without delegating responsibility. Keep the expertise to supervise the provider and give the authority access Written outsourcing agreements and a monitoring file
74 If you provide custody, trading platform, exchange, order execution or placing services (Arts 75 to 79), keep a plan for an orderly wind-down that avoids undue economic harm to clients Wind-down plan, tested and updated

Which MiCA articles apply to each crypto-asset service (Arts 75 to 82)?

Each article applies only to a service you provide, so list every service you plan to offer in your application.

Service Article Core duties
Custody and administration 75 Written client agreement, custody policy that minimises loss from fraud, cyber threats or negligence, a register of positions per client, statements at least every three months, segregation from your own assets and legal segregation from your estate, liability for losses from incidents attributable to you capped at market value, and use of other custodians only if they are authorised CASPs
Trading platform 76 Operating rules including admission and due diligence, resilient and tested trading systems, market abuse detection and reporting to your authority, public bid and ask data
Exchange for funds or other crypto-assets 77 Non-discriminatory commercial policy, a published firm price or pricing method, execution at the displayed price, publication of transaction volumes and prices
Order execution 78 Best possible result for clients, a written execution policy, clear information to clients and their prior consent
Placing 79 Tell the offeror the type of placement, the fees, timing, price and targeted purchasers, and get the issuer's agreement, with specific conflict procedures
Reception and transmission of orders 80 Prompt and proper transmission, no remuneration for routing orders to a venue, no misuse of order information
Advice and portfolio management 81 Suitability assessment, disclosure of whether advice is independent, and proof that advisers have the knowledge and expertise (Art 62(2)(q))
Transfer services 82 Written client agreement describing the service, security systems, fees and applicable law

Which MiCA articles cover ownership changes and size (Arts 83 to 85)?

  • Article 83: anyone who decides to acquire a qualifying holding, or to increase one so that it reaches or passes 20%, 30% or 50% of voting rights or capital, or makes the CASP a subsidiary, must notify your authority first. A person who plans to sell or reduce a qualifying holding must also notify the authority beforehand. Article 84 sets the assessment criteria, which include the acquirer's reputation and financial soundness.
  • Article 85: a CASP with an average of at least 15 million active users in the Union in a calendar year counts as significant. You must notify your authority within two months of reaching that number.

Put these triggers in your shareholder agreements and your change-of-control procedure.

What else must a CASP comply with besides MiCA?

  • DORA. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 has applied since 17 January 2025 and covers CASPs authorised under MiCA. MiCA Article 68(7) and (8) cross-refer to it. See the MiCA ICT security and DORA guide.
  • Travel rule. Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 has applied since 30 December 2024 and requires originator and beneficiary information with each crypto-asset transfer (Article 14).
  • AML. MiCA Article 68(8) requires systems to comply with national AML law, and Article 64(1)(f) makes failing to have effective AML systems a mandatory ground for withdrawal. See AML and MLRO requirements.
  • Your national rules on top: tax, consumer law and marketing. These sit outside MiCA, so check them with your home regulator and local counsel.

What this means for you

Use the checklist as a yearly control calendar as well as an application guide.

  1. Before filing, build each file the right-hand column names. The regulator tests the application against Article 62 and its delegated rules.
  2. After authorisation, put the annual items in a calendar: overhead and capital calculation (Art 67) and conflicts policy review (Art 72). Add the periodic management body review of policies (Art 68(6)), which MiCA does not fix at a set frequency, and your own complaints procedure review.
  3. Set event triggers: change of director (Art 69), qualifying holding changes (Art 83), new Member State (Art 65), new service (extension of authorisation under Art 59).
  4. Watch the withdrawal triggers in Article 64. A licence you do not use for 12 months, or a service you stop for nine consecutive months, can be withdrawn.
  5. Keep records of all services, orders and transactions for five years, or up to seven where your authority asks before the five years end (Art 68(9)). Clients can ask for their records.

MiCA compliance checklist FAQs

What are the main MiCA requirements for a CASP?

Authorisation (Art 59), minimum prudential safeguards (Art 67), fit and proper management and shareholders (Art 68), safekeeping of client assets and funds (Art 70), complaints and conflicts procedures (Arts 71 and 72), outsourcing control (Art 73) and the specific service rules in Arts 75 to 82.

What is the minimum capital for a CASP?

EUR 50,000 for class 1 services, EUR 125,000 for class 2 and EUR 150,000 for class 3 (MiCA Annex IV). The requirement is the higher of that figure and a quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads (Art 67(1)).

Does a CASP have to comply with DORA?

Yes. DORA covers CASPs authorised under MiCA and has applied since 17 January 2025.

How soon must a CASP tell the regulator about a new director?

Without delay, and before the new member exercises activities (MiCA Art 69).

When can a CASP lose its authorisation?

The authority must withdraw it if you do not use it within 12 months, provide no services for nine consecutive months, or fail to keep effective AML systems, among other grounds (MiCA Art 64(1)).

Can a non-EU firm serve EU clients without authorisation?

Only where the client starts the service at its own exclusive initiative. Soliciting or advertising to EU clients removes that exemption (MiCA Art 61).
