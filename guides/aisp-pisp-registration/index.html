AISP and PISP: Open Banking Registration in the EU

An account information service provider (AISP) registers under PSD2 Art 33 with no initial capital but with professional indemnity insurance. A payment initiation service provider (PISP) is authorised as a payment institution with EUR 50,000 of initial capital and the same kind of insurance. National regulator fees for an AISP registration run from EUR 693 in Lithuania to CHF 15,000 in Liechtenstein, and Ireland and Spain charge nothing to apply.

Written by Jan Hagen, regulatory compliance expert. Jan has supported authorisations with the FCA and DNB and has held senior compliance and MLRO roles.

What does PSD2 require for AISPs and PISPs?

PSD2 Annex I lists payment initiation as service 7 and account information as service 8.

A PISP needs authorisation as a payment institution before it starts (PSD2 Art 11(1)). It must hold EUR 50,000 of initial capital at all times (Art 7(b)). Its own funds may not fall below that initial capital (Art 8(1)), but the Method A, B and C calculation in Art 9 is not applied to firms that offer only service 7 or 8 (Art 9(1)). The application includes professional indemnity insurance, or another comparable guarantee, covering the territories in which it offers services and the liabilities specified in Arts 73, 89, 90 and 92 (Art 5(2)).

An AISP registers instead of applying for authorisation (Art 33(1)). It does not need initial capital or the safeguarding description. It keeps a shorter file: the programme of operations, business plan, governance and internal controls, incident procedure, sensitive payment data process, business continuity, security policy, structural organisation, identity and suitability of directors, legal status and head office address (Art 5(1) points (a), (b), (e) to (h), (j), (l), (n), (p), (q)). It also holds professional indemnity insurance covering its liability to the account servicing provider or the user from unauthorised or fraudulent access to account information (Art 5(3)).

An AISP is treated as a payment institution, except that Titles III and IV, the transparency and conduct rules, do not apply to it apart from a short list of articles (Art 33(2)). A firm that provides both services is authorised, not registered, and carries both insurance duties.

Art 33(1) does not list the Art 12 decision period among the provisions that still apply, so the clock for a registration comes from national law. The Banco de España, for example, states 3 months for an AISP registration (guide section 1.8).

How much professional indemnity insurance do you need?

PSD2 Art 5(4) told the EBA to set the criteria. The EBA's guidelines (EBA/GL/2017/08), applicable from 13 January 2018, tell regulators to use a formula with three parts: a risk profile amount, a type of activity amount and a size of activity amount (Guideline 3).

  • Risk profile: the value of refund requests in the past 12 months, plus the number of initiated payments for a PISP or accounts accessed for an AISP, on a sliding scale from 40% of the first 10,000 down to 0.025% above 10 million (Guideline 5).
  • Type of activity: set to zero for a firm that provides only PIS or only AIS. A firm that provides both is calculated for each service and the results are added. Other non-payment business adds EUR 50,000 (Guideline 6).
  • Size of activity: the value of payments initiated for a PISP, or the number of clients for an AISP, on a similar sliding scale (Guideline 7).

A new firm uses its forecasts, and where the forecast is missing or the resulting value for an indicator is below 50,000, the regulator sets that indicator at 50,000, which for refund requests means EUR 50,000 (Guidelines 5.4, 5.7, 5.10 and 7.4). The cover must be a policy or a comparable guarantee, not both (1.1), without an excess or deductible that could prejudice refunds (1.4), and it must cover every territory in which you offer services (1.5). You review the amount at least annually and recalculate it if necessary (9.1).

Regulators should publish the criteria, indicators and formula for their state (Guideline 4). Ask it for the figures before you approach insurers.

Do national rules change the PISP capital?

Most states copy the EUR 50,000 figure. Two list it in local currency.

Registered AISPs hold no initial capital in the states whose pages we reviewed, including Ireland, Lithuania, Malta, Hungary and Slovenia. See capital requirements for the full comparison.

Can an AISP or PISP passport?

A PISP holds a payment institution authorisation, which is valid in all Member States (PSD2 Art 11(9)). It notifies its home regulator before serving another state (Art 28). Malta's Financial Institutions Act extends the passport to registered AISPs (Art 4(6)). The mechanics are in the passporting guide.

What will PSD3 change for open banking firms?

The rules above are current law. Under the Commission proposal (COM(2023) 366) and the Council position, which are not adopted, PIS and AIS providers could hold initial capital or own funds instead of professional indemnity insurance, because insurance at authorisation had proved hard to obtain. PIS capital stays at EUR 50,000. The Commission proposal also adds a machine-readable list of PISPs and AISPs. On the conduct side, the PSR proposal and Council text require banks to give open banking providers a dedicated interface or fair access, without artificial obstacles. See the PSD3 and PSR guide.

What this means for you

  • Read-only data product: register as an AISP. Budget for insurance, a short governance file and the national fee, and no capital.
  • Payment initiation, even alone: apply for authorisation, hold EUR 50,000 and the insurance, and expect the full payment institution file.
  • Both services, or any other payment service: apply as a payment institution. See the payment institution licence.
  • Insurance first: ask your regulator for its formula and an insurer for a quote before you set a launch date, because the cover is a condition of registration or authorisation.
  • Cross-border plan: check whether your home state treats a registered AISP as passportable before you pick it.

AISP and PISP FAQs

Do you need a licence to be an AISP?

No licence. You register (PSD2 Art 33). You still hold professional indemnity insurance (Art 5(3)).

How much capital does a PISP need?

EUR 50,000 at authorisation and at all times (PSD2 Art 7(b)), plus professional indemnity insurance (Art 5(2)).

What is the difference between an AISP and a PISP?

An AISP reads account data for the customer. A PISP starts a payment from the customer's account at another provider. Only the PISP needs authorisation and initial capital.

How is the insurance amount calculated?

By the EBA formula in EBA/GL/2017/08: a risk profile, type of activity and size of activity amount, with a floor of 50,000 for each indicator when forecasts are missing or low. It is reviewed at least annually.

How long does registration take?

PSD2 does not set a clock for registration in Art 33. National rules do. Spain states 3 months for an AISP registration. Ask your regulator.

Can one firm be both an AISP and a PISP?

Yes. It applies for authorisation covering both, and the insurance covers both sets of liabilities, with the amounts calculated separately and added (EBA/GL/2017/08, 6.3).
