Cyprus EMI and PI License: the Central Bank's Pre-Application Stage

Since 3 August 2026 the Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) starts every electronic money institution (EMI) and payment institution (PI) application with a pre-application exploratory stage. You send a Pre-Application Document with a non-refundable EUR 5,000 fee, the CBC assesses it within 30 working days (50 if it asks for a due diligence report), and only then can you file the EUR 10,000 application.

Written by Jan Hagen, regulatory compliance expert. Jan has supported authorisations with the FCA and DNB and has held senior compliance and MLRO roles.

What changed on 3 August 2026?

The CBC's licensing page describes a procedure with three stages: a pre-application exploratory stage, an application and assessment stage, and post-authorisation notifications. The first stage is new for applicants. It applies to both EMI and PI licences, and the CBC's pre-application page says the applicant states which of the two it intends to apply for.

Third-party websites that put the Cyprus application fee at EUR 5,000 may be mixing the new pre-application fee with the application fee, or describing the procedure as it was before 3 August 2026. The CBC pages show two separate payments: EUR 5,000 first, EUR 10,000 with the application.

How does the pre-application stage work?

  1. You complete the CBC's Pre-Application Document and email it to the Licensing Section, saying you intend to apply for an EMI or PI licence. You attach proof of payment of the EUR 5,000 fee. The CBC says the fee goes to its "Application Fees" account.
  2. The CBC assesses the reputation of the ultimate beneficial owners and the group, and the risk of your business model. It may request an enhanced due diligence report from an independent third party. You can supply it, or the CBC can obtain it at your cost.
  3. The assessment must finish within 30 working days, or 50 working days if the report is requested.
  4. The CBC invites the beneficial owners and your representatives to an initial meeting, gives feedback and flags concerns.

The CBC states that completing this stage is not an approval. The page does not say what happens if the feedback is negative, so ask the Licensing Section before you pay the fee if you have a point you want to test.

What happens after the pre-application stage?

The formal application goes through the CBC e-platform. For an EMI you submit the application form, the QHL or QHN forms for qualifying shareholders and the MMB/KFH questionnaires for managers, with the EUR 10,000 receipt. A PI application follows the same pattern with the PI form.

The CBC publishes a list of common errors seen in EMI and PI applications. Check your file against it before you submit. During assessment the CBC verifies the information and keeps in touch. If you miss a deadline it sets, it warns you and then treats the application as withdrawn.

For the decision, EU law gives the regulator 3 months from a complete application and requires reasons for a refusal (PSD2 Art 12, applied to EMIs by EMD2 Art 3(1)). The CBC pages do not restate that period. Pre-application time, preparation and question rounds come on top of the statutory clock, so we do not quote a total timeline.

What this means for you

Budget EUR 15,000 in regulator fees before any capital or advisers: EUR 5,000 for the pre-application stage and EUR 10,000 for the application. Add a due diligence report if the CBC asks for one, since you may have to pay for it.

Prepare the beneficial owner story first. The pre-application stage is about who owns and controls the group and whether the business model is credible. Put your ownership chart, source of funds and the people who will attend the initial meeting in order before you pay the fee.

Plan the Cypriot substance early. The CBC authorises only a company incorporated in Cyprus with registered and head office there. Directors and management go through the CBC's suitability assessment under its 2025 directive.

Do not read the stage as a shortcut. The CBC says completing it is not approval, and the formal application then runs its own assessment.

Cyprus pre-application stage FAQs

What is the new pre-application stage for Cyprus EMIs and PIs?

From 3 August 2026 the CBC requires a Pre-Application Document and a non-refundable EUR 5,000 fee before any formal application. It assesses the beneficial owners' reputation and the business risk, may request an enhanced due diligence report and holds an initial meeting.

How long does the pre-application stage take?

The CBC's assessment must finish within 30 working days, or 50 working days if it asks for an enhanced due diligence report. The initial meeting follows.

How much is the Cyprus EMI application fee?

EUR 10,000, paid with the application. The EUR 5,000 pre-application fee is paid earlier and is separate.

Is the pre-application stage an approval?

No. The CBC says completing it is not approval.

How much capital does a Cyprus EMI need?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMI Law s 12(1); EMD2 Art 4). A PI needs EUR 125,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 20,000 by service (PSD2 Art 7).

Do you need an office in Cyprus?

Yes. You must be a Cypriot company with registered and head office in Cyprus and carry out at least part of your business there.

How long does the whole licence take?

EU law gives the regulator 3 months from a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). The pre-application stage and your own preparation come on top, and we do not quote a total.
