Why EMI Applications Get Refused or Delayed

Regulators say the same few things: incomplete files, missing safeguarding proof, business models that change mid-application, and weak local substance or fit and proper evidence. The statements below describe delay and dismissal for incomplete files more than they describe formal refusal. The Central Bank of Ireland reports an average of 763 calendar days for payment and e-money applications in 2025.

What does the law say about refusal?

PSD2 Art 11(2) tells the regulator to grant authorisation if the file meets all the requirements of Art 5 and its overall assessment is favourable. EMD2 Art 3(1) applies these rules to EMIs. The refusal grounds sit in four places:

  • Governance. The regulator grants authorisation only if you have sound governance, with a clear organisational structure, risk procedures and internal controls proportionate to your business (PSD2 Art 11(4)).
  • Shareholders. It must refuse if it is not satisfied about the suitability of shareholders with qualifying holdings (Art 11(6)).
  • Substance. Your head office must be in the same Member State as your registered office, and you carry out part of your business there (Art 11(3)).
  • Completeness. The file lists the programme of operations, a 3-year business plan, capital evidence, safeguarding measures, governance, incident, business continuity and security descriptions, and fit and proper evidence for directors (Art 5(1)).

The decision clock is 3 months from receipt of the application or, if it is incomplete, of all the information needed, and the regulator must give reasons for a refusal (Art 12). Because the clock waits for a complete file, each round of missing information pushes the start back.

What do regulators say about delays?

Netherlands: DNB, 19 March 2026

DNB published revised explanatory notes for payment service providers and EMIs on 19 March 2026. It describes two phases. Phase 1 checks that the application is complete. Phase 2 is the substantive assessment of organisation, governance and safeguarding. DNB processes only fully submitted applications. It generally asks once for missing documents. If the file is still incomplete, phase 2 does not start, the application may be dismissed and the fees remain payable.

For the customer accounts foundation and segregated assets account methods, DNB says proof of an opened bank account is often missing, which can lead to significant delays or dismissal. It requires either proof of an opened account or a bank-signed commitment letter showing that customer acceptance is complete and the account is blocked until the licence is granted. DNB's application timeline page adds that it may cease consideration within four weeks if requested information is missing or insufficient. For an EMI, DNB also expects at least two day-to-day policymakers working from the Netherlands.

Ireland: Central Bank of Ireland, June 2026

The Authorisations and Gatekeeping Report (Edition 3, June 2026) records nine payment and e-money applications received in 2025, two withdrawn or closed, four approved and 13 firms in the pipeline at year end. Average time from initial application to authorisation rose from 688 days in 2024 to 763 days in 2025. The shortest was 497 days.

The Central Bank gives its own reasons: a lack of preparedness by some applicants, delays in responding to its queries, and fundamental changes to business models or business plans during assessment. It also says some firms drew out the process through a lack of operational readiness. Two firms withdrew at an advanced stage for group strategy reasons. Its chapter on expectations describes what makes the pathway efficient: understanding the regulations, early engagement and timely responses, a clear business model that does not change mid-stream, strong governance and local risk frameworks instead of reliance on group frameworks, proof of capital and financial viability, and a substantive presence in the jurisdiction.

The report also says its Gatekeeping Decisions Committee has received two proposed authorisation refusals across all sectors since it was created, and refused both. In its fitness and probity section it records one 2025 application, across sectors, refused because the sole proposed role holder did not demonstrate fitness and probity.

United Kingdom: FCA

The FCA rejects an application without assessing it if the fee is wrong or the minimum information is missing, and expects answers to every question on the form. Its Approach Document (March 2026, version 7) says an incomplete application must be decided within 12 months of receipt and is likely to be refused if the information is still missing (para 3.198). A refusal needs a warning notice with 28 days for representations, then a decision notice, and the firm can refer the matter to the Upper Tribunal (paras 3.204 to 3.206). The FCA takes non-disclosure of material facts very seriously (para 3.118). A firm can withdraw before a decision, but the fee is not refunded (para 3.199).

Finland: FIN-FSA

FIN-FSA states that the whole process averages six to twelve months and depends mostly on application quality. The statutory 3-month period only runs from complete documentation. It charges the processing fee for refusals as well as approvals.

Is there a crypto precedent?

Denmark's Finanstilsynet refused a MiCA CASP application from Kittrow Finance ApS on 21 August 2025 and the Business Appeals Board ruled on 10 November 2025 that the appeal does not suspend the decision. Our Denmark CASP page read this through a search extract, which gave too little staff and management presence in Denmark as the main ground. We could not open the Finanstilsynet page, so treat the ground as unverified. It concerns a CASP, not an EMI, and is listed here because the same substance question comes up for EMIs.

Will refusals be easier to track under PSD3?

The Council position on the PSD3 directive, which is not adopted law, asks every applicant to list the Member States where it or its group has applied in the past three years, and to give any decision on those applications with the main reasons for refusal (Art 3(3)(q)). A refusal in one state would then have to be disclosed to the next regulator. See the PSD3 and PSR guide.

What this means for you

  • Send a complete file the first time. DNB asks once, the Bank of Lithuania can return it within 5 business days, and the FCA rejects it without assessment.
  • Open the safeguarding account or get the bank's commitment letter before you apply in the Netherlands.
  • Fix your business model before you file. The Central Bank of Ireland names mid-application changes as a cause of delay.
  • Bring local people and decisions. DNB expects two policymakers in the Netherlands, and the Central Bank of Ireland expects local risk frameworks and substance.
  • Prepare fit and proper evidence for every director and qualifying shareholder, and disclose everything material.
  • Plan the calendar on regulator averages, not on the 3-month legal clock. Use the best country guide to compare.

EMI refusal and delay FAQs

Why are EMI applications refused?

Regulators cite incomplete or poor quality files, missing safeguarding evidence, unsuitable shareholders or managers, and insufficient local governance (PSD2 Art 11(4), (6); DNB; Central Bank of Ireland; FCA).

How long do EMI applications actually take?

The legal clock is 3 months from a complete file (PSD2 Art 12). The Central Bank of Ireland reports an average of 763 calendar days for payment and e-money applications in 2025, and FIN-FSA says six to twelve months on average.

What does DNB say about missing bank account proof?

On 19 March 2026 DNB said proof of an opened bank account is often missing for two of the safeguarding methods, which can lead to significant delays or dismissal. It accepts a bank-signed commitment letter instead.

Can I withdraw instead of being refused?

In the UK you can withdraw before a decision, but the fee is not refunded (FCA Approach Document, para 3.199). Hungary does not refund the fee on refusal or withdrawal (MNB Decree 23/2026), and FIN-FSA charges for refusals and for costs already incurred on withdrawal.

Can I reapply after a refusal?

Bulgaria sets a wait of 6 months after a refusal takes effect (ZPUPS Art 15(2)). The other country pages state no such wait, so ask the regulator.

Does silence from the regulator mean approval?

Not for authorisation. In Spain and Malta silence after 3 months counts as refusal, and in Luxembourg a deemed refusal follows after 12 months.
