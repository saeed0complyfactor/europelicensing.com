MiCA License Cost by Country

A MiCA licence has three costs: the regulator's fees, the capital you must hold, and the cost of running the firm the regulator authorises. Published application fees run from nothing in Portugal to EUR 30,000 and more: EUR 30,000 in Luxembourg, EUR 30,677.51 for a class 3 licence in Bulgaria, up to EUR 30,000 per service in Cyprus and up to EUR 100,000 by the hour in the Netherlands. Capital is EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by service class in every EU state.

MiCA license cost in short

  • Regulator fees are set nationally. Every figure here comes from a regulator's fee schedule or the law that sets it. The cheapest country guide sets the fees side by side.
  • Capital is set by MiCA Art 67 and Annex IV: EUR 50,000 (class 1), EUR 125,000 (class 2) or EUR 150,000 (class 3), or one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads if higher (ESMA).
  • Capital stays in your company. It is a safeguard and a balance sheet item, and the regulator does not collect it.
  • Several regulators charge an annual fee that matches or exceeds the application fee, including Luxembourg, Malta and France, and Liechtenstein for custody, platform or exchange firms.
  • The other first-year lines, such as staff, audit and custody systems, have no official price. Our pages list them and give no amounts.
  • Poland has no authority designated for CASPs, and Romania has no confirmed route. Neither has a fee to quote.

What goes into a MiCA license cost

Part What it is Who sets it
Regulator fees Application or processing fee, then annual supervision The national regulator's fee schedule
Capital Minimum own funds, insurance or a mix, held in the firm MiCA Art 67 and Annex IV, the same in every state
Running the firm Staff, office, audit, custody and ICT systems, AML, advisers You and your suppliers

Regulators publish only the fees, so those are the only figures you can compare by country. Running costs differ too, because local substance and language rules differ, but no regulator prices them.

MiCA capital by class (Art 67)

Class Minimum capital Services it covers
1 EUR 50,000 Order reception and transmission, advice, execution, placing, transfer, portfolio management
2 EUR 125,000 Class 1 plus custody and exchange
3 EUR 150,000 Class 2 plus operating a trading platform

Source: ESMA, MiCA Annex IV. The class list follows the Lithuania CASP page.

Three rules from Art 67 shape the amount you hold:

  1. You hold the higher of the class minimum and one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads (Art 67(1), (2)). In your first year you use projected overheads.
  2. The safeguard can be common equity tier 1 own funds, an insurance policy or both (Art 67(4)).
  3. The same Annex IV figures apply in every member state, so capital does not differ between countries.

Some firms that already hold a licence, such as credit institutions and investment firms, can notify under MiCA Art 60 instead of applying, at least 40 working days before they start. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues (Art 60(4)), and a payment institution has no Art 60 route. Bulgaria's FSC charges its class fee by the services you notify, and FIN-FSA charges EUR 3,500 for a notification. See the MiCA CASP licence guide.

First-year cost lines for a MiCA licence

After the regulator and capital, a first-year budget carries these lines. Our pages give no amounts for them, because they depend on your model and the country.

  • Company formation and a registered office in the state where you apply.
  • At least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)) and the staff the regulator expects locally.
  • Translation, where the file must be in a local language. Bulgaria requires Bulgarian. Estonia takes Estonian and allows English documents if the application says so.
  • Statutory audit.
  • Insurance or own funds for the Art 67 safeguard.
  • A client-funds account at a credit institution or central bank, with client funds placed by the end of the next business day (Art 70).
  • Custody arrangements: a client agreement, a custody policy, a position register and statements at least quarterly (Art 75).
  • ICT systems that meet DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (Art 68(7)).
  • AML tooling and the AML/CFT controls Art 68(8) requires. Plan for a named AML compliance officer.
  • Legal and policy drafting for the Art 62(2) file.
  • A payment institution licence, if your service also counts as a payment service. The EBA advised authorities in June 2025 to require one from 2 March 2026 for certain e-money token services.

Later events carry their own fees. Bulgaria charges EUR 6,135.50 to extend a class 1 licence to class 2, EUR 18,917.80 from class 2 to class 3, and EUR 800 to 2,900 per acquirer of a qualifying holding. Other regulators charge for changes under their own schedules.

What this means for you

  1. Work out your services and their class. The class sets your capital and, in Austria, Bulgaria and Malta, your regulator fee. In Croatia, Cyprus, Slovakia, Slovenia and Liechtenstein the services you choose set the fee.
  2. Treat capital as a balance sheet item. It stays in the firm.
  3. Add the application fee and the first annual fee for each shortlisted country.
  4. Ask for quotes on the running lines, and get the regulator's own confirmation of any figure marked "not found".
  5. Check each fee against the regulator's current schedule before you pay, because schedules change.

MiCA license cost: FAQs

How much does a MiCA license cost?

Capital of EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class (or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher), plus the regulator's application and annual fees, plus your running costs. Application fees range from none in Portugal to EUR 30,000 and more, such as EUR 30,677.51 for class 3 in Bulgaria and up to EUR 100,000 by the hour in the Netherlands. our fee range for a MiCA CASP licence

How much capital does a crypto license need?

EUR 50,000 for class 1, EUR 125,000 for class 2 and EUR 150,000 for class 3, or one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads if that is higher (MiCA Art 67, Annex IV).

Is MiCA capital a fee I pay to the regulator?

No. It stays in your company as the safeguard. You can hold it as own funds, insurance or both (Art 67(4)).

Which country has the lowest MiCA license cost?

We do not name one. Portugal says no costs attach. Among the published amounts, Slovakia (EUR 1,700 to 3,400), Latvia (EUR 2,500) and Estonia (EUR 3,000) sit low, and the Czech Republic charges CZK 20,000. Croatia prices each service separately, from EUR 900 for custody, plus EUR 1,400 per board member consent. Belgium, Denmark, Greece, Lithuania and Norway publish no application amount, so they cannot be compared, and annual fees can outweigh the application fee. See the cheapest country guide for the full table.

Does a MiCA license cost more than a VASP registration did?

We found no official comparison. The old national registrations were AML registrations without an EU passport, while a CASP authorisation carries capital, governance and custody rules (MiCA Arts 59 to 75).

Are there annual costs after I get the license?

Yes. Regulators charge annual supervision fees. In Luxembourg and Malta the annual amount exceeds the application fee, in France it equals it, and in Liechtenstein it can exceed it. Ireland states no application fee on its CASP page and charges a EUR 30,000 annual levy. See the annual fee table above.

Do I pay again to serve other EU countries?

Estonia's FI charges no processing fee for the passport notification, and we found no passport fee on the other pages. You notify your home regulator (MiCA Art 65), which forwards the notice within 10 working days.
