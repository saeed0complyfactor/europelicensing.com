How Long Does an EMI License Take?

EU law gives the regulator three months to decide on an electronic money institution (EMI) application, but the three months start only when the file is complete. The regulators that publish real figures report much longer: FIN-FSA says six to twelve months on average, and the Central Bank of Ireland's 2025 report gives an average of 763 calendar days.

What is the statutory clock for an EMI license?

PSD2 Article 12 says that within 3 months of receipt of an application, or, if the application is incomplete, of all of the information required for the decision, the authority informs the applicant whether authorisation is granted or refused. It gives reasons for a refusal. EMD2 Article 3(1) applies the payment institution authorisation articles to EMIs, and PSD2 Article 114 reads references to the old payment directive as references to PSD2.

The clock covers only the regulator's decision time. Your own preparation comes on top: the programme of operations, the business plan, the capital evidence, the safeguarding and AML documents and the fit and proper files for managers and shareholders. So do the weeks you take to answer questions.

What stops or restarts the EMI license clock?

The Directive text has one mechanism: an incomplete application moves the start of the 3 months to the date the information arrives. National rules add others, as the country pages record.

  • Incomplete file. Austria, Slovenia, Malta and Luxembourg count the 3 months from when the missing information is in. The Czech Republic counts from the start of proceedings.
  • Pause while the regulator waits. DNB says its three months are suspended while it waits for information. The Central Bank of Ireland says its 90 business day assessment standard pauses while an information request is open.
  • Suspension. The Banca d'Italia says the 90 days can be suspended for at most 180 days.
  • Extension. The MNB can extend once by up to 3 months in justified cases.
  • Outer limit. Belgium, Luxembourg and Portugal set 12 months from submission. Estonia sets 6 months. Liechtenstein rejects a file still incomplete after 12 months, and so does Germany, where BaFin must reject a file still insufficient 12 months after receipt.
  • Withdrawal. The Central Bank of Ireland sends a "Minded to Deem Withdrawn" letter when it receives no adequate response within 50 working days, and gives 10 working days to reply. DNB may stop considering an application within four weeks if you do not supply requested information.

How long does an EMI license take in practice?

Few regulators publish a real duration. The ones we found:

Regulator Published figure Source
FIN-FSA (Finland) The whole process averages 6 to 12 months, depending mostly on application quality FIN-FSA
Central Bank of Ireland Average 763 calendar days from initial application to authorisation for payment and e-money firms, shortest 497 days. Nine applications received and four firms authorised in 2025. It attributes the gap to unprepared applicants, slow replies and business model changes 2025 report, s 3.9
DNB (Netherlands) Total processing tends to be longer than 3 months. No average is stated DNB
Banco de España Processing tends to run long when the programme of operations is vague or the documents are incomplete. No average is stated Banco de España guide 1.3

The Irish figure is an average over applications that finished a long process, and it covers payment and e-money firms together, so read it as a ceiling for an unprepared file. The FIN-FSA range is the regulator's own description of a normal case. We did not find a published average for the other states, and we do not quote consultancy figures.

For the FCA, the figures sit in a PDF attached to the metrics page. We could not read the payment services and e-money row reliably, so we do not quote a median here: FCA median days for payment services and e-money authorisations, from the latest operating service metrics.

What this means for you

Plan in three blocks. First, preparation before filing, which depends on you. Second, the regulator's clock, which runs from a complete file and pauses while it waits for you. Third, the work after the licence, such as opening safeguarding accounts and passport notifications.

  • The part you control is how complete your first filing is. The regulators that explain the gap between 3 months and the real figure point to application quality, slow replies and changes to the business model.
  • Ask the regulator whether it holds a pre-application meeting, and use it to learn what it treats as complete.
  • Do not set a launch date from the statutory clock. Set it from the published averages, and leave room for one round of questions.
  • Capital is due before you apply or at authorisation, depending on the country. The EU minimum is EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4). Check the capital section of the country page you choose.
  • If speed decides your choice of country, compare the rows above and ask the regulator in writing how long a complete file takes today.

Is the clock the same for a MiCA crypto licence?

No. MiCA Article 63 sets 25 working days for the completeness check and 40 working days for the assessment, with a pause of up to 20 working days if the authority asks questions. The Estonia, Norway and Liechtenstein pages record these figures from their regulators and from ESMA. An EMI that only wants custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues can notify under MiCA Art 60(4) at least 40 working days before it starts. A payment institution has no Art 60 route. See the MiCA CASP licence guide.

EMI license timeline FAQs

How long does an EMI license take in the EU?

The legal decision period is 3 months from a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). The whole process takes longer. FIN-FSA says 6 to 12 months on average, and the Central Bank of Ireland's 2025 report gives 763 calendar days on average.

Does the 3-month clock start when I submit?

It starts on receipt if the file is complete. If it is incomplete, the 3 months run from receipt of all the information needed for the decision (PSD2 Art 12). The Czech Republic counts from the start of proceedings.

What happens if the regulator does not decide in time?

It depends on the country. Malta and Spain treat silence as a refusal, and Luxembourg treats it as a deemed refusal after 12 months. Check the country page for the state you choose.

Is there a faster EMI route?

Small EMI tiers exist in some states, with lower capital and no passport, and each has its own limits on the country page. Lithuania gives 2 months for a restricted EMI. The tier fits only a domestic business of limited size.

How long does an FCA EMI authorisation take?

The legal limit is 3 months from a complete application and 12 months from receipt if it is incomplete (EMRs 2011 reg 9). The FCA publishes quartile days each quarter. See the UK EMI page.

Which country has the fastest EMI license?

No regulator publishes a comparable figure for every state, so we cannot rank them. Compare the statutory clocks and published averages above, and ask the regulator how long a complete file takes now.

Does the clock include time to open a bank account?

No. The clock covers the regulator's decision on the application. Safeguarding accounts and banking are separate steps.
