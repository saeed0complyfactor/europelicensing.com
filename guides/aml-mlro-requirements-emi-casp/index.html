AML and MLRO Requirements for EMIs, PIs and CASPs

EMIs, payment institutions (PIs) and crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) must run AML/CFT controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (AMLD), name a compliance officer at management level where their size and business call for it, and, for crypto transfers and fund transfers, send and check information under the travel rule in Regulation (EU) 2023/1113. From 10 July 2027 the AML Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (AMLR) replaces much of the AMLD with directly applicable rules, including a compliance manager on the management body and a compliance officer for every obliged entity.

What AML law applies to an EMI, PI or CASP?

Three instruments apply now or soon.

  1. The AMLD. National laws implement it, so the detail differs by country. It applies to PIs, EMIs and, since the TFR amended it, to CASPs (TFR Article 38 added CASPs to the definition of financial institution). The core duties are:
  2. a documented business-wide risk assessment (Article 8(1) and (2));
  3. policies, controls and procedures approved by senior management (Article 8(3) to (5));
  4. customer due diligence (Article 13(1)) and enhanced due diligence for high-risk third countries and higher-risk cases (Article 18);
  5. suspicious transaction reports to the national financial intelligence unit (FIU), including attempted transactions (Article 33);
  6. five years of record keeping (Article 40(1));
  7. ongoing staff training (Article 46(1)).
  8. The TFR, Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, which has applied since 30 December 2024.
  9. The AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, which applies from 10 July 2027.

EBA guidelines add detail. The ML/TF risk factors guidelines now cover CASPs (EBA/GL/2024/01, from 30 December 2024). The travel rule guidelines (EBA/GL/2024/11) and the guidelines on restrictive measures (EBA/GL/2024/14 and EBA/GL/2024/15) address sanctions screening of transfers.

Does an EMI, PI or CASP need an MLRO?

The AMLD does not use the term MLRO. Article 8(4)(a) requires a compliance officer at management level, where that is appropriate to the size and nature of the business. Article 46(4) requires institutions that have a management body to identify the member who is responsible for implementing AML law.

The EBA Guidelines on the AML/CFT compliance officer (EBA/GL/2022/05) have applied since 1 December 2022 and add the following.

  • If you do not appoint a separate compliance officer, you organise the officer's tasks through a management body member, a senior manager responsible for AML/CFT, outsourced operational functions, or a mix of these (paragraph 34).
  • The independent audit function should not be combined with the AML/CFT compliance function (paragraph 65).
  • Operational functions of the compliance officer may be outsourced only where national law permits it. Even then, responsibility stays with you, the agreement must be in writing, and strategic decisions stay with the management body. Those decisions include approving the business-wide risk assessment, adopting the AML policies and approving the criteria used to detect suspicious transactions. You remain responsible for the decision to report to the FIU (paragraph 68).

Because national law decides whether outsourcing is permitted, ask your home regulator before you name an outsourced MLRO in an application. PSD2 and MiCA add the general rule that outsourcing does not delegate your responsibility (MiCA Article 73(1)(a)).

For a CASP, MiCA Article 68(1) adds that members of the management body must not have been convicted of money laundering or terrorist financing offences. Article 68(8) requires systems and risk assessment procedures for national AML law, evaluated regularly.

What changes when the AMLR applies on 10 July 2027?

The AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 (Article 90) and, being a regulation, applies without national transposition. Article 2(6)(i) lists CASPs as financial institutions, and obliged entities include financial institutions (Article 3).

Topic Today (AMLD) From 10 July 2027 (AMLR)
Senior role Identify the management body member responsible, where there is a management body (Art 46(4)) A compliance manager appointed from the management body in its management function. Responsible for compliance with the AMLR, the TFR and supervisors' acts, and for resources (Art 11(1))
Compliance officer At management level where appropriate (Art 8(4)(a)) Every obliged entity has a compliance officer with sufficiently high hierarchical standing. Responsible for day-to-day AML/CFT controls including targeted financial sanctions, the contact point for regulators and the person who reports suspicious transactions to the FIU (Art 11(2))
Combining roles Not covered in the articles cited here The two roles may be held by one person where the business's nature, risks, complexity and size justify it, and may be combined with other functions (Art 11(7))
Reporting Not covered in the articles cited here Annual report on implementation to the management body, or more often (Art 11(6)). The officer can report directly to the management body (Art 11(5))
Protection Not covered in the articles cited here Measures to protect the officer from retaliation and to keep decisions free of undue commercial influence (Art 11(4))
Crypto accounts Not covered in the articles cited here CASPs may not keep anonymous crypto-asset accounts, or accounts allowing anonymisation or increased obfuscation of transactions, including through anonymity-enhancing coins (Art 79(1))

A group member may appoint as its compliance officer an individual who performs that function in another entity of the group, where the size of the entity and the low risk of its activities justify it (Article 11(2)). Removal of the compliance officer needs prior notice to the management body, and you must tell the supervisor (Article 11(2)).

Commission technical standards and AMLA guidance will add detail beyond what this page covers.

What does the travel rule require of a PI, EMI or CASP?

The TFR has two sets of rules.

For transfers of funds, the payer's PSP sends the payer's name, payment account number, and address including the country, official personal document number and customer identification number (or, instead, the date and place of birth), and the payee's name and account number (Article 4). The payee's PSP must detect missing information and decide whether to execute, reject or suspend a transfer that lacks it (Articles 7 and 8). A PI or EMI follows these rules whenever it is a PSP in the chain.

For crypto-asset transfers, the originator's CASP sends the originator's name, distributed ledger address or crypto-asset account number, and address including the country, official personal document number and customer identification number (or date and place of birth), and the beneficiary's name and distributed ledger address or crypto-asset account number, before or at the same time as the transfer (Article 14(1), (2), (4)). It must verify the originator information and must not initiate or execute the transfer before it fully complies (Article 14(6), (8)). The beneficiary's CASP must run procedures to detect missing information (Article 16(1)) and risk-based procedures to execute, reject, return or suspend a transfer that lacks it (Article 17).

For a transfer above EUR 1,000 to or from a self-hosted address, the CASP must take adequate measures to assess whether the originator or the beneficiary owns or controls that address (Articles 14(5) and 16(2)).

The EBA Travel Rule Guidelines (EBA/GL/2024/11) apply to both PSPs and CASPs from 30 December 2024.

What does AMLA change for supervision?

AMLA, the EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority, is seated in Frankfurt am Main and has applied since 1 July 2025 (AMLA Regulation, Articles 4 and 108). It must start its first selection of directly supervised entities by 1 July 2027 (Article 13(4)). The detail of AMLA's direct supervision is outside this page, so read AMLA's published selection criteria when they appear.

What this means for you

  1. In your application, name the person who will act as MLRO or compliance officer, and put a management body member in charge of AML compliance. Write the role description against EBA/GL/2022/05.
  2. If you plan an outsourced or part-time MLRO, ask your regulator first. Where national law permits it, keep the strategic decisions and the final reporting decision in house.
  3. Write the business-wide risk assessment, CDD and EDD procedures, suspicious transaction reporting process, training plan and sanctions screening before you file. For a CASP, add travel rule procedures and the self-hosted address checks.
  4. Plan the AMLR gap review before 10 July 2027. The compliance manager and compliance officer structure, the annual report to the management body and the CASP account rules start on 10 July 2027.
  5. Keep CDD records and transaction evidence for five years (AMLD Article 40(1)). National law may set longer periods.

AML and MLRO FAQs

Does an EMI need an MLRO?

Under the AMLD, it needs a compliance officer at management level where its size and nature make that appropriate, and an identified management body member responsible for AML compliance (Articles 8(4)(a) and 46(4)). From 10 July 2027, the AMLR requires every obliged entity to have a compliance officer and a compliance manager (Article 11).

Can the MLRO be outsourced?

Only where national law permits it, according to EBA/GL/2022/05 paragraph 68. Responsibility, strategic decisions and the final decision to report to the FIU stay with the institution. Ask your home regulator before you rely on it.

Does a crypto exchange need an MLRO?

Yes. CASPs are financial institutions under the AMLD as amended by the TFR, and MiCA Article 68(8) requires AML systems. Lacking effective ML/TF systems is a mandatory ground for withdrawal of authorisation (MiCA Article 64(1)(f)).

What is the travel rule for crypto?

It requires originator and beneficiary information to accompany each crypto-asset transfer. It has applied since 30 December 2024 (TFR Articles 14 and 40).

When does the new EU AML Regulation apply?

From 10 July 2027 (AMLR Article 90). It is directly applicable, so national transposition is not needed.

What is AMLA?

The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority, seated in Frankfurt am Main. It has applied since 1 July 2025 and must start selecting directly supervised entities by 1 July 2027.
