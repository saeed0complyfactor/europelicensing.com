Passporting an EMI or PI License Across the EEA, Step by Step

An EMI or payment institution passports by sending a notification to its own regulator under PSD2 Article 28. The home regulator has 1 month to forward it, the host regulator has 1 month to respond, and the home regulator communicates its decision within 3 months. A MiCA CASP follows a shorter route under Article 65.

What does passporting mean for an EMI or PI?

Passporting lets an institution authorised in one EEA state serve customers in another without a second licence. The licence stays with the home regulator, which supervises you. The host regulator has a narrower role: it receives the notice, comments on it and can ask for periodic reports on activity in its territory (PSD2 Art 29(2)).

The right works in two ways. You can provide services cross-border, with no local presence. You can also use the right of establishment, through an agent or a branch. PSD2 Article 28(1) covers both when you provide payment services in another Member State for the first time.

EMIs use the same procedure. EMD2 Article 3(1), as amended by PSD2 Article 111, applies PSD2 Article 5, Articles 11 to 17, 19(5) and (6) and 20 to 31 to EMIs with the necessary changes, and Article 28 sits inside that range.

How to passport a payment institution or EMI license, step by step

  1. Check you can passport. A small EMI or small payment institution cannot (EMD2 Art 9(3); PSD2 Art 32(3)). A UK licence cannot either, because UK and EEA passporting ended on 31 December 2020.
  2. Prepare the notification. PSD2 Article 28(1) asks for your name, address and authorisation number if you have one, the Member States where you will operate and the payment services you will provide there.
  3. Add agent or branch details if you will use them. For an agent, you give the Article 19(1) information. For a branch, you give the business plan information from Article 5(1)(b) and (e), the branch's organisational structure and the identity of those who manage it.
  4. Tell your home regulator if you will outsource operational functions to entities in the host state.
  5. The home regulator forwards the complete file to the host regulator within 1 month.
  6. The host regulator has 1 month to assess it and report back. It must flag in particular any reasonable grounds for concern about money laundering or terrorist financing linked to an agent or branch.
  7. If the home regulator disagrees with the host's assessment, it gives the host its reasons. If its own assessment is unfavourable, it refuses to register the agent or branch, or withdraws a registration already made.
  8. The home regulator communicates its decision to the host regulator and to you within 3 months of receiving your information.
  9. On entry in the home register, the agent or branch may start. You tell the home regulator the date you start, and it informs the host.

Article 28(3) names the register entry as the start trigger for agents and branches. It does not name a trigger for plain cross-border services, so ask your home regulator how it handles that case before you fix a launch date.

Any later change goes through the same steps. That includes an extra agent, a new branch or a new outsourcing provider in a host state (Art 28(4)).

Passporting periods: what the law fixes and what it does not

Step Period Who it binds Source
Forward the file to the host 1 month from all information Home regulator PSD2 Art 28(2)
Host assessment 1 month from receipt Host regulator PSD2 Art 28(2)
Decision communicated 3 months from receipt Home regulator PSD2 Art 28(3)
Agent registration (home state) 2 months Home regulator PSD2 Art 19(2)
CASP: inform hosts, ESMA, EBA 10 working days Home regulator MiCA Art 65(2)
CASP: latest start 15th calendar day after you submit Applies to you MiCA Art 65(4)

These periods bind the regulators. Your preparation, and any time the home regulator needs to treat the file as complete, come on top. The directive does not promise a total elapsed time, so we do not quote one.

Passporting through agents and branches

Using an agent in your own home state is a separate registration under PSD2 Article 19. You give the agent's name and address, a description of its AML and counter-terrorist-financing controls, the identity of its managers and, for agents that are not themselves payment service providers, evidence that they are fit and proper, and the services for which it is mandated. The home regulator tells you within 2 months whether the agent is in the register, and the agent can start on entry (Art 19(2)).

Using an agent or a branch in another Member State sends you through Article 28 instead (Art 19(5)). Two host-state rules can follow. The host regulator may require periodic reports on the activity of your agents and branches in its territory (Art 29(2)). A Member State may also require a central contact point from institutions that operate there through agents under the right of establishment (Art 29(4)).

EMIs have a limit: they cannot issue e-money through agents. They can distribute and redeem it through distributors, and they can provide payment services through agents on the Article 19 conditions (EMD2 Art 3(4), (5)). Distribution in another Member State follows PSD2 Articles 27 to 31, except Article 29(4) and (5). Our agents and distributors guide covers this in detail.

National examples: where local rules add steps

Some Member States describe the notice in their own law, and a few set periods that differ from the directive's. These examples come from our country pages.

As the Irish PI example shows, the home regulator's own rules can differ from the directive. Read the national text and the regulator's guidance for the notice form, any fee and the timing it expects, then plan on whichever period is longer.

How a CASP passports under MiCA

MiCA is shorter than PSD2. A crypto-asset service provider that intends to provide services in more than one Member State submits four items to its home regulator (Art 65(1)):

  • the Member States where it will provide services;
  • the services it will provide there on a cross-border basis;
  • the intended starting date;
  • a list of any other activities it carries on that MiCA does not cover.

The home regulator has 10 working days to communicate that information to the single points of contact of the host states, to ESMA and to EBA. It tells you without delay that it has done so. You may begin from the date you receive that communication, or at the latest from the 15th calendar day after you submitted the information (Art 65(4)). Article 65 has no host assessment step, so the start date does not wait on a host decision.

What this means for you

If you hold or plan an EMI or PI licence, build passport notices into your launch plan. Count 3 months for the regulator's decision as the statutory maximum for agents and branches, and add your own file preparation. For a CASP, count on 10 working days plus the 15 calendar day backstop.

Decide your home state with the passport in mind. A restricted or small tier is cheaper to obtain and gives no passport, so it fits a domestic business only. Our small EMI and small PI country table shows which states offer one.

If you need the UK as well, passporting will not get you there. You need a separate FCA authorisation. See FCA authorisation.

Passporting FAQs

What does passporting mean for an EMI?

It means serving customers in another EEA state on your home authorisation. You notify your home regulator under PSD2 Article 28, which EMD2 Article 3(1) applies to EMIs.

How long does it take to passport a payment institution license?

The home regulator has 1 month to forward your complete notification, the host has 1 month to respond and the home regulator communicates its decision within 3 months (PSD2 Art 28(2), (3)).

Can I passport a small EMI or small PI?

No. The small EMI waiver gives no passport (EMD2 Art 9(3)) and neither does the small payment institution waiver (PSD2 Art 32(3)). You apply for full authorisation to passport.

Do I need to passport to use agents in another country?

Yes. PSD2 Article 19(5) sends agents and branches in another Member State through the Article 28 procedure, and the agent can start once it is entered in the register.

How does a CASP passport under MiCA?

File the Article 65(1) information with your home regulator. You can start when it tells you it has informed the hosts, ESMA and EBA, or on the 15th calendar day after you submitted, whichever comes first.

Can an EMI issue e-money through agents in another country?

No. EMD2 Article 3(5) bars issuing e-money through agents. An EMI can use distributors and can provide payment services through agents.

Does a UK licence passport into the EU?

No. UK and EEA passporting ended on 31 December 2020.
