PSD3 and the PSR: What Changes for EMIs and PIs

The Council adopted its first-reading position on PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation (PSR) on 29 October 2026, and Parliament's second-reading vote is indicated for 14 December 2026, so neither text is law yet. If the Council text stands, e-money institutions become payment institutions, the capital figures change, and new fraud and open banking duties apply to every payment service provider.

What are PSD3 and the PSR?

The Commission put forward a payment services package on 28 June 2023 after reviewing PSD2. It has two files:

  • A directive on licensing and supervision, procedure 2023/0209(COD), proposed as COM(2023) 366. It would repeal PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and EMD2 (Directive 2009/110/EC).
  • A regulation on the conduct rules, procedure 2023/0210(COD), proposed as COM(2023) 367. It carries the rules on fraud, open banking and customer rights that PSD2 holds today.

"PSD3" is the working name. The Council's position text is titled as a directive on payment services in the internal market that amends Directive 98/26/EC and Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 and repeals Directives 2009/110/EC and (EU) 2015/2366.

The Observatory shows both files as "Awaiting Parliament 2nd reading". The ECON chair wrote to the Council on 13 May 2026 that she would recommend plenary accept the Council position, subject to legal-linguistic verification. Plenary approval would complete adoption. Publication in the Official Journal follows, and the dates below run from entry into force on the twentieth day after publication.

The Council's voting results and the Council position texts give 29 October 2026 as the adoption date. The Commission's communication on the PSR, COM(2026) 609, gives 28 October 2026, the day Coreper approved the texts. We use the Council's own record. The Observatory lists the matching PSD3 communication, COM(2026) 610, on 3 November 2026, and we did not open it.

When would the rules apply?

Both Council texts count from entry into force. The directive gives Member States 21 months to adopt and apply their national laws (Art 49). The PSR applies 21 months after entry into force, except Arts 51 and 58, which apply after 27 months. As an illustration only: publication on 15 January 2027 would give entry into force on 4 February 2027 and a 21-month date of 4 November 2028. No publication date exists yet.

The Commission proposal had said 18 months for transposition and application, so the Council text adds three months.

The Council text adds two rules. Recital 20 explains that e-money services are now limited to issuance and classed as a distinct payment service, so the old EUR 350,000 level "would appear disproportionate". And where a firm provides services under more than one of points (b), (c) and (d), the minimum amounts "shall be added together". On our reading of that cumulation rule, an issuer of e-money that also provides services 1 to 4 would need EUR 150,000 plus EUR 250,000, so EUR 400,000. That total is our interpretation, not a figure in the text, so check it against the final published text.

Own funds keep Methods A, B and C for payment services. For e-money the Council text has a separate calculation (Art 8), and the Commission proposal noted that one method should become the default option. Account information providers and payment initiation providers can hold initial capital or own funds instead of professional indemnity insurance, which the Commission proposal also introduced because insurance at authorisation was hard to obtain. Details for the existing rules are in the EMI capital requirements guide.

What changes in licensing for EMIs and PIs?

Commission proposal and Council position agree on the main points.

  • One regime. E-money institutions become a sub-category of payment institutions, and EMD2 is repealed (explanatory memorandum, COM(2023) 366). The Council text states that the PI regime "will replace" the EMI regime.
  • E-money tokens. The PI authorisation regime also applies to issuers of e-money tokens that are not credit institutions, and references in MiCA to EMIs issuing e-money tokens are read as references to PIs (recital 6). Article 47 of the Council text would insert MiCA Art 60(4a), under which a PI could provide crypto-asset services for e-money tokens where they are equivalent to its authorised payment services, after notifying at least 40 working days ahead. That is a Council position, not law. Today a PI has no Art 60 route, and an EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of its own tokens (MiCA Art 60(4)).
  • Winding-up plan. The application must include a plan for orderly winding-up in case of failure for firms providing services 1 to 5 or e-money (Council Art 3(3)(r); first proposed in COM(2023) 366).
  • Safeguarding. The Commission proposal added the option of safeguarding at a central bank, at the central bank's discretion, and a duty to avoid concentration risk. The Council text keeps both in its recitals, with EBA standards to follow.
  • Prior applications. The Council text asks for the list of Member States where the applicant or its group has applied or plans to apply, and for any decision on applications in the past three years, with the main reasons for refusal (Art 3(3)(q)).
  • Small firms. The Council text lets Member States exempt PIs under EUR 3 million of monthly payment volume and e-money issuers under EUR 5,000,000 of average outstanding e-money, with no convictions among managers.

What happens to existing licences?

The two texts differ, so label them when you plan.

Commission proposal (COM(2023) 366): licences are grandfathered until 30 months after entry into force, on condition that the firm applies for the new authorisation within 24 months.

Council position (Arts 43 and 44):

  • A PI authorised under PSD2 by 21 months after entry into force may keep providing its services without a new authorisation until 27 months after entry into force.
  • An EMI that has taken up activities under national EMD2 law before the 21-month date may keep operating, in its home state or elsewhere, until 27 months.
  • In both cases the firm submits the information its regulator needs to assess it against listed Art 3(3) items, among them initial capital (point c) and safeguarding (point d). A firm that complies is deemed authorised as a PI and entered in the register. One that does not is suspended from providing services until it supplies verified information.
  • Member States must provide for automatic authorisation where the regulator has evidence that a firm already complies (Arts 43(2) and 44(3)).
  • A regulator that cannot process a file in time may extend the cut-off by up to three months (Art 45).
  • Small EMIs that used the EMD2 Art 9 waiver may continue under the national rules transposing it until the 27-month date.

What does the PSR change?

These points come from the Council position, which reflects the agreement of 26 November 2025. The Commission proposal of 2023 set out the same direction on several of them.

  • Payee verification. Payment service providers must run a check that matches the payee's name to the account identifier before a credit transfer is executed. Art 51 applies Art 5c of Regulation (EU) No 260/2012 to all credit transfers, and applies after 27 months. The 2023 proposal had a similar check for transfers outside the instant payments rules.
  • Fraud tools and monitoring. PSPs must offer spending limits and delay periods, monitor transactions for fraud risk before execution and suspend a transaction where fraud is suspected. They must share data on suspected fraud with each other.
  • Spoofing. Providers of interpersonal communication services must have measures against spoofing fraud. Very large online platforms and search engines must make sure financial service advertisements are placed only for authorised or registered providers. Consumers get a refund right, under conditions, where a fraudster impersonates their PSP.
  • Open banking. Banks may not put artificial technical obstacles or discriminatory delays in front of authorised third-party providers. The 2023 proposal required a dedicated interface, removed the permanent fallback interface except where authorised, and required a permissions dashboard for customers.
  • Device access. Manufacturers of mobile devices and providers of electronic communications services must give payment service providers and technical service providers access to the technical features needed for payments on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms.
  • Bank accounts. The 2023 proposal reinforced PI access to accounts held with credit institutions, including for licence applicants, and required written, detailed reasons for refusing or withdrawing access.

What this means for you

  • Holding an EMI licence: you do not need to apply now. Plan for a re-assessment file within the transition window, and for the capital change, which falls from EUR 350,000 to EUR 250,000 in the Council text.
  • Holding a PI licence: capital rises to EUR 150,000 for services 1 to 5 and EUR 40,000 for remittance only in the Council text, and your file is assessed against the new Art 3(3) items.
  • Open banking firms: the PII-or-capital option and the interface rules help, and the payee check and fraud duties apply to the banks you connect to.
  • Applying now: you apply under PSD2 and EMD2 and transition later. A PI licence and an EMI licence stay separate until the directive is applied. See EMI vs payment institution.
  • Budget for fraud tooling: the monitoring, payee check and data sharing duties touch every PSP.

PSD3 and PSR FAQs

Is PSD3 in force?

No. The Council adopted its first-reading position on 29 October 2026. Parliament's second reading is pending, with an indicative plenary date of 14 December 2026 (Legislative Observatory).

Does PSD3 abolish the EMI licence?

On both the Commission proposal and the Council position, yes in time. EMD2 is repealed, and e-money issuance becomes a payment service under a payment institution authorisation. Existing EMIs get a transition.

What is the new capital requirement for e-money?

EUR 250,000 in the Council position (Art 5(d)). The Commission proposed EUR 400,000, and EMD2 requires EUR 350,000 today (Art 4).

When will PSD3 apply?

Directive: 21 months after entry into force in the Council text (Art 49). PSR: 21 months, with Arts 51 and 58 at 27 months. Entry into force is the twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal.

What does the PSR change for open banking?

Banks may not obstruct authorised providers, and mobile device makers must give access to technical features on fair terms (Council position). The Commission proposal added a dedicated interface and a permissions dashboard.

Will payment institutions get a MiCA route?

Only if the Council text becomes law. It would insert MiCA Art 60(4a) so a PI could provide crypto-asset services for e-money tokens where equivalent to its authorised payment services. Today MiCA Art 60 lists no payment institution.
