EMI License in Estonia

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence in Estonia lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from an Estonian company. Finantsinspektsioon (FI), the Estonian financial supervision authority, grants it under the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act. Initial capital under EU law is EUR 350,000, and FI decides within three months of a complete file.

Who needs an EMI license in Estonia?

You need an FI licence to issue e-money in your own name from Estonia. FI defines e-money as an electronic payment instrument, such as a card or computer memory, storing monetary units. The stored sum cannot exceed what the customer paid, and one e-money device can store up to EUR 1,000.

A payment service that moves money for one transaction, with no stored balance, may fit a payment institution licence instead. Other routes:

  • Agent or branch of another EEA EMI. Home-country supervisors oversee EEA branches in Estonia. An EMI authorised elsewhere can serve Estonia by notification.
  • E-money tokens. An issuer of e-money tokens must be a credit institution or an EMI (MiCA Art 48). See the Estonia crypto page.
  • Small EMI. EMD2 Art 9 allows a waiver below EUR 5,000,000 average outstanding e-money, with no passport. FI's pages do not describe a small EMI tier, so confirm with FI before planning one.

EMI license requirements in Estonia

Capital and own funds. EMD2 Art 4 sets initial capital at EUR 350,000. Own funds must be the higher of that amount and a calculated amount, using method D of at least 2% of average outstanding e-money for e-money activity (EMD2 Art 5). FI asks for documents that certify the paid-in capital, and for an existing company, own funds documents with an auditor's report.

Management and fit and proper. FI's document list asks for managers' names, ID codes, residence, education, work history and responsibilities, with documents on their trustworthiness. It also asks for the list of shareholders with holdings and voting weights, and for information on companies where the applicant or its managers hold more than 20%.

Substance. PSD2 Art 11(3) puts the head office in the same member state as the registered office. FI's pages do not set a number of Estonian-resident board members or an office size, so ask FI.

AML. The document list includes your anti-money laundering and terrorist financing controls and payer information obligations.

Safeguarding. The list asks how you apply client asset and client protection requirements. EMD2 Art 7 sets the rules for e-money funds.

ICT and DORA. FI asks for the IT, technological, security and control systems needed for the services. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly.

Organisation. You describe agents, branches and outsourcing, and any participation in payment systems. Internal rules and bookkeeping rules go in the file, as do the auditor and internal auditor.

How to get an EMI license in Estonia: process

  1. Open an account on the FI portal. From 18 March 2026, FI takes applications for payment and e-money institution licences only through its application portal (taotlus.fi.ee).
  2. Prepare the 18-item file. FI's list, identical for PIs and EMIs, covers: statutes or a notarised foundation decision, proof of capital, an operating plan, a business plan, opening balance or financial statements and three years of annual reports where they exist, client protection arrangements, internal and bookkeeping rules, IT and security systems, AML controls, organisation chart, shareholder list, qualifying holder data, manager data, holdings above 20%, auditors, own funds documents for an existing company, and Eesti Pank-approved rules if you will manage a payment system.
  3. Language. FI's licence page does not state one. For the crypto licence it requires Estonian and allows English documents when the application says so. Confirm the position with FI for e-money.
  4. Submit. FI may request more information.
  5. Decision. FI decides within three months after it has all documents and information, and no later than six months after the application.
  6. Start. The licence has no expiry date and cannot be transferred.

The statutory periods cover FI's decision. Preparing the file and answering questions come on top of them.

After you get the EMI license

  • Register. FI lists you as a supervised entity.
  • Supervision. FI supervises you under your licence, and home-country supervisors oversee EEA branches in Estonia.
  • Reporting. FI publishes a payment services market reporting page. Check it for the returns that apply to you.
  • Passport. Notify FI before you serve another EEA state (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Change of control. PSD2 Art 6 requires prior notification of changes in qualifying holdings. Report management changes to FI too, since it holds your managers' fit and proper data.
  • E-money tokens. If you handle e-money tokens, you need MiCA authorisation too.

EMI in Estonia compared with other countries

For a different regulator, see Lithuania and Ireland, or Poland, where e-money has a separate EUR 350,000 capital rule and a EUR 125,000 option inside a payment institution. In Estonia itself, the payment institution licence carries lower capital, and the crypto page covers MiCA. For the EU rules, read the EMI licence guide.

EMI in Estonia FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Estonia?

EUR 350,000 under EMD2 Art 4. FI asks you to document the capital paid in or to be paid in.

How long does FI take to decide on an EMI licence?

Within three months after FI has all the documents and information, and no later than six months after it receives the application.

Who grants an EMI licence in Estonia?

Finantsinspektsioon. Applications go through its portal at taotlus.fi.ee from 18 March 2026.

How much does an EMI application cost in Estonia?

FI's licence page for payment and e-money institutions lists no fee. FI's crypto page names a EUR 3,000 processing fee for CASP, ART issuer and e-money institution applications. Ask FI which applies.

Can I get a small EMI licence in Estonia?

FI's pages do not describe one. EMD2 Art 9 lets member states waive authorisation below EUR 5,000,000 average outstanding e-money. Confirm with FI.

Can an Estonian EMI serve other EU countries?

Yes, by notifying FI under PSD2 Art 28. No small-EMI passport exists.

Do I need an office in Estonia?

The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). FI's pages do not set a minimum office or board residence.

Does an EMI licence cover crypto services?

No. Under MiCA Art 60 an EMI may notify to provide custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues. Wider services need CASP authorisation. See the MiCA guide.
