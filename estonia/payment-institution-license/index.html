Payment Institution License in Estonia

A payment institution licence in Estonia lets an Estonian company provide payment services such as transfers, card acquiring or money remittance. Finantsinspektsioon (FI), the Estonian financial supervision authority, grants it under the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act. Capital under EU law is EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the service, and FI decides within three months of a complete file.

Who needs a payment institution license in Estonia?

FI defines a payment service provider as a company whose main business is payment services, and it needs an FI licence. PSD2 Annex I lists eight services, among them transfers, card payments, acquiring, money remittance, payment initiation and account information.

Other routes can fit better:

  • E-money. A product that holds a balance for later spending is e-money and needs an EMI licence.
  • Account information only. PSD2 Art 33 lets member states register account information providers without full authorisation. FI's pages do not describe the Estonian rule, so confirm with FI.
  • Small payment institution. PSD2 Art 32 lets member states waive authorisation below EUR 3,000,000 average monthly transactions, with no passport. FI's pages do not describe a small payment institution tier, so do not plan around one before FI confirms it.
  • Agent or branch of another EEA firm. Home-country supervisors oversee EEA payment institutions' branches in Estonia, so an institution authorised elsewhere can serve Estonia by notification.
  • Crypto. Payment licences do not cover crypto-asset services. See the Estonia crypto page.

Payment institution license requirements in Estonia

Capital. PSD2 Art 7 sets initial capital at EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money remittance only. FI asks for a document certifying the paid-in capital or the existence of initial capital, and for an existing company, own funds documents with an auditor's report.

Own funds. Own funds must stay above the higher of initial capital and the PSD2 Art 9 amount, using method A, B or C as the regulator decides. FI's page does not set the method.

Management and fit and proper. FI asks for managers' names, ID codes, residence, education, work history and responsibilities, with documents showing trustworthiness. It asks for the shareholder list with holdings and voting weights and for data on companies where the applicant or its managers hold more than 20%.

Substance. The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). FI's pages do not set an Estonian board or office minimum, so ask FI.

AML. The 18-item list includes AML and terrorist financing controls and payer information obligations.

Safeguarding. For services 1 to 6 you safeguard users' funds by segregation or insurance (PSD2 Art 10). FI asks how you apply client asset and client protection requirements.

Insurance. Payment initiation and account information providers need professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee (PSD2 Art 5(2) and (3)).

ICT and DORA. FI asks for IT, technological, security and control systems. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly.

How to get a payment institution license in Estonia: process

  1. Use the FI portal. From 18 March 2026, FI takes licence applications only through its application portal (taotlus.fi.ee).
  2. Prepare the 18-item file. FI's list covers statutes or a notarised foundation decision, proof of capital, an operating plan describing the services, a business plan, financial statements and three years of annual reports where they exist, client protection arrangements, internal and bookkeeping rules, IT and security systems, AML controls, organisation including agents, branches and outsourcing, the shareholder list and qualifying holder data, manager data, holdings above 20%, auditors, own funds documents for an existing company, and Eesti Pank-approved rules if you will manage a payment system.
  3. Language. FI's licence page does not state one. For the crypto licence it requires Estonian and allows English documents when the application says so. Confirm the position for payments.
  4. Submit. FI may ask for further information.
  5. Decision. FI decides within three months after it has all documents and information, and no later than six months after the application.
  6. Start. FI lists you as a supervised entity once the licence is granted. Check the licence terms with FI before you launch.

The statutory periods cover FI's decision. Preparing the file and answering questions come on top of them.

After you get the payment institution license

  • Register. FI lists you in its supervised entities register.
  • Supervision. FI supervises payment institutions it has licensed.
  • Reporting. FI publishes a payment services market reporting page. Check it for the returns that apply to you.
  • Passport. Notify FI before you provide services in another EEA state, through a branch, an agent or cross-border (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Change of control. PSD2 Art 6 requires prior notification of changes in qualifying holdings.
  • Changes. Tell FI when managers, shareholders or services change, since FI holds that data on file.

Payment institution in Estonia compared with other countries

For a different regulator, see Lithuania and Poland, where small payment institutions register for domestic activity with a EUR 1,500,000 monthly limit. In Estonia itself, an EMI licence covers e-money, and the crypto page covers MiCA. For the EU rules, read the payment institution guide.

Payment institution in Estonia FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Estonia?

EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money remittance only (PSD2 Art 7). FI asks you to document the capital paid in or to be paid in.

How long does FI take to decide?

Within three months after FI has all documents and information, and no later than six months after the application.

Who grants a payment institution licence in Estonia?

Finantsinspektsioon, through its portal at taotlus.fi.ee from 18 March 2026.

How much does a payment institution application cost in Estonia?

FI's licence page lists no fee. Its crypto page names EUR 3,000 for CASP, ART issuer and e-money institution applications. Ask FI whether anything applies to a PI application.

Is there a small payment institution in Estonia?

FI's pages do not describe one. PSD2 Art 32 allows member states to waive authorisation below EUR 3,000,000 average monthly transactions. Confirm with FI.

Can an Estonian payment institution serve other EU countries?

Yes, by notifying FI under PSD2 Art 28.

Do I need an office in Estonia?

The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). FI's pages do not set a minimum office or board residence.

Which language do I file in?

FI's licence page does not state it. Its crypto page says Estonian, with English documents allowed if the application says so. Confirm for payments.
