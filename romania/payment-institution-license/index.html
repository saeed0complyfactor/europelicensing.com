Payment Institution License in Romania

A Romanian payment institution (PI) licence lets you provide payment services from Romania and passport them across the EU and EEA. The National Bank of Romania (BNR) grants it under Law 209/2019, which transposes PSD2. You need a Romanian company with its real seat in Romania, and BNR approves each manager before they start.

Who needs a payment institution licence in Romania?

Law 209/2019 bans anyone who is not a payment service provider from providing the services in Art 7 as a business (Art 8). You need BNR authorisation if you provide those services in Romania and you are not a bank, an EMI or a provider from another EEA state using its passport. The services are money remittance, payment initiation, account information, card issuing and acquiring and the others listed in Art 7(1).

Check these alternatives first:

  • You only read account data. Law 209/2019 covers specialised account information providers as a separate category (Art 1 and 7(1)(h)). They hold no initial capital (PSD2 Art 33). Ask BNR about the registration route.
  • You hold customer balances as stored value. That is e-money, and you need an EMI licence in Romania.
  • You hold a licence in another EEA state. Notify your home regulator and use the passport.
  • You want crypto services next to payments. See our MiCA page for Romania. The competent authority was not designated at our last check.
  • You hope for a small-institution waiver. We found none in the text of Law 209/2019 we read.

Requirements for a payment institution licence in Romania

Capital and own funds

The initial capital at authorisation cannot be less than the lei equivalent of EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only and EUR 125,000 for services a) to e) of Art 7(1). If you provide services in different tiers, the highest tier applies (Art 18). Account information providers hold no initial capital (PSD2 Art 33). Own funds then follow Art 34 and the calculation methods in Art 36 and 37.

Legal form and substance

BNR can authorise only a Romanian legal person incorporated under Companies Law 31/1990, with its real seat in Romania, that carries out at least part of its payment services there (Art 9(2)). If you have other business activities, BNR can require a separate entity for payments when those activities harm your finances or its supervision (Art 12).

Management and shareholders

Each person responsible for running the payment business needs good repute, and knowledge and experience that fit the activity, and BNR approves each of them before they start (Art 13). BNR also checks the quality of shareholders with qualifying holdings (Art 14) and close links (Art 15).

Governance and controls

BNR grants authorisation only if you have a clear organisational structure, effective risk procedures and adequate internal control, including accounting procedures (Art 11). Your security policy must protect users adequately, or BNR can refuse (Art 27(1)(j)).

Safeguarding and insurance

Firms providing services a) to f) must protect users' funds (Art 38). Firms providing payment initiation need professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee, and firms providing account information need it for the damage from unauthorised access to account data (Art 19).

ICT and AML

Add your DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) documentation and AML/CFT controls. BNR may consult the national AML office and the consumer protection authority on parts of the file (Art 10). See our AML and compliance programme page.

How to apply for a payment institution licence in Romania

  1. Incorporate the Romanian company under Law 31/1990 and set up its real seat.
  2. Build the file. BNR sets the documents by regulation and follows the EBA authorisation guidelines (Art 17; EBA/GL/2017/09). The BNR regulations are in Romanian.
  3. File the application with BNR (Art 25).
  4. Completeness. If the file falls short, BNR tells you what is missing. You have up to 3 months to supply it, and BNR can extend that on a reasoned request (Art 26(2) to (4)).
  5. Questions. BNR can ask for more information within 20 working days of receiving a complete application. You answer within 30 working days, and the review clock is suspended meanwhile (Art 26(5)).
  6. Decision. BNR decides within 3 months of a complete application (Art 26(1)). A refusal comes with reasons (Art 27(2)). BNR must refuse in listed cases, for example capital below the minimum or an incomplete file (Art 27).
  7. Register entry. The decision takes effect from the date of entry in BNR's register (Art 26(8)). If you do not start the licensed activity within 12 months, BNR can withdraw the licence (Art 28(1)(a)).

Documents sent after the deadlines are not considered and are returned to you (Art 26(7)). You may add documents on your own initiative until 30 days before BNR's decision deadline (Art 26(6)).

After authorisation

  • Register: BNR keeps the register of authorised institutions, agents and branches. We could not open the register, so we give no count.
  • Reporting: a semi-annual report by 16 August and annual financial statements within 130 days of year end, in Romanian and in lei (BNR reporting page).
  • Qualifying holdings: prior notice for crossings of 20%, 30% and 50% (Art 30). BNR has up to 60 working days to assess.
  • Changes: notify changes to your situation as BNR's regulations require (Art 29).
  • Agents: you can use an agent only after BNR registers it (Art 49).
  • Passport: authorisation lets you provide the services in any member state (Art 16). You apply to BNR, which forwards the details to the host authority within 1 month (Art 61(2); PSD2 Art 28).
  • Withdrawal: BNR can withdraw the licence if you do not start within 12 months or stop for more than 6 months (Art 28).

Romania vs other countries

Romania asks for a Romanian company, BNR approval of each manager and a 3-month clock that pauses for missing documents. Compare the payment institution licence in Cyprus, the payment institution licence in Lithuania and the one in Malta. In Romania, the EMI licence needs EUR 350,000, and the MiCA CASP licence depends on an authority that was not designated at our last check.

Payment institution in Romania FAQs

How much capital do you need for a payment institution licence in Romania?

The lei equivalent of EUR 125,000 for services a) to e) of Art 7(1), EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only and EUR 20,000 for money remittance only (Law 209/2019 Art 18). If you mix tiers, the highest applies. Account information providers hold none.

Who issues payment institution licences in Romania?

The National Bank of Romania (BNR), under Law 209/2019.

How long does a payment institution licence take in Romania?

BNR decides within 3 months of a complete application (Art 26(1)). If the file is incomplete, you get up to 3 months to supply what is missing, and BNR's questions can suspend the clock for up to 30 working days. File preparation comes first, and we do not quote a timeline.

How much does BNR charge?

We found no fee figure in the sources we read. BNR's fee schedule sets any fee, so ask BNR.

What legal form do you need?

A Romanian legal person incorporated under Law 31/1990, with its real seat in Romania, that carries out at least part of its services there (Art 9(2)).

Does BNR approve my managers?

Yes. Each person who runs the payment business needs BNR approval before they start (Art 13(3)).

Can I file in English?

The BNR pages we read do not say. Its reporting forms are Romanian-only, so plan for Romanian-language filings and ask BNR what it accepts in English.

Is there a small payment institution tier in Romania?

We found none in the text of Law 209/2019 we read. PSD2 Art 32 leaves the choice to each state, so ask BNR.
