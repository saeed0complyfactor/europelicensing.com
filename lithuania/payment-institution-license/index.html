Payment Institution License in Lithuania

A payment institution (PI) licence from the Bank of Lithuania lets you provide PSD2 payment services such as accounts, transfers, card acquiring, remittance and payment initiation, and passport them across the EEA. It is for firms that move customer money without issuing e-money. Besides the full licence, Lithuania offers a restricted-activity licence and an account-information-only licence. Each has its own state fee, starting at EUR 682.

Who needs a payment institution license in Lithuania?

You need authorisation before you provide a PSD2 Annex I payment service in or from Lithuania, unless you already hold a bank, EMI or other PSD2 licence (PSD2 Art 11(1)). The services are cash placement and withdrawal, execution of transfers, direct debits and card payments, issuing or acquiring payment instruments, money remittance, payment initiation and account information.

Pick the route that matches your business:

  • A full licence passports into the EEA and carries the capital tier for your services.
  • A restricted-activity licence has no minimum initial capital and is valid only in Lithuania. PSD2 Art 32(1)(a) lets a member state cap the monthly average of the last 12 months' payment transactions at no more than EUR 3 million, and the Bank's page has listed EUR 3 million. The Bank's page also gives a 2-month decision period. Confirm both with the Bank.
  • An account-information-only firm applies for the account information route, needs professional indemnity insurance and no initial capital (PSD2 Art 33; Art 5(3)).
  • An agent of an authorised PI in another EEA state needs no Lithuanian licence.
  • If customers keep a balance with you for later spending, that is e-money and you need an EMI licence in Lithuania.

Own funds must stay at or above the higher of the initial capital and the Method A, B or C amount the Bank selects (PSD2 Art 8(1), Art 9).

Management and fit and proper

Directors need good repute and the knowledge and experience for the role. Qualifying shareholders must be suitable (PSD2 Art 5(1)(m), (n); Art 11(6)). A restricted licence is exempt from the shareholder test.

Substance

Your head office sits in the same state as your registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). We could not retrieve the Bank's written rule on local management or staff, so raise it at the pre-application meeting.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Appoint a named AML compliance officer. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

For services 1 to 6 you segregate customer funds in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or cover them by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886).

ICT and DORA

The file covers ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, incident handling, business continuity and a security policy (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e), (f), (h), (j)). Payment initiation and account information providers also hold professional indemnity insurance (Art 5(2), (3)).

How to get a payment institution license in Lithuania

  1. Map your services to PSD2 Annex I to find your capital tier and licence route.
  2. Contact the Bank of Lithuania before applying and ask for a pre-application discussion.
  3. Set up a Lithuanian legal entity and pay in any required capital.
  4. Prepare the file from the Bank's payment institution authorisation page, which links its checklists.
  5. Pay the state fee to the State Tax Inspectorate before you apply.
  6. Submit in Lithuanian or English. The Bank may ask for Lithuanian translations.
  7. The Bank decides within 3 months of a complete file (2 months for a restricted licence). A request for more information restarts the count from receipt of the documents.

After you get the payment institution license

  • You appear in the Bank of Lithuania's register of financial market participants.
  • You report to the Bank, keep own funds at the required level and safeguard customer funds.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee.
  • To serve another EEA state, a full PI notifies the Bank, which forwards it within 1 month. The host assesses within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1), (2)). Agents and branches take up to 3 months (Art 28(3)).
  • A restricted PI that exceeds its conditions has 30 calendar days to apply for full authorisation (PSD2 Art 32(5)).
  • Acquiring a qualifying holding in a PI needs the regulator's prior approval.

Lithuania payment institution license vs other countries

In Lithuania one regulator handles the full licence, a restricted tier and an account-information-only route. Compare the payment institution licence in Ireland, Malta and Cyprus. In Lithuania, the sibling pages are the EMI licence and the MiCA CASP licence. The EU rules sit in our payment institution licence in the EU guide.

Payment institution license in Lithuania FAQs

What is a payment institution in Lithuania?

A firm authorised by the Bank of Lithuania to provide PSD2 payment services. It may not issue e-money.

How much capital does a payment institution need in Lithuania?

EUR 20,000 for remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5 (PSD2 Art 7). A restricted-activity licence and account-information-only registration have none.

How much does a payment institution licence cost in Lithuania?

The Bank of Lithuania state fee is EUR 898 for a standard licence, EUR 682 for a restricted one and EUR 693 for account information only. Capital and running costs come on top.

How long does it take to get a payment institution licence in Lithuania?

The Bank decides within 3 months of a complete file, or 2 months for a restricted licence (PSD2 Art 12; the restricted period comes from the Bank's page). Preparation time is extra, and we do not quote a real-world average.

What is a restricted payment institution licence in Lithuania?

A licence for smaller firms with no minimum capital and no shareholder suitability test, as the Bank's page describes it. It is valid only in Lithuania, and average monthly payment transactions must stay under the cap, which PSD2 limits to EUR 3 million at most. Confirm the current conditions with the Bank.

Can I passport a Lithuanian payment institution licence?

A full licence can be passported by notifying the Bank (PSD2 Art 28). A restricted licence cannot (PSD2 Art 32(3)).

Do I need an EMI or a payment institution licence?

Choose an EMI if customers hold a stored balance you issue as e-money. Choose a payment institution if you only execute payments or provide initiation or account information.

Can I file the application in English?

Yes. The Bank accepts Lithuanian or English and may ask for Lithuanian translations.
