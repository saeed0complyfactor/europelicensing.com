EMI License in Lithuania

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence from the Bank of Lithuania lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Lithuania, then passport them across the EEA. It is for fintechs, wallets, prepaid and card programmes and neobanks that hold customer balances. A full EMI needs EUR 350,000 of capital. A restricted EMI needs no minimum capital, but its licence works in Lithuania only.

Who needs an EMI license in Lithuania?

You need it if you issue electronic money from Lithuania: a stored balance that customers hold with you and spend with third parties (EMD2 Art 2(2); Art 10). Prepaid cards, wallets and accounts that hold a spendable balance usually fall in scope. An EMI can also provide the payment services in PSD2 Annex I (EMD2 Art 6(1)).

Another route can fit better:

  • If you only move money and never hold a balance for later spending, a payment institution licence in Lithuania needs less capital.
  • If your business is small and local, the Bank of Lithuania offers a restricted-activity EMI licence. It has no minimum capital and skips the shareholder suitability test, but it is valid only in Lithuania. The restricted licence carries a cap on average outstanding e-money and a monthly cap on payment transactions unrelated to e-money. The Bank's page has listed these as EUR 900,000 and EUR 3,000,000, but we could not confirm the current figures, so check both caps with the Bank before you design around them.
  • If you already hold an EMI licence in another EEA state, you passport into Lithuania by notification and need no Lithuanian licence.
  • If you also want to custody or transfer your own e-money tokens, an EMI can notify under MiCA Art 60(4). Wider crypto services need a Lithuanian CASP authorisation.

EMI license requirements in Lithuania

Capital and own funds

A full EMI needs EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMD2 Art 4). Afterwards own funds must stay at or above the higher of that figure and the calculated amount, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (Art 5(1) and (3)). The regulator may require up to 20% more or allow up to 20% less (Art 5(5)).

Management and fit and proper

The Bank expects the heads of the institution to be of good repute with the qualifications and experience for their duties. Qualifying shareholders are tested for suitability for a full EMI (PSD2 Art 5(1)(m), (n); Art 11(6), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). A restricted EMI is exempt from the shareholder test.

Substance

The head office must sit in the same Member State as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). We could not retrieve the Bank of Lithuania's written rule on local directors, staff or office, so ask the Bank at the pre-application meeting.

AML officer and controls

The file must describe internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money (EMD2 Art 7(1)) either by segregating them in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). Card-funded money must be safeguarded within five business days of issuance (EMD2 Art 7(1)).

ICT and DORA

The file describes ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, incident handling, business continuity and a security policy (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e), (f), (h), (j)).

How to get an EMI license in Lithuania

  1. Contact the Bank of Lithuania early and ask for a pre-application meeting before filing.
  2. Set up a Lithuanian legal entity and pay in the capital.
  3. Prepare the file from the Bank's EMI authorisation page, which links its document checklists and forms.
  4. Pay the state fee to the State Tax Inspectorate before you apply.
  5. Submit in Lithuanian or English. Where you file in English, the Bank may ask for Lithuanian translations.
  6. If documents are missing, the Bank may return the application unexamined within 5 business days of receipt (Board Resolution No 58, para 14). It decides within 3 months of receiving a complete file (2 months for a restricted EMI). If it asks for more information, the period runs from receipt of those documents.
  7. The Board of the Bank of Lithuania, or a unit acting on its instruction, grants or refuses the licence (Board Resolution No 58, para 21).

These are statutory limits. Time spent preparing the file and answering questions comes on top, and we give you a dated plan after scoping.

After you get the EMI license

  • The Bank enters you in its register of financial market participants.
  • You report to the Bank under its prudential and payment-services rules and keep own funds above the required level.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee.
  • To serve another EEA state, you notify the Bank, which forwards the notice within 1 month. The host state assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1) and (2)). You may use distributors abroad but not agents to issue e-money (EMD2 Art 3(4) and (5)).
  • Material changes to safeguarding need advance notice (EMD2 Art 3(2)). A change of qualifying holding is a change of control and needs the regulator's consent before it completes.

Lithuania EMI license vs other countries

Lithuania has a restricted EMI tier, and its statutory decision period is 3 months. To compare, see the EMI licence in Ireland, the EMI licence in Malta and the EMI licence in Cyprus. Within Lithuania, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence. For the EU rules behind all of them, read our EMI licence in Europe guide.

EMI license in Lithuania FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Lithuania?

EUR 350,000 for a full EMI (EMD2 Art 4). A restricted-activity EMI has no minimum initial capital but is valid only in Lithuania.

How much does an EMI licence cost in Lithuania?

The Bank of Lithuania state fee is EUR 1,463 for a full EMI and EUR 1,235 for a restricted one. Add EUR 350,000 of capital and the running costs of a regulated firm: local people, audit, safeguarding, ICT and advisers. We do not give amounts for those running costs.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Lithuania?

The Bank decides within 3 months of a complete file, or 2 months for a restricted EMI. Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

What is the difference between a full and a restricted EMI in Lithuania?

A restricted EMI has no minimum capital, a shorter decision period and no shareholder suitability test. It is valid only in Lithuania and carries volume caps. A full EMI is capitalised at EUR 350,000 and can passport.

Do I need an office or staff in Lithuania?

You need a Lithuanian legal entity, and your head office must be in the state of your registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). We could not confirm the Bank's written expectations on local staff, so raise it at the pre-application meeting.

Can I file in English?

Yes. The Bank accepts applications in Lithuanian or English, and may ask for Lithuanian translations of English documents.

Can I passport a Lithuanian EMI licence?

A full EMI can, by notifying the Bank of Lithuania (PSD2 Art 28). A restricted EMI cannot (EMD2 Art 9(3)).

Can I buy a Lithuanian EMI instead of applying?

Yes, but the buyer needs the regulator's consent to acquire a qualifying holding. We do not list firms for sale.
