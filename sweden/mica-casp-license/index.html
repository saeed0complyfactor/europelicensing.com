Crypto License in Sweden

A crypto licence in Sweden is an authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) under MiCA, granted by Finansinspektionen (FI), the Swedish Financial Supervisory Authority. It is for exchanges, custodians, brokers, transfer services and other crypto firms that want an EU passport from Sweden. The Swedish MiCA transition ended on 30 June 2026, and FI's application fee is set in a range of SEK 135,000 to 690,000.

Who needs a crypto license in Sweden?

You need CASP authorisation to provide any of the ten MiCA crypto-asset services as a business: custody, trading platform, exchange for funds or other crypto-assets, order execution, placing, order reception and transmission, advice, portfolio management and transfer services (MiCA Art 3(1)(16)). The applicant needs a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).

Other routes can fit:

  • If you already hold an EMI licence, a credit institution, investment firm or similar licence, you may notify FI instead of applying, at least 40 working days before you start (MiCA Art 60). FI's notification fee is SEK 25,500. An EMI is limited to custody and transfer of its own e-money tokens. See the EMI licence in Sweden.
  • If you are authorised in another member state, you passport into Sweden under Art 65 and need no Swedish licence. FI says 77 companies authorised elsewhere in the EU may serve Swedish consumers.
  • If you handle e-money tokens in ways that count as payment services, you may also need payment institution authorisation. See the payment institution licence in Sweden.

Sweden crypto license requirements

Capital by class

Class Minimum capital Services
Class 1 EUR 50,000 Order reception and transmission, advice, execution, placing, transfer, portfolio management
Class 2 EUR 125,000 Class 1 plus custody and exchange services
Class 3 EUR 150,000 Class 2 plus operating a trading platform

You hold the higher of the class minimum and one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads, using projected overheads in your first year (MiCA Art 67(1), (2)). Safeguards can be CET1 own funds, insurance or both (Art 67(4)).

Management and fit and proper

Directors need good repute, knowledge, skills, experience and enough time. Qualifying shareholders need good repute (MiCA Art 68(1), (2)). The regulator must refuse if they fail the test (Art 63(10)). The fee ordinance lists SEK 11,500 (class F) for notifying a new board member or CEO. FI's crypto page does not cover this, so confirm it with FI.

Substance

MiCA requires a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and an EU-resident director (Art 59(2)). We could not find a further Swedish presence rule on FI's page, so confirm local expectations with FI before you apply.

AML officer

Art 68(8) requires AML/CFT controls. Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a business-wide risk assessment. See our AML and compliance programme.

Client assets and custody

Client funds go to a credit institution or central bank by the end of the next business day, in separate accounts (MiCA Art 70). Custody needs a client agreement, a custody policy, a position register and statements at least quarterly (Art 75).

ICT and DORA

Your ICT systems must meet Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, with continuity and recovery plans (MiCA Art 68(7)).

How to get a crypto license in Sweden

  1. Map your services to the ten crypto-asset services to fix your capital class.
  2. Read FI's crypto-asset services page.
  3. Set up a legal entity with the capital and an EU-resident director.
  4. Build the MiCA Art 62(2) file under delegated regulation (EU) 2025/305, using the form in the annex to implementing regulation (EU) 2025/306.
  5. Email the application to finansinspektionen@fi.se.
  6. Wait for FI's case-handler letter with the case number, fee and payment details. FI tells you not to pay before you receive it.
  7. FI decides within 40 working days of a complete application, and it can extend that by up to 20 working days if it needs more information (MiCA Art 63(9), (12)).
  8. Within 2 working days of a grant, ESMA is informed for its register (Art 63(13)).

These are statutory limits. Preparation sits outside them.

After you get the crypto license

  • FI enters you in its company register, and ESMA lists you in its MiCA register.
  • You meet the ongoing MiCA rules on capital, governance, custody, complaints and conflicts of interest, and pay FI's supervision fee.
  • To serve other EU states, you notify FI, which forwards the notice within 10 working days. You may start on receipt of that notice or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (MiCA Art 65).
  • Adding services later needs an extension of authorisation (Art 59(8)).
  • A change in qualifying holders needs regulator approval.

Is the Swedish crypto transition over?

Yes. FI's news item of 1 July 2026 says the MiCA transition ended on 30 June 2026 and that only firms with authorisation may offer crypto services in Sweden from now on. Firms without authorisation must wind down in an orderly way and tell customers how to sell, move or close their holdings. EU-wide, ESMA says the transition ended on 1 July 2026 (MiCA Art 143(3)).

Sweden's own transition rule came from its MiCA supplementary law. FI's news item of 13 October 2025 says that from 1 October 2025 crypto firms that wanted to keep offering services in Sweden had to have applied to FI or wind down, and firms already registered with FI could continue until FI decided their application.

FI's news item named these outcomes:

Item Detail
Authorised by FI Safello AB
Authorised in other EU states and serving Swedish consumers 77 companies
Refused authorisation QB Europe AB and Ijort Invest AB, both appealed and may continue some activity pending a court decision

On 23 November 2026, the ESMA interim MiCA register lists one entry with Sweden as the home member state, Safello AB, dated 13 October 2025. ESMA keeps updating the register. Check the ESMA CASPS file and FI's company register for the current list.

Sweden crypto license vs other countries

Sweden has one authorised home firm on the ESMA register and a published fee range that depends on the case. See the MiCA licence in Denmark, Lithuania and Malta. In Sweden, the sibling pages are the EMI licence and the payment institution licence. The EU-wide rules sit in our MiCA CASP licence guide.

Crypto license in Sweden FAQs

How much does a crypto license cost in Sweden?

FI's application fee falls between SEK 135,000 and SEK 690,000, and FI sets the amount for your case. Capital is EUR 50,000, 125,000 or 150,000 by class, or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher (MiCA Art 67, Annex IV). The annual supervision fee has a minimum of SEK 150,000. Running costs come on top.

What are the Sweden crypto license requirements?

A registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU, an EU-resident director, minimum capital for your class, fit and proper directors and shareholders, AML/CFT controls, DORA-compliant ICT, custody and complaints policies (MiCA Art 59, 62, 67, 68).

How long does a MiCA license take in Sweden?

FI decides within 40 working days of a complete application and can extend that by up to 20 working days (MiCA Art 63). Preparation comes on top.

Is the Swedish MiCA transition period over?

Yes. FI says it ended on 30 June 2026. Firms without authorisation must wind down and may do only what customers need to sell, move or close their holdings.

Which Swedish firms hold a MiCA authorisation?

On the ESMA register, Safello AB is the one entry with Sweden as home member state on 23 November 2026. FI says 77 companies authorised in other EU countries may also serve Swedish consumers.

Can I notify instead of applying?

If you hold a credit institution, investment firm, EMI or similar licence, yes. You notify at least 40 working days before you start (MiCA Art 60), and FI's notification fee is SEK 25,500. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues.

Can I passport a Swedish crypto licence?

Yes. An authorised CASP notifies FI and can serve any EU member state without a local presence (MiCA Art 59(7); Art 65).
