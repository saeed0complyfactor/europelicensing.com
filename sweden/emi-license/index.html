EMI License in Sweden

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence from Finansinspektionen (FI), the Swedish Financial Supervisory Authority, lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Sweden and passport them across the EEA. It is for wallets, prepaid and card programmes and neobanks that hold customer balances. A full licence needs EUR 350,000 of capital and a Swedish aktiebolag or ekonomisk förening, and the application fee in FI's current fee ordinance is SEK 405,000.

Who needs an EMI license in Sweden?

You need it if you issue electronic money from Sweden: a stored balance that customers hold with you and spend with third parties (EMD2 Art 2(2); Art 10). Prepaid cards, wallets and accounts that hold a spendable balance usually fall in scope. An EMI can also provide the payment services in PSD2 Annex I (EMD2 Art 6(1)).

Another route can fit better:

  • If you only move money and never hold a balance for later spending, a payment institution licence in Sweden needs less capital.
  • Sweden used to let small issuers register under an exemption. The Electronic Money Act sections for that exemption were repealed by SFS 2025:254, and issuers already registered could continue to 31 December 2025, or until FI decided an application they filed before 1 January 2026. A new small issuer now applies for a full licence.
  • If you already hold an EMI licence in another EEA state, you passport into Sweden by notification and need no Swedish licence.
  • If you also want to custody or transfer your own e-money tokens, an EMI can notify under MiCA Art 60(4). Wider crypto services need a Swedish CASP authorisation.

EMI license requirements in Sweden

Legal form and substance

FI can authorise only a Swedish aktiebolag or a Swedish ekonomisk förening (Lag 2011:755, 2 kap. 1 section). The institution must have its head office in Sweden (3 kap. 11 section; PSD2 Art 11(3)). A foreign firm therefore sets up a Swedish company, and FI's page sets no minimum headcount, so agree your local team with FI before you file.

Capital and own funds

A full EMI needs at least EUR 350,000 of starting capital, measured when the licence decision is made (Lag 2011:755, 3 kap. 1 section; EMD2 Art 4). Afterwards own funds must stay at or above the higher of that figure and the calculated amount, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(1) and (3)).

Management and fit and proper

The heads of the institution need good repute and the knowledge and experience for their duties. Qualifying shareholders are tested for suitability (PSD2 Art 5(1)(m), (n); Art 11(6), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). FI's regulations FFFS 2011:49 set the application contents.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money (EMD2 Art 7(1)) either by segregating them in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). Card-funded money must be safeguarded within five business days of issuance (EMD2 Art 7(1)).

ICT and DORA

The file describes ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, incident handling, business continuity and a security policy (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e), (f), (h), (j)). If you also provide payment services, FI asks for a description of those operations under FFFS 2010:3 and FFFS 2018:4.

How to get an EMI license in Sweden

  1. Set up a Swedish aktiebolag or ekonomisk förening and pay in the capital.
  2. Prepare the file from FI's electronic money authorisation page, which links the application form. Contents follow FFFS 2011:49.
  3. Submit the application to FI. FI registers it and issues a reference number.
  4. Wait for FI's administrator letter, which gives the fee and payment details. FI tells you not to pay before you have the letter and reference number.
  5. Pay the fee. FI starts processing only when payment arrives, and it may reject the application if payment does not arrive within 30 days (Forordning 2001:911, section 6).
  6. FI decides within three months of receiving a complete application and the fee (Lag 2011:755, 2 kap. 8 section). The statute text we read adds that the licence is deemed granted if FI does not decide in time. Do not plan around that rule, and confirm how FI applies it.

These are statutory limits. Preparation time and question rounds come on top, and we give you a dated plan after scoping. FI's page does not state an application language, so ask FI which language it accepts.

After you get the EMI license

  • FI enters you in its company register, which you can search by main business, including electronic money issuers.
  • You report to FI under its prudential and payment-services rules and keep own funds above the required level.
  • You pay the annual supervision fee.
  • To serve another EEA state, you notify FI, which forwards the notice within 1 month. The host state assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28(1) and (2)). You may use distributors abroad but not agents to issue e-money (EMD2 Art 3(4) and (5)).
  • Material changes to safeguarding need advance notice (EMD2 Art 3(2)). A change of qualifying holding needs FI's permission, and FI's fee for that permission is SEK 33,000.

Sweden EMI license vs other countries

Sweden charges SEK 405,000 to apply, and it no longer has a small-issuer exemption. To compare, see the EMI licence in Denmark, the EMI licence in Lithuania and the EMI licence in Ireland. Within Sweden, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence. For the EU rules behind all of them, read our EMI licence in Europe guide.

EMI license in Sweden FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Sweden?

EUR 350,000 (Lag 2011:755, 3 kap. 1 section; EMD2 Art 4). It is measured when FI makes its licence decision.

How much does an EMI licence cost in Sweden?

The application fee in FI's fee ordinance is SEK 405,000, and the annual supervision fee shown by FI is SEK 150,000. Add EUR 350,000 of capital and the running costs of a regulated firm: local people, audit, safeguarding, ICT and advisers.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Sweden?

FI decides within three months of a complete application and payment of the fee (Lag 2011:755, 2 kap. 8 section). Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

Does Sweden have a small or restricted EMI licence?

No. The registered-issuer exemption in the Electronic Money Act was repealed by SFS 2025:254. Issuers registered before that change could continue to 31 December 2025, or until FI decided an application filed before 1 January 2026.

Do I need a Swedish company?

Yes. FI can authorise only a Swedish aktiebolag or ekonomisk förening, and the head office must be in Sweden.

Can I file in English?

FI's page does not state a language rule for applications. Its guidance exists in English and Swedish. Ask FI which language it accepts before you draft.

Can I passport a Swedish EMI licence?

Yes, by notifying FI (PSD2 Art 28).

Can I buy a Swedish EMI instead of applying?

Yes, but the buyer needs FI's permission to acquire a qualifying holding. We do not list firms for sale.
