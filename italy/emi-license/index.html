EMI License in Italy

An electronic money institution (EMI, or istituto di moneta elettronica, IMEL) licence from the Banca d'Italia lets an Italian company issue e-money and passport it across the EU and EEA. The procedure runs 90 days from a complete and regular application, the registered office and head office must be in Italy, and the Disposizioni di vigilanza provide a limited-operation IMEL (IMEL a operatività limitata) for up to EUR 5 million of average e-money outstanding, with no passport.

Who needs an EMI license in Italy?

You need authorisation as an IMEL if you issue electronic money in Italy as a business. Wallets and prepaid products with a spendable balance usually fall in scope. An IMEL can also provide payment services. If you offer payment initiation or account information, you also need liability insurance or an equivalent guarantee.

Some firms need a different route.

  • You only transmit money and hold no stored value. The payment institution licence in Italy carries lower capital.
  • You are already an EMI in another EEA state. You passport into Italy by notification (PSD2 Art 28).
  • You are small. Chapter IX of the Disposizioni di vigilanza (consolidated text to 22 February 2022) covers the IMEL a operatività limitata: average e-money outstanding up to EUR 5 million, no activity in other countries, lighter prudential rules and a EUR 150 loading limit per customer. If you pass the threshold, you tell the Banca d'Italia within 30 days and have 60 more days to meet the full rules or stop. Confirm the current text with the Banca d'Italia.
  • You also want to offer crypto services. An IMEL can notify the Banca d'Italia under MiCA Art 60 for notifiable services. See the MiCA crypto licence in Italy.

EMI license requirements in Italy

Capital and own funds

Initial capital must be paid in, at not less than EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4; Banca d'Italia IMEL page). The bank that holds the deposit issues the proof of payment. Own funds then stay at or above 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

Legal form and substance

The IMEL must be a società per azioni, a società in accomandita per azioni, a società a responsabilità limitata or a cooperative. Both the registered office (sede legale) and the head office (direzione generale) must be in Italy.

Fit and proper

Managers need professionalism, honorability and independence, and meet the competence and correctness criteria for their full term, not only at appointment (TUB art. 26; Ministerial Decree 169/2020). Qualifying shareholders are assessed under TUB art. 19. The interlocking directorship ban in art. 36 of Decree-Law 201/2011 applies.

Safeguarding

Funds received for issued e-money are safeguarded by segregation or by insurance or a guarantee (EMD2 Art 7). If the IMEL also runs a commercial business, TUB art. 114-terdecies ring-fences the e-money equity.

AML, governance and ICT

The business programme with its organisational structure report covers governance, controls and the user-funds protection measures. Add ICT and DORA arrangements (PSD2 Art 5(1)). See our AML and compliance programme.

How to apply for an EMI license in Italy

  1. Define the business model. The programme of operations and a business plan that shows the capital is sustainable sit at the centre of the file.
  2. Ask for a pre-filing meeting if you want one. The Banca d'Italia says it is optional and does not change the time limits. Write to Servizio.Riv.Costituzioni@bancaditalia.it or riv@pec.bancaditalia.it with your contact details. Questions that the website already answers get no reply.
  3. Prepare the documents the Disposizioni di vigilanza list. They include the deed of incorporation and by-laws, the programme of operations, shareholder lists and qualifying-shareholder documents, a group map, proof of the capital deposit, source of funds, manager suitability documents and user-funds protection measures. The application itself is free-form. Documents under items d, g and h must be dated within 6 months of filing.
  4. File by PEC. Under the Disposizioni (consolidated text to 22 February 2022), the administrators file after the deed of incorporation and before registration in the companies register.
  5. The 90 days run from a complete and regular application. If it has defects, you get a notice listing them and the procedure does not start. The Banca d'Italia can ask for information, inspect, or take opinions from other authorities, and the clock can be suspended for at most 180 days.
  6. If the outcome looks negative, you get a notice of grounds for refusal and 10 days to send written observations.
  7. After authorisation, send the companies-register certificate. The Banca d'Italia enters you in the IMEL register with a code, and you join an out-of-court dispute resolution scheme (TUB art. 128-bis). Start operating within 12 months, or the authorisation lapses. The IMEL page and the Disposizioni count the 12 months from entry in the register, while the FAQ counts from authorisation, so plan on the earlier date. An extension is usually of up to 6 months.

The pages we read do not state the language of the file. Ask in the pre-filing meeting.

EMI license cost in Italy

The Banca d'Italia IMEL pages and market-access page state no application fee. Ask the Banca d'Italia whether any supervisory charge applies to IMELs before you budget.

After the Banca d'Italia grants the license

  • Register. You are entered in the IMEL register with an identification code (TUB art. 114-quater).
  • Start of business. Notify the Banca d'Italia within 12 months of entry in the register, as the IMEL page sets out.
  • Passport. You notify the Banca d'Italia of the states and services. It forwards the notice within 1 month and the host state assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Change of control. Qualifying holdings are assessed under TUB art. 19. Confirm the notice route with the Banca d'Italia before any share transfer.

Italy compared with other countries

If you only provide payments, see the payment institution licence in Italy. For crypto services the authority is Consob, covered in the MiCA crypto licence in Italy. For the same licence elsewhere, compare the EMI licence in France, Spain and Lithuania. The EU baseline is on the EMI licence page.

EMI license in Italy FAQs

How much does an EMI license cost in Italy?

The Banca d'Italia pages we read state no application fee. You need EUR 350,000 of paid-in capital (EMD2 Art 4) and an Italian entity with its head office in Italy. our fee range for EMI licence in Italy

How much capital do you need for an EMI license in Italy?

EUR 350,000, paid in, at authorisation. Own funds then stay at or above 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

How long does the Banca d'Italia take to decide?

The procedure runs 90 days from a complete and regular application. Suspensions can last at most 180 days. Your preparation time comes before the clock starts.

How do I get an EMI license in Italy?

You form an Italian company, prepare the documents the Disposizioni list, file by PEC, answer information requests, and enter the IMEL register after authorisation.

Is there a small EMI license in Italy?

Yes. The Disposizioni di vigilanza provide a limited-operation IMEL for up to EUR 5 million of average e-money outstanding. It cannot operate in other countries.

Do I need an office in Italy?

Yes. The registered office and the head office must be in Italy.

Does the application have to be in Italian?

The pages we read do not say. The application is free-form. Ask at the pre-filing meeting.

Can an Italian EMI serve customers in other EU countries?

Yes, by notification under PSD2 Art 28 applied through EMD2 Art 3(1).
