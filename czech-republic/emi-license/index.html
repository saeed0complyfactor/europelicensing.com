EMI License in Czech Republic

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from the Czech Republic. The Czech National Bank (ČNB) grants it under the Payment Services Act, and the EU passport carries it into the rest of the EEA. The ČNB application fee is CZK 50,000, and the capital requirement is EUR 350,000.

Who needs an EMI licence in the Czech Republic?

You need it if you issue e-money in the Czech Republic or from a Czech company: prepaid cards, wallets with a stored balance and similar products. An EMI may also provide payment services, and the Czech act sets out the permitted activities in Sec 67.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A payment institution licence if you never hold stored value. See payment institution licence in the Czech Republic.
  • The small e-money issuer authorisation, if you expect average e-money in circulation in the Czech Republic to stay under EUR 5,000,000. Payment services unrelated to e-money are limited to EUR 3,000,000 in monthly average transactions (Sec 99(2)-(3)). It is a domestic tier, so it carries no passport (EMD2 Art 9(3)).
  • An e-money token issuer. Under MiCA Art 48(1) only credit institutions and EMIs may offer e-money tokens. The ČNB said on 19 October 2026 that it is assessing two EMI applications from firms planning to issue them.
  • An EMI authorised in another EEA state that passports into the Czech Republic.

EMI licence requirements in the Czech Republic

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is EUR 350,000, made up of the items in Art 26(1)(a) to (e) of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (Act 370/2017 Sec 74(1)-(2)). Capital may not fall below that level, and the EMI must keep capital adequacy (Sec 74(3)). The ČNB may require up to 20% more or allow up to 20% less based on your governance and control system (Sec 74(5)). The EU own funds method for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

Seat and substance

The applicant must be a legal person with its registered seat and its actual seat in the Czech Republic, and it must carry on at least part of its payment business there (Sec 68(1)(a), (b), (n)). A company that does not yet exist can be the applicant through the person named as its statutory body in the founding act (Sec 68(3)).

Fit and proper

Managers must be trustworthy, professionally qualified and experienced (Sec 68(1)(l), (m)). Persons with a qualifying holding must be trustworthy and able to secure sound and prudent management (Sec 68(1)(j)). The ČNB publishes its own notices on trustworthiness and professional competence, and it provides separate forms for natural persons and legal entities.

Business plan, governance and safeguarding

The business plan includes a budget for the first 3 accounting periods backed by real economic calculations (Sec 68(1)(e)). Your material, technical, staffing and organisational set-up must suit sound e-money issuance (Sec 68(1)(f)), and your governance and control system must meet the act (Sec 68(1)(g)). You must protect the funds exchanged for e-money and the funds users hand over for payments (Sec 68(1)(h)). The EU safeguarding methods are in PSD2 Art 10 as applied to EMIs. ICT risk follows Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), and AML controls follow the Czech AML framework and Directive (EU) 2015/849.

How to apply for an EMI licence in the Czech Republic

  1. Map your products to the permitted activities and fix your capital and own funds model.
  2. Set up the Czech company with its registered and actual seat in the Czech Republic.
  3. Pay in the EUR 350,000 and keep the evidence.
  4. Build the file against the ČNB EMI application form and the attachments listed in Sec 11 of Decree No. 1/2022 Coll.
  5. File electronically only: through the data box, or by email with a recognised electronic signature to the ČNB's electronic registry (Act 370/2017 Sec 69(1); ČNB licensing page).
  6. Pay the CZK 50,000 administrative fee.
  7. Answer the ČNB's questions. It decides within 3 months of the start of proceedings (Sec 69(2)).

The ČNB's licensing page links its 2025 methodology for assessing applications and the EBA guidelines on authorisation (EBA-GL-2017-09). Its pages show no pre-application meeting offer and do not state the filing language, so ask the ČNB before you translate the file.

The Czech text counts the 3 months from the start of proceedings. PSD2 Art 12, which applies to EMIs through EMD2 Art 3(1), counts from receipt of a complete application. Preparing the file and answering questions comes on top of either clock.

After authorisation

The ČNB keeps public lists of regulated institutions. You must notify the ČNB without undue delay of any change to the data in your application (Sec 70(1)), and a change to the scope of the authorisation needs a new application with a CZK 20,000 fee. Acquiring a qualifying holding, appointing managers and outsourcing also go through the ČNB, using its forms (Decree 1/2022 Sec 17 and 20). To serve other EEA states you notify the ČNB under PSD2 Art 28, which forwards the file to the host state. The host state assesses it within 1 month, and for an agent or branch the home regulator decides within 3 months.

EMI in the Czech Republic compared

The capital and the 3-month clock come from EU law, so they match other states. Fees, language and local expectations differ. Compare the EMI licence in Lithuania, Ireland and the Netherlands. In the Czech Republic you can also read the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence pages. The EU-wide rules are in our EMI licence guide.

EMI in Czech Republic FAQs

How much capital does a Czech EMI need?

EUR 350,000 of initial capital (Act 370/2017 Sec 74(1), matching EMD2 Art 4). The ČNB can set ongoing capital adequacy up to 20% higher or lower (Sec 74(5)).

How much does the ČNB charge for an EMI licence?

CZK 50,000 for accepting the application, under Item 65 of the annex to Act 634/2004. A later change to the authorisation costs CZK 20,000.

How long does the ČNB take to decide?

The Czech act says within 3 months of the start of proceedings (Sec 69(2)). The EU rule is 3 months from a complete application. Your preparation time is extra.

Does the Czech Republic have a small EMI?

Yes. The small e-money issuer may issue e-money while average e-money in circulation in the Czech Republic stays within EUR 5,000,000 (Sec 99(2)). It cannot passport (EMD2 Art 9(3)).

Can a Czech EMI issue e-money tokens?

Yes. MiCA Art 48(1) lets credit institutions and EMIs offer e-money tokens. The ČNB reported on 19 October 2026 that two EMI applications under assessment came from firms planning to issue them.

Can I apply from outside the Czech Republic?

The applicant must be a legal person with its registered and actual seat in the Czech Republic, and a company that does not yet exist can apply through its designated statutory body (Sec 68(1), (3)). The act has an exception for non-EU applicants only where an international treaty requires it (Sec 68(2)).

Can I file on paper?

No. Applications can be filed only electronically (Sec 69(1)).

Can I passport a Czech EMI licence?

Yes, for an authorised EMI, by notification through the ČNB under PSD2 Art 28 applied by EMD2 Art 3(1). A small e-money issuer cannot.
