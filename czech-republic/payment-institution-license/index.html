Payment Institution License in Czech Republic

A payment institution (PI) licence lets you provide payment services such as money remittance, payment initiation and card acquiring from a Czech company, without issuing e-money. The Czech National Bank (ČNB) grants it under the Payment Services Act. The ČNB fee is CZK 50,000, and the capital requirement is EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 depending on the services.

Who needs a payment institution licence in the Czech Republic?

You need one if you provide payment services in the Czech Republic as a business and you are not a bank or an e-money institution. Typical cases are money remittance, payment initiation, card acquiring and issuing of payment instruments, and the account servicing that goes with them.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A small payment service provider authorisation, if the monthly average of your transactions in the Czech Republic over the last 12 months stays at or under EUR 3,000,000. It is a domestic tier (Sec 58(2)).
  • An account information service provider authorisation, which has its own ČNB fee of CZK 25,000 and its own application section in Decree 1/2022 (Sec 6).
  • Acting as an authorised representative (agent) of a Czech payment institution, which the ČNB enters in its list on the institution's notification (Sec 26 and 27). An agent of a payment institution from another EEA state is registered by that institution's home regulator (Sec 40(1)).
  • An EMI licence if you also issue stored value.

Payment institution licence requirements in the Czech Republic

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is EUR 20,000 if you provide only money remittance, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation, and EUR 125,000 for any of services 1 to 5 of PSD2 Annex I (Act 370/2017 Sec 15(1)). It consists of the items in Art 26(1)(a) to (e) of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (Sec 15(2)). A PI keeps capital at or above that level and, for most service types, also keeps capital adequacy (Sec 16(1)-(2)). The ČNB sets the calculation approach in the authorisation decision and may require up to 20% more or allow up to 20% less (Sec 16(3)-(4)).

Seat and substance

The applicant must be a legal person with its registered seat and its actual seat in the Czech Republic, and it must carry on at least part of its payment business there (Sec 9(1)(a), (b), (l)). Where you hold other business next to payments, it must not threaten the PI's financial stability or block supervision (Sec 9(1)(i)).

Fit and proper

Managers must be trustworthy, professionally qualified and experienced (Sec 9(1)(m), (n)). Persons with a qualifying holding must be trustworthy and able to secure sound and prudent management (Sec 9(1)(j)). Close links must not block supervision (Sec 9(1)(k)).

Insurance, business plan and safeguarding

For payment initiation or account information you need an insurance contract or comparable guarantee (Sec 9(1)(d)). The business plan carries a budget for the first 3 accounting periods backed by real calculations (Sec 9(1)(e)). Your set-up and governance must meet the act (Sec 9(1)(f), (g)), and you must protect funds users hand over for payment transactions (Sec 9(1)(h)). The EU safeguarding methods are in PSD2 Art 10. ICT risk follows Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), and AML controls follow the Czech AML framework and Directive (EU) 2015/849.

How to apply for a payment institution licence in the Czech Republic

  1. Map your services to PSD2 Annex I to fix your capital tier and own funds approach.
  2. Set up the Czech company with its registered and actual seat in the Czech Republic. A company not yet formed can apply through the person named as its statutory body, and the licence lapses if the company is not formed within 6 months of the decision becoming final (Sec 9(3)).
  3. Pay in the capital and keep the evidence.
  4. Build the file against the ČNB PI application form and the attachments in Sec 3 of Decree No. 1/2022 Coll.
  5. File electronically only: through the data box, or by email with a recognised electronic signature to the ČNB's electronic registry (Sec 10(1); ČNB licensing page).
  6. Pay the CZK 50,000 administrative fee.
  7. Answer the ČNB's questions. It decides within 3 months of the start of proceedings (Sec 10(2)) and names the authorised payment services in its decision (Sec 10(3)).

The ČNB links its 2025 assessment methodology and the EBA guidelines on authorisation (EBA-GL-2017-09) on its licensing page. The page shows no pre-application meeting offer and does not state the filing language.

PSD2 Art 12 counts 3 months from receipt of a complete application, and the Czech act counts from the start of proceedings. Preparing the file takes time on top of either.

After authorisation

The ČNB keeps public lists of regulated institutions. You must tell the ČNB without undue delay about changes to the data in your application (Sec 11(1)), and a wider service scope needs a new application with a CZK 20,000 fee. You also report on your finances and results, and keep records of your obligations for at least 5 years (Sec 30 and 31). To serve other EEA states you notify the ČNB under PSD2 Art 28. The host state assesses the notice within 1 month, and for an agent or branch the home regulator decides within 3 months. The ČNB provides the passport forms in English based on Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2055. Qualifying holdings, new managers and outsourcing need their own ČNB filings.

The capital tiers and the 3-month clock come from PSD2, so they match other states. Fees, language and local expectations differ. Payment institution in Czech Republic FAQs

How much capital does a Czech payment institution need?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation, and EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5 of PSD2 Annex I (Act 370/2017 Sec 15(1)).

How much does the ČNB charge for a payment institution licence?

CZK 50,000 for accepting the application, CZK 25,000 for an account information service provider, and CZK 10,000 for a small payment service provider (Act 634/2004, annex Item 65).

How long does the ČNB take to decide?

The Czech act says within 3 months of the start of proceedings (Sec 10(2)). PSD2 Art 12 says 3 months from a complete application. Your preparation time is extra.

Is there a small payment institution in the Czech Republic?

Yes, the small payment service provider. It may operate while the monthly average of transactions in the Czech Republic over 12 months stays at or below EUR 3,000,000 (Sec 58(2)). Managers and holders must be trustworthy (Sec 59(1)(f)).

Can I apply from outside the Czech Republic?

A PI applicant needs its registered and actual seat in the Czech Republic (Sec 9(1)(b)). The act waives this for non-EU applicants only where an international treaty requires it (Sec 9(2)).

Can I file on paper?

No. Applications can be filed only electronically (Sec 10(1)).

Do I need a PI licence to be an agent of another EEA payment institution?

No separate PI licence. A foreign institution serves the Czech Republic through its own home authorisation, and its agent is registered with its home regulator (Sec 40(1)). The ČNB's own list of authorised representatives covers agents of Czech institutions (Sec 26).

Can I passport a Czech payment institution licence?

Yes, by notification through the ČNB under PSD2 Art 28. The small payment service provider tier cannot passport (PSD2 Art 32(3)).
