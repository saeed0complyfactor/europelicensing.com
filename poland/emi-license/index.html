EMI License in Poland

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence in Poland lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from a Polish company. The Polish Financial Supervision Authority (KNF) grants it, and the Act requires EUR 350,000 in initial capital. A smaller route exists inside a payment institution licence, but it limits e-money to Poland.

Who needs an EMI license in Poland?

Issuing e-money or providing payment services as a domestic e-money institution (krajowa instytucja pieniadza elektronicznego, KIPE) needs KNF authorisation (Art 132a(1)). The applicant must be a legal person seated in Poland (Art 132a(2)). A balance that your customer can hold and spend later is e-money. A product that only moves money for a single payment is a payment service and may fit a payment institution licence instead.

Other routes can fit better:

  • Payment institution with e-money rights. A domestic payment institution with at least EUR 125,000 capital may issue e-money under Art 73a. It may do so only in Poland, and average e-money in circulation cannot exceed EUR 5,000,000 in any month.
  • Agent or branch of another EEA firm. An EMI authorised in another member state can serve Poland by notification. KNF's 2024 report records 90 notifications from other regulators about EU e-money institutions operating in Poland.
  • Small payment institution. Registration only, Poland only, and no e-money. See the payment institution page.

KNF's 2024 report counts one authorised KIPE at 31 December 2024, against 46 domestic payment institutions.

EMI license requirements in Poland

Capital and own funds. Initial capital must be at least EUR 350,000 in PLN (Art 132b(1)). The funds covering initial capital must be free of credit, loans and other encumbrances, and must come from lawful, disclosed sources (Art 132b(2) applying Art 64(1)(5)). You also need own funds at the required level (Art 64(1)(2)). Art 132m sets own funds at the higher of the initial capital and the calculated requirement, and the e-money part cannot be less than 2% of average outstanding e-money (Art 132m(2) and (4), following EMD2 Art 5).

Management and fit and proper. Managers must give a guarantee of prudent and stable management (Art 64(1)(4)). Your application carries documents on education, work history and any criminal, disciplinary, administrative and civil proceedings for managers and significant shareholders. KNF reviews the same facts for owners.

Substance. The licence goes to a legal person seated in Poland. KNF's pages do not set a number of Polish directors or a local office size, so ask UKNF before you fix your board.

AML. Your risk management and internal control system must cover anti-money laundering duties (Art 64(1)(3)). The Act of 1 March 2018 on counteracting money laundering and terrorist financing applies to the institution.

Safeguarding. You must protect funds received in exchange for e-money under Art 132n, which allows segregation or a bank or insurance guarantee (Art 132b(4)). KNF can withdraw the licence if you use a guarantee route and fail to sign the contract in time (Art 132b(5)). Your risk system must also describe how you handle complaints from e-money holders (Art 132b(2)).

ICT and DORA. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly. Build your ICT risk framework and incident procedures before you file, since KNF reads the security and continuity description in the application.

How to get an EMI license in Poland: process

  1. Check the route. Decide between a KIPE, a payment institution with Art 73a, or an authorisation elsewhere with a Polish passport.
  2. Look at KNF's before-filing page. KNF publishes a "before filing" page for payment authorisations. The filing page does not mention a pre-application meeting, so ask UKNF whether one is available.
  3. Prepare the file. Art 132a(3) applies Art 61(1): registered office, articles, list of services, a business programme and financial plan for at least three years that states planned average e-money in circulation, proof of own funds, the risk management and control description, close links, managers and shareholders, and the auditor. A Ministry of Finance regulation of 13 November 2020 sets the form of each document.
  4. File in writing. Send the signed application through UKNF e-Doreczenia, by post, or in person at the KNF registry office. Submit searchable electronic files. Use the KNF application form.
  5. Completeness. KNF must give you at least 7 days to fix gaps. Unfixed gaps lead to the application being left unexamined.
  6. Decision. KNF decides within 3 months of receiving the complete application or its last supplement (Art 62(2) via Art 132b(3)). The time to supplement documents extends the clock.

The statutory period covers KNF's decision. Preparation and your answers to KNF's questions come on top of it.

For comparison, KNF's fee page lists EUR 1,250 for a domestic payment institution licence (page updated 7 November 2023). We did not find a KIPE amount on a KNF page, so ask UKNF for the current figure.

Other cost lines, without amounts: your Polish company and registered office, directors and local staff, an AML officer, a safeguarding account or guarantee, statutory audit, ICT security, and legal and translation work. Our fee depends on scope: our fee range for an EMI licence in Poland.

After you get the EMI license

  • Register entry. KNF enters you in its register of payment service providers and e-money issuers.
  • Supervision payment. Pay by 30 November and file the declaration within 10 days after the deadline.
  • Change of control. Anyone who will reach 20%, 30% or 50% of votes or capital must notify KNF first (Art 132c-132e). KNF can object by decision. In 2024 it issued two objections across KIP and KIPE cases.
  • Passport. Notify KNF in writing before you serve another EEA state through a branch, an agent or cross-border services. KNF forwards the notification to the host regulator.
  • Changes to the licence. Changes to services or details are subject to a fee under Art 132zf, except a change that only narrows the services.

For comparison, see Lithuania. For other regulators, see Ireland and Estonia. In Poland itself, a payment institution licence costs less capital, and the crypto page explains why a Polish MiCA licence is not available at present. For the EU rules, read the EMI licence guide.

EMI in Poland FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Poland?

EUR 350,000, converted to PLN at the NBP average rate on the day KNF issues the licence (Art 132b(1)). A payment institution with at least EUR 125,000 can issue e-money in Poland only, up to EUR 5,000,000 average outstanding (Art 73a).

How long does KNF take to decide on an EMI licence?

KNF has 3 months from a complete application or its supplement (Art 62(2) via Art 132b(3)). The clock extends by the time you need to supplement the file.

Can I get a small EMI licence in Poland?

The Act has no separate small EMI. EMD2 Art 9 lets member states waive full authorisation below EUR 5,000,000 average e-money. The nearest Polish route is Art 73a, which needs a payment institution licence and limits e-money to Poland.

Does a Polish EMI licence work in the rest of the EU?

Yes. A KIPE can notify KNF of its intention to serve other EEA states, and KNF informs the host regulator. An Art 73a payment institution cannot issue e-money outside Poland.

How many e-money institutions are there in Poland?

KNF's report counts one domestic e-money institution at 31 December 2024, next to 46 domestic payment institutions and 184 small payment institutions. Check the KNF register for today's figure.

Do I need a Polish company?

Yes. The authorisation goes to a legal person seated in Poland (Art 132a(2)).

Which language do I file in?

KNF's filing page does not state it. Confirm with UKNF before you prepare translations.

Can I buy an existing Polish EMI?

You can buy shares, but KNF must not object to acquiring 20%, 30% or 50% of votes or capital (Art 132c-132e). Plan for that review.
