Payment Institution License in Poland

A payment institution licence in Poland lets a Polish company provide payment services such as transfers, card acquiring or money remittance. The Polish Financial Supervision Authority (KNF) grants it, and initial capital is EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the service. Poland also runs a registration route for small firms, with a lower threshold than the EU default.

Who needs a payment institution license in Poland?

You need KNF authorisation to provide payment services as a domestic payment institution (krajowa instytucja platnicza, KIP). The applicant is a legal person seated in Poland that carries out at least part of its payment business there (Art 60(2)).

Check the lighter routes first:

  • Small payment institution (MIP). Registration instead of authorisation. The monthly average of your payment transactions over the previous 12 months, including agents' transactions, cannot exceed EUR 1,500,000 (Art 117f(3)). You may operate only in Poland (Art 117f(2)). KNF decides on a complete application within 3 months, and the registration fee is PLN 616 stamp duty. This is lower than the EUR 3,000,000 limit that PSD2 Art 32 allows.
  • Account information only. Providers of account information services register instead of seeking authorisation (Art 117b). KNF's 2024 report lists 15 on the register.
  • Agent or branch of another EEA payment institution. KNF's 2024 report records 162 notifications from other regulators about EU payment institutions operating in Poland.
  • Payment institution that issues e-money. With at least EUR 125,000 capital, a KIP may issue e-money in Poland only, up to EUR 5,000,000 average in any month (Art 73a). Wider e-money needs an EMI licence.

KNF's 2024 report shows 46 KIPs and 184 MIPs at 31 December 2024. In 2024 KNF received 8 KIP applications, 3 of them for changes, and issued 4 final authorisation decisions.

Payment institution license requirements in Poland

Capital. Initial capital must be at least EUR 125,000 for any of services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation, or EUR 20,000 for money remittance only (Art 64(1)(1)). Converted to PLN, the funds must be free of credit and other encumbrances and come from lawful, disclosed sources (Art 64(1)(5)).

Own funds. You need own funds at the required level (Art 64(1)(2)). Ministry of Finance regulations set the calculation methods under the Act, and PSD2 Art 9 defines methods A, B and C.

Management and fit and proper. Managers must give a guarantee of prudent and stable management (Art 64(1)(4)). The application documents education, work history and criminal, disciplinary, administrative and civil proceedings for managers and significant shareholders.

Substance. The company is seated in Poland (Art 60(2)). PSD2 Art 11(3) also puts the head office in the same member state as the registered office. KNF's pages do not set a number of local directors, so ask UKNF.

AML. Your risk management and internal control system must cover anti-money laundering duties (Art 64(1)(3)). The Act of 1 March 2018 on counteracting money laundering and terrorist financing applies to you.

Safeguarding. Your organisation must protect users' funds under Art 78 (Art 64(1)(3)), by segregation or by insurance or a guarantee.

Insurance. Payment initiation and account information providers must provide professional liability insurance, a bank guarantee or similar security for user claims.

ICT and DORA. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly, and KNF reads the security and continuity description in the application.

How to get a payment institution license in Poland: process

  1. Pick the route. Authorisation, registration as a small payment institution, or a passport from another state.
  2. Check KNF's before-filing page. KNF publishes one for KIP authorisations. The filing page does not mention a pre-application meeting, so ask UKNF.
  3. Prepare the file. The KNF list covers: registered office, articles, services to be provided, a business programme and financial plan for at least three years, proof of own funds, risk management and internal control (including audit, compliance, security, continuity, complaints and incident handling), close links, managers and shareholders with evidence of sound management, and statutory auditors. A Minister of Finance regulation of 13 November 2020 sets the form of each document.
  4. File in writing. Send the signed application through UKNF e-Doreczenia, by post, or in person at the KNF registry office. Submit searchable electronic files, and send later corrections as a consolidated text and as tracked changes. Use the KNF form.
  5. Completeness. If documents are missing, KNF gives you at least 7 days to fix them. Gaps left open mean the application is left unexamined.
  6. Decision. KNF decides within 3 months of receiving the complete application or its last supplement (Art 62(2)). The time you take to supplement the file extends the clock.
  7. Register and start. KNF enters you in the register of payment service providers.

KNF reviews formal completeness first, then the substance. It writes to you with comments if the documents need changes.

After you get the payment institution license

  • Register entry. KNF records you in its register of payment service providers, and later changes to the register data, such as a new name, seat, branch or agent, carry a EUR 400 fee.
  • Reporting and supervision. KNF supervises KIPs and publishes their statistics. Its 2024 report tracks KIP payment volumes quarter by quarter.
  • Change of control. KNF's 2024 report records non-objection decisions for buyers reaching 20%, 30% or 50% of votes or capital in a KIP. Notify KNF before you transfer shares.
  • Passport. A KIP gives KNF written notice before using a branch, an agent or cross-border services in another EEA state (Art 92; PSD2 Art 28). In 2024 KIPs submitted 7 first notifications.
  • Moving to e-money. A KIP that wants to issue e-money notifies KNF and updates its business plan (Art 73a(3)).

Payment institution in Poland compared with other countries

Poland's small payment institution limit is EUR 1,500,000 a month, half the PSD2 ceiling. Payment institution in Poland FAQs

How much capital does a payment institution need in Poland?

EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, converted to PLN (Art 64(1)(1)).

How long does KNF take to decide?

3 months from receiving a complete application or its last supplement (Art 62(2)). The clock extends while you supplement documents.

What is a small payment institution in Poland?

A firm registered with KNF that serves only Poland, with monthly payment transactions averaging no more than EUR 1,500,000 over 12 months (Art 117f). It registers instead of seeking authorisation.

Can a Polish payment institution passport into other EU countries?

Yes. A KIP notifies KNF in writing, and KNF informs the host regulator (Art 92). A small payment institution cannot, since it operates in Poland only.

What does the KNF licence fee cost?

KNF's fee page lists EUR 1,250 in PLN for granting a KIP licence and EUR 400 for amending one, as updated on 7 November 2023. A small payment institution pays PLN 616 stamp duty.

Do I need an office in Poland?

You need a legal person seated in Poland that carries out part of its payment business there (Art 60(2)). KNF's pages do not set a minimum office size.

Can I provide payment initiation or account information only?

Yes. Payment initiation needs EUR 50,000 capital and professional liability cover. Account information needs registration (Art 117b) and cover, but no capital.

Which language do I file in?

KNF's filing page does not state it. Ask UKNF before you prepare translations.
