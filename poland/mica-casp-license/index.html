Crypto License in Poland

You cannot get a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) licence in Poland today, because no Polish authority has been designated to grant one. The Sejm failed to override the President's veto of the crypto-asset market act on 4 September 2026, and as of 23 November 2026 we found no law in force. Polish firms get a MiCA authorisation in another member state and passport it into Poland.

Who needs a crypto license in Poland?

A firm that provides crypto-asset services to clients in Poland on a professional basis needs CASP authorisation under MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114. The transitional period ended on 1 July 2026, as the UKNF statement says, and the deadline cannot be extended by law or KNF decision. Services include custody, trading platforms, exchange, execution, placing, order reception, advice, portfolio management and transfers. See the MiCA licence guide for the full list.

Which route fits a Polish firm:

  • Apply in another member state. Under Art 59(2) the applicant needs a registered office in a member state where it carries on part of its services, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director. A Polish founder can form a company there. A Polish company cannot apply in Poland.
  • Credit institutions, EMIs, investment firms and others. MiCA Art 60 lets them notify instead of applying, but an EMI only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues. Whether a Polish KIPE can use it with no designated Polish authority is a question for the UKNF.
  • Serve Polish clients from another EEA CASP. A CASP authorised elsewhere may serve Poland after its home authority forwards the Art 65 notification. The UKNF confirms that this works without a Polish authority.
  • Issuing e-money tokens. The UKNF says KNF is competent for e-money token issuers. See the EMI licence page for the Polish e-money licence.

Crypto license requirements for a Polish-based firm

Because Poland has no authority, the requirements come from MiCA and the state where you apply.

Requirement MiCA rule
Capital Higher of the Annex IV class minimum (EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000) or one quarter of last year's fixed overheads (Art 67)
Management Directors of good repute with the right knowledge, skills, experience and time (Art 68(1))
Substance Registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU, one EU-resident director (Art 59(2))
Client assets Segregation and custody policy (Art 70, Art 75)
ICT and DORA Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (Art 68(7))
AML AML/CFT controls (Art 68(8))

Directors and shareholders based in Poland go through the fit and proper tests of the regulator where you apply (Art 68(1) and (2)).

How to get a crypto license if you are based in Poland

  1. Choose the home state. Compare regulators on fees, language, and the number of CASPs they have authorised. See Lithuania, Malta and Estonia.
  2. Set up the EU entity. Satisfy Art 59(2).
  3. File the Art 62 application. The regulator confirms completeness within 25 working days and decides within 40 working days of a complete file (Art 63). Questions raised by the 20th working day can pause the clock for up to 20 working days.
  4. Passport into Poland. Send the Art 65 notification to your home regulator. It forwards within 10 working days, and you can start on receipt or on the 15th calendar day.
  5. Check whether any Polish rules apply. The AML register for virtual currency activities, kept by the Tax Administration Chamber in Katowice, remains an AML registration. The UKNF statement lists it as the pre-MiCA basis for Polish entities, and it gives no MiCA authorisation after 1 July 2026.

How much does a crypto license cost for a Polish firm?

There is no Polish fee. Official cost lines:

Item Amount Source
Minimum capital EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class, or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher MiCA Art 67, Annex IV
Application and supervision fees Set by the regulator of the state where you apply That regulator's fee schedule
Polish application fee None, since no authority is designated UKNF statement

Polish fee figures quoted on other websites do not come from an enacted law, because none exists.

Other cost lines, without amounts: an EU legal entity, EU-resident directors, local staff and an office where required, audit, AML officer, ICT security, custody arrangements, professional indemnity or own funds cover and legal work. After authorisation

Your home regulator supervises you and lists you on the ESMA register. You report to it, pay its supervision fee, and notify it of changes. Before you serve Polish clients, your home regulator must forward your Art 65 notification, and you can start on receipt or on the 15th calendar day.

The UKNF statement says Polish entities that relied on Art 143(3) lose the right to provide crypto-asset services after 1 July 2026 unless they obtain authorisation, and that Polish proceedings can only start once a law designates an authority. We could not find a UKNF statement dated after 1 July 2026. This is the position on 23 November 2026, so check the current status with the UKNF before you act on it.

ESMA's interim MiCA register, read on 23 November 2026, lists about 400 entries, none with Poland as the home member state. More than 100 entries are listed as able to serve Poland.

Poland compared with other countries

Polish firms most often look at Lithuania, Malta and Cyprus. Estonia is another option, and its page compares it with Lithuania. In Poland, the sibling licences are the EMI licence and the payment institution licence.

Crypto license in Poland FAQs

Can I get a crypto license in Poland in 2026?

No. The UKNF says no Polish authority is designated for CASPs, and proceedings can only start after a law designates one. The act passed on 15 May 2026 was vetoed and not passed again on 4 September 2026.

Is the Polish crypto-asset act in force?

As of 23 November 2026, we found no act in force. The Sejm record shows the veto stands, and an MPs' bill (print 3173) shows no reading.

Can I still operate with a Polish VASP registration?

No. The Katowice register of virtual currency activities was the pre-MiCA basis. After 1 July 2026 it gives no right to provide MiCA services.

How do Polish crypto firms get licensed?

They apply in another member state and passport into Poland under Art 65. The UKNF statement confirms that CASPs from other states may serve Poland.

How much does a crypto license cost in Poland?

There is no Polish fee. Capital is EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 by class under MiCA, plus the fees of the state where you apply.

Is Poland moving to a MiCA regime?

The government, the President and a group of MPs all filed bills with the Sejm in 2026. As of 23 November 2026 none is in force. Check the Sejm site for print 3173 and any later print.

Which regulator will supervise a Polish-based CASP?

The home member state regulator, once you are authorised there. A Polish authority is not designated.

Can Polish clients use an EU exchange?

Yes, if the exchange is authorised in an EEA state and has sent its Art 65 notification. Check the ESMA register.
