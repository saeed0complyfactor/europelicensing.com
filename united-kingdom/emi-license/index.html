EMI License in the UK

An electronic money institution (EMI) issues e-money in the UK under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 (EMRs 2011). The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) grants the licence. Your route depends on volume. Up to EUR 5m average outstanding e-money you can apply as a small EMI; above it you need full authorisation with EUR 350,000 initial capital.

Do you need an FCA EMI license?

You need one if you issue e-money to UK customers: prepaid accounts, wallets, prepaid cards or similar stored value that you redeem at par. Payment services without e-money issuance fit a payment institution licence better, and the capital is lower.

Four routes sit below full authorisation or beside it:

  • Small EMI. Average outstanding e-money up to EUR 5,000,000, unrelated payment transactions up to EUR 3,000,000 a month on average, and no account information or payment initiation services (EMRs reg 13). A new firm can base the payment transactions limit on projected figures (reg 13(11)).
  • Authorised EMI. Required above those limits, or if you want account information or payment initiation.
  • Agent of an existing EMI. An EMI may provide payment services through an agent only if the FCA register includes that agent (EMRs reg 34(1)).
  • Account information only. That is RAISP registration under the PSRs 2017, not an EMI.

An EU EMI licence does not cover the UK. If you serve EU clients too, see the EMI licence in Lithuania or the EMI licence in Ireland.

FCA EMI license requirements

Capital and own funds

An authorised EMI needs initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 (EMRs Sch 2 para 2). A small EMI needs 2% of average outstanding e-money where that figure is EUR 500,000 or more (Sch 2 para 3(1)). Once authorised, own funds for the e-money business are calculated under Method D at 2% of average outstanding e-money (Sch 2 para 23(2)).

People and governance

The FCA's application page asks for forms for directors, managers and anyone with a qualifying holding, and evidence that the people involved know e-money or payment services. Directors and managers must be of good repute. It also asks for a main contact who works at the firm and for policies written for your business.

Safeguarding

Relevant funds received for e-money must be protected under the EMRs. The FCA's safeguarding rules changed on 7 May 2026 under PS25/12, which introduced a supplementary regime for payment and e-money firms. The FCA's application page lists CASS 15, CASS 10A, SUP 3A and SUP 16.14A among the safeguarding rules a firm should read. PS25/12 describes a later, CASS-style end state that needs further legislation, so read the current text of each rule before you design your safeguarding account structure.

AML and other controls

An EMI needs AML controls under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and a nominated officer. The application also needs wind-down arrangements, professional indemnity insurance where it applies, and financial forecasts including balance sheet forecasts and stress-tested scenarios where relevant.

How to apply for an EMI license in the UK

  1. Pick your status (small or authorised EMI) and confirm your capital.
  2. Ask for a pre-application meeting. The FCA's pre-application support service (PASS) is open to PI, EMI and RAISP applicants.
  3. Register on Connect, the FCA's online system, and submit the application through it.
  4. Pay the non-refundable fee. The FCA rejects an application without assessment if the fee is wrong.
  5. The FCA allocates a case officer within a maximum of 20 working days.
  6. Answer the case officer's questions.
  7. Decision. The FCA must decide a complete application within 3 months of receiving it, and an incomplete one within 12 months of receipt (EMRs reg 9).

The FCA lists what the application must contain on its application page, but its pages do not state an application language. The 3-month clock runs from a complete application, so time spent preparing and answering questions comes on top of it.

Other cost lines, with no amounts here: the UK company and registered office, senior and compliance staff, systems, audit, safeguarding accounts at a credit institution, professional indemnity insurance where required, and adviser fees.

After authorisation

  • Register entry. The FCA adds you to the Financial Services Register.
  • Agents. Register every agent you use for payment services before it acts. The FCA must decide a complete agent application within 2 months (EMRs reg 34(10B)), and you must tell it without undue delay about changes to the information supplied (reg 34(15)).
  • Safeguarding. Follow the rules in force since 7 May 2026 and the reporting they require.
  • Fees. Pay the FCA's periodic fees as the fee schedule sets them.
  • Change of control. Acquirers of qualifying holdings need FCA approval before they act.
  • No passport. A UK EMI cannot serve EEA clients on a passport basis; that needs an EU or EEA licence.

UK EMI compared with other options

The UK has a small-EMI tier with a EUR 5m ceiling and no passport. The EMI licence in Lithuania and the EMI licence in Ireland give EEA access under EMD2. In the UK, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and FCA crypto authorisation. The wider guide is FCA authorisation.

EMI in the UK FAQs

How much capital does a UK EMI need?

EUR 350,000 for an authorised EMI (EMRs Sch 2 para 2). A small EMI needs 2% of average outstanding e-money where that is EUR 500,000 or more (Sch 2 para 3(1)).

What is a small EMI in the UK?

A firm whose average outstanding e-money is up to EUR 5,000,000 and whose unrelated payment transactions are up to EUR 3,000,000 a month on average, with no account information or payment initiation services (EMRs reg 13).

How much is the FCA application fee for an EMI?

GBP 5,640 for an authorised EMI and GBP 1,130 for a small EMI, per the FCA fee page updated on 19 November 2026. The fee is non-refundable.

How long does the FCA take to approve an EMI?

By law, 3 months from a complete application and no more than 12 months from receipt of an incomplete one (EMRs reg 9). The FCA allocates a case officer within a maximum of 20 working days.

Can a UK EMI passport into the EU?

No. UK-EEA passporting ended on 31 December 2020.

Does a UK EMI need safeguarding?

Yes. The supplementary safeguarding regime has applied since 7 May 2026 (PS25/12), and the FCA lists CASS 15 among the safeguarding rules to read.

Can a UK EMI use agents?

For payment services, yes, if each agent is on the FCA register (EMRs reg 34(1)).

Can an EMI offer crypto services in the UK?

Not under its EMI authorisation alone. The FCA states EMR and PSR firms also need FSMA authorisation for the new cryptoasset activities. See the crypto page.
