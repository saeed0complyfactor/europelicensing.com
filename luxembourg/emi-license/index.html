EMI License in Luxembourg

An electronic money institution licence [agrément d'établissement de monnaie électronique] from the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from Luxembourg, then passport them across the EEA. It suits wallets, prepaid and card programmes and neobanks that hold customer balances. The CSSF wants a real decision-making centre in Luxembourg, with at least two members of the management body.

Who needs an EMI license in Luxembourg?

You need a CSSF authorisation if you issue electronic money from Luxembourg. An EME can also provide the PSD2 Annex I payment services, but it may not take deposits and must exchange funds for e-money without delay.

Another route can fit better:

  • If you only move money and never hold a balance for later spending, a payment institution licence in Luxembourg needs less capital.
  • If you are small, the CSSF may exempt an e-money issuer on written request where average outstanding e-money does not exceed EUR 5 million and no manager has relevant convictions (PSL Art 48-1).
  • If you already hold an EMI licence in another EEA state, you passport into Luxembourg and need no Luxembourg licence.
  • If you also want to custody or transfer your own e-money tokens, an EMI can notify the CSSF under MiCA Art 60. Wider services need a Luxembourg CASP authorisation.

EMI license requirements in Luxembourg

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is not less than EUR 350,000 and must consist of the items in CRR Art 26(1)(a) to (e) (PSL Art 24-11). Own funds then stay at or above the higher of that figure and the calculated amount, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5).

Management and fit and proper

Directors and managers must be of good repute with the knowledge and experience for their duties, and qualifying shareholders are tested for suitability (PSD2 Art 5(1)(m), (n); Art 11(6), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). The CSSF asks for declarations of honour from each legal and natural person, in versions it updated on 22 May 2024.

Substance

Art 24-7(1) requires evidence of central administration and a registered office in Luxembourg, and the EMI must provide at least part of its e-money activity in Luxembourg. Circular CSSF 26/906, which applies from 30 June 2026, spells out what central administration means: besides a registered office, the institution needs its decision-making centre and administrative centre in Luxembourg. The decision-making centre includes the supervisory body and at least two members of the management body who can effectively set the direction of the business. It also covers the people responsible for internal control, commercial, administrative, IT and operational functions. Outsourcing stays possible under the CSSF's outsourcing circular, but the institution must keep control over its activities at all times.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

You safeguard funds received for e-money (EMD2 Art 7(1)) either by segregating them in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). The CSSF may determine the method (PSL Art 24-10(6)).

ICT and DORA

The file describes ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, incident handling, business continuity and a security policy (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e), (f), (h), (j)).

How to get an EMI license in Luxembourg

  1. Ask the CSSF for an initial meeting through ipig@cssf.lu and present the applicant and the project. Bring a business plan, fund and data-flow diagrams, and details of business partners and their contractual roles.
  2. Set up a Luxembourg legal entity, put its central administration in Luxembourg and pay in the capital.
  3. Prepare the file on the CSSF's EMI application form and declarations of honour, available on the CSSF procedure page.
  4. File exclusively electronically through the CSSF Managed File Transfer system. The CSSF gives you the link on request.
  5. The CSSF reviews the file and may contact you by email, letter, conference call or meeting.
  6. It notifies a reasoned decision within 3 months of receiving the application or, if the file is incomplete, within 3 months of receiving the missing information. In any case it decides within 12 months, otherwise the application is deemed refused (PSL Art 24-3(3)).
  7. You can appeal a decision to the Tribunal administratif within one month.
  8. After authorisation, the CSSF publishes you in its national register and the EBA's central register.

These are statutory limits. Time spent preparing the file and answering questions comes on top, and we give you a dated plan after scoping.

After you get the EMI license

  • The CSSF publishes you in its national register and the EBA's central register.
  • You report to the CSSF under its prudential and payment-services rules and keep own funds above the requirement.
  • You pay the annual flat fee.
  • To serve another EEA state, you notify the CSSF, which forwards the notice (PSD2 Art 28). You may use distributors abroad but not agents to issue e-money (EMD2 Art 3(4) and (5)).
  • A change of qualifying holding is notified to the CSSF, and the fee regulation sets EUR 8,000 for that notification.

Luxembourg EMI license vs other countries

Luxembourg puts the weight on a genuine decision-making centre in the country, and its fee schedule is published in full. To compare, see the EMI licence in the Netherlands, Lithuania and Ireland. Within Luxembourg, the sibling pages are the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence. The EU-wide rules sit in our EMI licence in Europe guide.

EMI license in Luxembourg FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Luxembourg?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation, made up of CRR Art 26(1)(a) to (e) items (PSL Art 24-11; EMD2 Art 4).

How much does an EMI licence cost in Luxembourg?

The CSSF charges EUR 30,000 one-off for an application, then an annual flat fee of EUR 30,000 or EUR 40,000 by volume, with a reduced EUR 25,000 in the first years for smaller volumes. Add EUR 350,000 of capital and the running costs of a regulated firm.

How long does it take to get an EMI licence in Luxembourg?

The CSSF decides within 3 months of receiving the application or the missing information, and in any case within 12 months (PSL Art 24-3(3)). Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

Do I need an office or staff in Luxembourg?

Yes. Circular CSSF 26/906 requires the decision-making centre and administrative centre to be in Luxembourg, with at least two members of the management body in the decision-making centre.

Can I apply in English?

The CSSF publishes its declarations of honour in English and French. We did not find a written language rule, so confirm it at the initial meeting.

Can I passport a Luxembourg EMI licence?

Yes, by notifying the CSSF (PSD2 Art 28).

Is there a small EMI route in Luxembourg?

Yes. The CSSF may exempt an issuer on written request where average outstanding e-money stays at or below EUR 5 million (PSL Art 48-1). An exempt issuer cannot passport (EMD2 Art 9(3)).

What is the difference between an EME and a payment institution?

An EME [établissement de monnaie électronique] issues e-money and may also provide payment services, with EUR 350,000 of capital. A payment institution [établissement de paiement] provides payment services only. See the payment institution page.
