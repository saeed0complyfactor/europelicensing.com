Payment Institution License in Luxembourg

A payment institution licence [agrément d'établissement de paiement] from the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) lets you run payment accounts, execute transfers, acquire card payments, send remittances or initiate payments from Luxembourg, then passport those services across the EEA. It is for payment firms that do not issue e-money. The CSSF expects a decision-making centre in Luxembourg, with at least two members of the management body.

Who needs a payment institution license in Luxembourg?

You need CSSF authorisation to provide payment services as a payment institution in Luxembourg, unless you are a bank, an EMI or another provider PSD2 already covers (PSL Art 6). The authorisation names the services you may provide, and a change in the type of services needs prior authorisation (Art 7(2), (3)).

Another route can fit better:

  • If your product holds a balance for later spending, that is e-money and needs an EMI licence in Luxembourg.
  • If you are small, the CSSF may exempt you on written request for services 1 to 6 where the monthly average of the previous 12 months' payment transactions stays at or below EUR 3 million and no manager has relevant convictions. You must actually carry on business in Luxembourg and have your central administration or residence there. You cannot passport.
  • If you only provide account information, the CSSF registers you (PSL Art 48-1a) on a shorter file and with professional indemnity insurance. The CSSF charges EUR 8,000 for registration and EUR 11,000 a year.
  • If you already hold a licence in another EEA state, you passport into Luxembourg and need no Luxembourg licence.

Payment institution license requirements in Luxembourg

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is EUR 20,000, EUR 50,000 or EUR 125,000 depending on the services, made up of CRR Art 26(1)(a) to (e) items (PSL Art 15). Own funds then stay at or above the higher of that figure and your Method A, B or C result (PSD2 Art 8, 9).

Management and fit and proper

Directors and managers must be of good repute with the knowledge and experience for their duties, and qualifying shareholders are tested for suitability (PSD2 Art 5(1)(m), (n); Art 11(6)). The CSSF asks for declarations of honour for legal and natural persons.

Substance

PSL Art 11(1) requires evidence of central administration and a registered office in Luxembourg, and at least part of the payment services business must be provided in Luxembourg. Circular CSSF 26/906, which applies from 30 June 2026, says that besides a registered office, the institution needs its decision-making centre and administrative centre in Luxembourg. The decision-making centre includes the supervisory body and at least two members of the management body, plus the people in charge of internal control, commercial, administrative, IT and operational functions. Outsourcing stays possible, but the institution keeps control over its activities.

AML officer and controls

The file describes internal controls under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(k)). Plan for a named AML compliance officer and a risk assessment of your product. See our AML and compliance programme.

Safeguarding

For services 1 to 6, you safeguard funds received for payment transactions by segregation in a credit institution or central bank account or in secure low-risk assets, or by insurance or a guarantee from outside your group (PSD2 Art 10(1), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/886).

Professional indemnity insurance and ICT

Payment initiation and account information providers need professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee (PSD2 Art 5(2), (3)). The file describes ICT arrangements under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (PSD2 Art 5(1)(e)).

How to get a payment institution license in Luxembourg

  1. Map each product feature to PSD2 Annex I to fix your capital tier and own-funds method.
  2. Ask the CSSF for an initial meeting through ipig@cssf.lu and present the project with a business plan, fund and data-flow diagrams, and details of business partners.
  3. Set up a Luxembourg legal entity, put its central administration in Luxembourg and pay in the capital.
  4. Prepare the file on the CSSF's PI application form and declarations of honour, available on the CSSF procedure page.
  5. File exclusively electronically through the CSSF Managed File Transfer system. The CSSF gives you the link on request.
  6. The CSSF reviews the file and may contact you by email, letter, conference call or meeting. It consults the Banque centrale du Luxembourg or other authorities where it needs to.
  7. It notifies a reasoned decision within 3 months of receiving the application or the missing information. In any case it decides within 12 months, otherwise the application is deemed refused. You can appeal to the Tribunal administratif within one month (PSL Art 7(5)).
  8. After authorisation, the CSSF publishes you in its national register and the EBA's central register.

These are statutory limits. Preparation and question rounds come on top, and we give you a dated plan after scoping.

After you get the payment institution license

  • The CSSF publishes you in its national register and the EBA's central register.
  • You report to the CSSF and keep own funds above the requirement.
  • You pay the annual flat fee.
  • To serve another EEA state, you notify the CSSF, which forwards the notice within 1 month, and the host state assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28).
  • A change of qualifying holding is notified to the CSSF, which charges EUR 8,000 for that notification.

Luxembourg payment institution license vs other countries

Luxembourg publishes its whole fee schedule and applies a 12-month long-stop to the 3-month decision period. To compare, see the payment institution licence in the Netherlands, Lithuania and Ireland. Within Luxembourg, the sibling pages are the EMI licence and the MiCA CASP licence. The EU rules sit in our payment institution licence in Europe guide.

Payment institution license in Luxembourg FAQs

What is an établissement de paiement?

A payment institution authorised by the CSSF under the Law of 10 November 2009 to provide payment services. It cannot issue e-money, which needs an EMI licence.

How much capital does a payment institution need in Luxembourg?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for services 1 to 5 (PSL Art 15).

How much does a payment institution licence cost in Luxembourg?

The CSSF charges EUR 30,000 one-off for an application, then an annual flat fee of EUR 30,000 or EUR 40,000 by volume, with a reduced EUR 25,000 in the first years for smaller volumes. Add the capital and the running costs of a regulated firm.

How long does it take to get a payment institution licence in Luxembourg?

The CSSF decides within 3 months of receiving the application or the missing information, and in any case within 12 months (PSL Art 7(5)). Preparation comes on top, and we do not quote a real-world average.

Do I need an office or staff in Luxembourg?

Yes. The decision-making centre and administrative centre must be in Luxembourg, with at least two members of the management body in the decision-making centre (Circular CSSF 26/906).

What is the Luxembourg small payment institution exemption?

An exemption on written request for services 1 to 6, with a monthly average of payment transactions up to EUR 3 million over the previous 12 months and no relevant manager convictions. The firm must actually operate from Luxembourg and cannot passport.

Can I passport a Luxembourg payment institution licence?

Yes, by notifying the CSSF (PSD2 Art 28).

Do I need an EMI or a payment institution licence?

If customers keep a balance with you for later use, you issue e-money and need an EMI licence. If you only execute payments, a payment institution licence is enough.
