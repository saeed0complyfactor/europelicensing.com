EMI License in Greece

A Greek EMI licence lets you issue electronic money and provide payment services from Greece, then passport both across the EU and EEA. The Bank of Greece grants it under Law 4021/2011 and its own Executive Committee Act 164/2/2019. Initial capital is EUR 350,000. For payment institutions the Bank takes paper files in Athens, so ask whether the same applies to an EMI.

Who needs an EMI licence in Greece?

You need Bank of Greece authorisation to issue electronic money from Greece unless you hold a licence in another EEA state and use your passport rights. Prepaid cards, wallets with a stored balance and similar products usually fall inside the e-money definition of EMD2 Art 2(2).

Another route may fit better in these cases:

  • You only move money and never hold a customer balance. A payment institution licence in Greece needs lower initial capital.
  • You only want account information services. The Bank of Greece runs a register for those providers under Article 14 of Law 4537/2018 (Act 164/2/2019, Chapter II), with no authorisation procedure.
  • You already hold an EMI licence elsewhere in the EEA. Notify your home regulator and use the passport instead of applying in Athens.
  • You want to issue or handle e-money tokens. An EMI can issue them after a white paper notification (MiCA Art 48), and it may notify under MiCA Art 60(4) to offer custody and transfer services for the tokens it issues. Other crypto services need full MiCA authorisation in Greece.
  • You expect to stay small. Under Article 19 of Law 4021/2011 (EMD2 Art 9) the Bank of Greece can exempt an issuer, fully or partly, from the initial capital, own funds and some other rules. An exempted institution cannot passport, and it must apply for full authorisation within 30 calendar days once it stops meeting the conditions. Ask the Bank for the current thresholds.

Requirements for an EMI licence in Greece

Capital and own funds

Initial capital is at least EUR 350,000 (EMD2 Art 4, transposed by Article 14 of Law 4021/2011), and the Bank of Greece's authorisation page states the same figure. Act 164/2/2019 requires you to show the capital fully paid up and deposited with a credit institution in Greece, or one operating there through a branch, that is not a related party. It must stay on deposit at least until the licence is granted (Chapter III, 6). A company that already exists also provides an auditor's certificate that net equity is not below the minimum, based on an updated balance sheet. You also document where each contributor's money came from.

Own funds then stay at the higher of the capital requirement and the Article 14(2) minimum (Act 164/2/2019, Chapter X, 1). For e-money the requirement is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)). An EMI that grants credit adds 10% of the outstanding credit balance.

Legal form and substance

The Bank of Greece service page we read covers payment institutions set up as a société anonyme (SA) or European company (SE). We found no page that states a legal form for EMIs, so confirm the form with the Bank before incorporating. Act 164/2/2019 asks for the articles of association, the registered seat and the head office address (Chapter I, 2.1). A company still in formation gets its licence only after incorporation (Chapter I, 1.8).

Management and shareholders

Board members and key function holders complete the Bank's suitability questionnaires. Shareholders with qualifying holdings complete the holdings questionnaires, and you disclose holders above 1% of share capital (Chapter VI, VII and 15.4).

Safeguarding

Act 164/2/2019 allows two methods (Chapter III, 7). You can keep segregated funds in a deposit or custody account at an unrelated credit institution in Greece. You can instead take insurance or a comparable guarantee from an insurer or credit institution in Greece or the EEA that sits outside your group. This follows EMD2 Art 7(1) and PSD2 Art 10.

Governance, ICT and AML

The file covers a business programme, a three-year business plan, organisation, outsourcing, internal controls, security and AML/CFT procedures. For ICT, add your DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) documentation. See our AML and compliance programme page for how we build those policies.

How to apply for an EMI licence in Greece

  1. Consider the pre-application procedure. It is optional and consists of two meetings with Bank of Greece staff, in person, online or hybrid. It does not prejudge the decision.
  2. Choose the services. The Bank asks you to state whether you will issue, redeem and distribute e-money, with or without payment services.
  3. Prepare the application form in Annex I of Act 164/2/2019 and the supporting documents in Chapter III. The Bank's checklist is in Act 164/2/2019. The level of detail should match your size, organisation and risk profile (Chapter I, 1.4).
  4. Pay in the capital and open the deposit account before filing, with proof of the origin of funds.
  5. File with the Bank of Greece. For payment institutions the Bank's service page describes a sealed paper file delivered in person or by post to 21 Eleftheriou Venizelou St., 102 50 Athens, with a USB or CD of PDF scans, and no online step. Ask the Directorate whether EMI files follow the same rule.
  6. Completeness. The Bank tells you in writing about any gaps and confirms the date it received a complete application (Chapter IV). Keep information current while the file is under review.
  7. Decision. EU law requires a decision within 3 months of a complete application (PSD2 Art 12, applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). The Bank may ask for more information at any stage, and the time you spend answering comes on top of the clock.

Act 164/2/2019 and the older Act 33/2013 state no decision deadline of their own.

After authorisation

  • Register: the Bank of Greece publishes lists of Greek EMIs and of EEA EMIs that notified it of services in Greece. We could not open the list pages, so we give no count.
  • Reporting: semi-annual reports are due within 20 working days after the half-year ends, and annual financial statements within 5 working days after publication (Act 164/2/2019, Chapter XI, 3.1).
  • Outsourcing: written notice at least 30 working days before outsourcing (Chapter VIII, 4).
  • Qualifying holdings: prior notification with the Bank's questionnaires (Chapter VI).
  • Changes: notify material changes to your application immediately (Chapter XII).
  • Agents and branches: notify new agent arrangements in Greece or another EEA state with supporting documents (Chapter X, 3 and Chapter XIII).
  • Passport: authorisation is valid across the EU (PSD2 Art 11(9), applied by EMD2 Art 3(1)). You notify the Bank of the states, services, agents and branches. The home regulator forwards the notice within 1 month and the host assesses it within 1 month (PSD2 Art 28).
  • Supervision fee: not found in the sources we read.

Greece vs other countries

Greece asks for a capital deposit held in Greece and offers an optional pre-application stage. Compare the EMI licence in Cyprus, where the Central Bank of Cyprus now starts with a paid pre-application stage, the EMI licence in Malta and the EMI licence in Lithuania. In Greece, the payment institution licence needs less capital, and the MiCA CASP licence comes from a different regulator, the HCMC.

EMI in Greece FAQs

How much capital do you need for an EMI licence in Greece?

EUR 350,000 at authorisation (EMD2 Art 4, transposed by Law 4021/2011 Art 14). It must be fully paid up and deposited with an unrelated credit institution in Greece until the licence is granted. Own funds then stay above the higher of that figure and the calculated requirement, which for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

Who issues EMI licences in Greece?

The Bank of Greece. Its Directorate of Supervision of Supervised Companies runs the supervision, and the authorisation terms sit in Executive Committee Act 164/2/2019, as amended.

How long does an EMI licence take in Greece?

EU law sets a decision within 3 months of a complete application (PSD2 Art 12). Neither Greek act we read adds a deadline. File preparation, the capital deposit and the Bank's questions come on top, and we do not quote a timeline.

How much does the Bank of Greece charge for an EMI application?

We could not confirm an EMI fee. For payment institutions the Bank's fee act sets EUR 5,000 at filing. Ask the Bank for the EMI figure under Act 238/2/10.01.2025.

Do you need an office in Greece?

Act 164/2/2019 asks for your registered seat and head office address, and the capital must sit in a Greek bank account. Confirm with the Bank how much management substance it expects in Greece.

Can a Greek EMI serve customers across the EU?

Yes. You notify the Bank of the states and services you want to add (PSD2 Art 28), and the Bank forwards the notice to the host regulator.

Can I file in English?

The Bank accepts certificates translated into Greek or English (Act 164/2/2019, Chapter I, 1.6). Ask the Directorate whether it accepts the rest of the file in English.

Is there a small EMI tier in Greece?

Yes. The Bank of Greece can exempt an issuer under Article 19 of Law 4021/2011 (EMD2 Art 9, which caps the waiver at EUR 5,000,000 of average outstanding e-money). An exempted institution cannot passport. Ask the Bank for the current conditions.
