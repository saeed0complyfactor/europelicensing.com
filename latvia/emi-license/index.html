EMI License in Latvia

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence in Latvia lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from a Latvian company. Latvijas Banka, the central bank, grants it under the Law on Payment Services and Electronic Money. Own funds must be at least EUR 350,000, the application fee is EUR 5,000, and Latvia also has a separate registered EMI tier that cannot serve customers outside Latvia.

Who needs an EMI license in Latvia?

You need a Latvijas Banka licence to issue e-money in your own name from Latvia. Latvijas Banka lists the permitted e-money issuers: credit institutions, EMIs, central banks, certain public bodies, institutions licensed in another member state that have started business in Latvia, and branches of foreign EMIs licensed in Latvia. A company that only distributes e-money issued by a foreign issuer may still need an issuance licence under Section 11 of the Law. Agents of EU-licensed EMIs working cross-border under the freedom to provide services are excepted.

Under Section 2(8), an e-money account sits in a closed system, where transfers stay between accounts at the same EMI. If you link the account to a card that any merchant accepts, the system becomes open and payment account rules apply.

Other routes may fit better:

  • Registered EMI. It has no minimum capital, but average e-money in circulation must stay at or below EUR 2 million, the system must be closed, and you cannot serve customers or use international payment systems outside Latvia. Besides e-money it permits only issuing payment instruments and money remittance.
  • A payment institution licence if you move money without holding a stored balance.
  • An EMI licensed elsewhere in the EEA, serving Latvia by notification.
  • E-money tokens. Issuers must be credit institutions or EMIs (MiCA Art 48). See the Latvia crypto page.

EMI license requirements in Latvia

Capital and own funds. Latvijas Banka's licensed EMI page sets own funds of at least EUR 350,000 (Section 12 of the Law) plus the capital requirement under Section 35. EMD2 Art 4 sets the same initial capital, and Art 5 sets ongoing own funds, using method D of at least 2% of average outstanding e-money for e-money activity. You provide proof of the initial capital and its origin.

Management and fit and proper. Management needs adequate education and experience (Section 20). Management and shareholders with 10% or more of the capital or voting rights need good repute and no criminal record (Section 21). Board members, the AML officer and the person responsible for payment services file CVs, education evidence and criminal record certificates (Regulation No 270, Annex 14).

Substance. PSD2 Art 11(3) puts the head office in the same member state as the registered office. Latvijas Banka's licensed EMI page sets no board or office minimum beyond that. For a registered EMI the management board and legal address must be in Latvia.

AML. You file internal AML and counter-terrorism financing and sanctions rules, a risk assessment, Annex 13 and Latvijas Banka's AML checklist form.

Safeguarding. You describe how user funds are protected (Section 38; Annex 5).

ICT and DORA. The file covers an information systems security policy and risk assessment (Annex 11), business continuity plans and tests (Annex 10), security incident handling (Annex 8) and sensitive payment data controls (Annex 12). Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applies directly.

Insurance. If you add payment initiation or account information services, you need professional civil liability insurance or a credit institution guarantee by the decision date. Regulation No 269 sets the minimum.

How to get an EMI license in Latvia: process

  1. Book the free pre-application meeting. Email info@bank.lv or call +371 6702 2300. Latvijas Banka answers advice questions within up to 30 days, depending on volume and complexity. It offers a pre-licensing checklist spreadsheet on the page.
  2. Prepare the file. Latvijas Banka's page lists the Regulation No 270 Annex 1 application with the shareholder structure, the programme of operations (Annex 3), a business plan for at least three financial years (Annex 4), proof of capital, safeguarding, internal control and risk management (Annex 7), AML documents, organisation, agents, branches and outsourcing (Annex 6), a close links list, management data, articles of association, incident, ICT, continuity, fraud statistics and group governance documents where they apply.
  3. Language. The English page does not state a filing language, and some regulations are published in Latvian only. Confirm in the meeting.
  4. Submit and pay EUR 5,000 at submission.
  5. Completeness check. Latvijas Banka checks the file within 15 working days.
  6. Assessment. Latvijas Banka assesses the application within 3 months of confirmed completeness. It can extend that if information is insufficient. PSD2 Art 12 also sets three months from a complete application.
  7. Decision. Latvijas Banka grants or refuses the licence and publishes the result on its website and in the EBA register.

The statutory periods cover the regulator's work. Preparing the file and answering questions come on top of them.

After you get the EMI license

  • Register. Latvijas Banka publishes the decision on its website and in the EBA register.
  • Supervision. Latvijas Banka assigns a curator and supervises you on an ongoing basis. You report under Regulation No 244 and notify it of changes.
  • Fees. The annual supervision fee applies from authorisation, as shown in the cost table.
  • Passport. A licensed EMI notifies Latvijas Banka before serving another EEA state (PSD2 Art 28). A registered EMI has no passport.
  • Change of control. Latvijas Banka Regulation No 241 covers the information for qualifying holding changes. Changes to documents, service procedures, management or shareholders are notified too.
  • E-money tokens. If you handle e-money tokens, Latvijas Banka's page for existing firms lists an Art 60 MiCA authorisation for EMIs, covering points (17) and (26) of Art 3(1).

EMI in Latvia compared with other countries

Latvia uses the EU capital figure and publishes a EUR 5,000 examination fee. Lithuania and Estonia license the same institution, and their fee pages differ. In Latvia itself, the payment institution licence carries lower capital, and the crypto page covers MiCA. For the EU rules, read the EMI licence guide.

EMI in Latvia FAQs

How much capital does an EMI need in Latvia?

Own funds of at least EUR 350,000 under Section 12 of the Law and EMD2 Art 4, plus the capital requirement under Section 35. A registered EMI has no minimum capital, but its own funds cannot be negative.

How long does Latvijas Banka take to decide on an EMI licence?

It checks completeness within 15 working days, then assesses within 3 months of confirmed completeness. It can extend the assessment if information is insufficient.

How much does an EMI application cost in Latvia?

The examination fee is EUR 5,000, paid at submission. A licence limited to an innovative service costs EUR 450. The supervision fee is EUR 7,000 a year plus up to 1.4% of gross income, capped at EUR 100,000.

What is a registered EMI in Latvia?

A lighter tier with no minimum capital. Average e-money in circulation must stay at or below EUR 2 million, the system must be closed, management and legal address must be in Latvia, and services cannot go outside Latvia.

Can a Latvian EMI serve other EU countries?

A licensed EMI can, by notification under PSD2 Art 28. A registered EMI cannot.

Do I need an office in Latvia?

The head office sits in the same state as the registered office (PSD2 Art 11(3)). A registered EMI needs its board and legal address in Latvia. The licensed EMI page sets no further minimum.

Is there free advice before I apply?

Yes. Latvijas Banka offers a free pre-application meeting. Book by email or phone, and expect an answer to written questions within up to 30 days.

Does an EMI licence cover crypto services?

No. An EMI can apply under MiCA Art 60 for custody and transfer services for the e-money tokens it issues. Wider services need CASP authorisation. See the MiCA guide.
