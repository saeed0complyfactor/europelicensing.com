Payment Institution License in Slovakia

A payment institution (PI) licence lets you provide payment services such as money remittance, payment initiation and card acquiring from a Slovak company, without issuing e-money. The National Bank of Slovakia (NBS) grants it under the Payment Services Act. The NBS fee is EUR 3,400, and paid-in capital is EUR 20,000, 50,000 or 125,000 depending on the services.

Who needs a payment institution licence in Slovakia?

You need one if you provide payment services in Slovakia as a business and you are not a bank or an e-money institution. Typical cases are money remittance, payment initiation, card acquiring and issuing of payment instruments, and the account servicing that goes with them.

Other routes can fit better:

  • A registration as a limited-scope payment service provider, if the monthly average of your payment transactions over the last 12 months stays at or under EUR 3,000,000 and no responsible person has an economic crime conviction (Sec 79a(1)).
  • An account information service provider registration. The NBS runs separate pages for AISP applications.
  • A branch or agent of a payment institution from another EEA state, which uses its home authorisation and a passport notification.
  • An EMI licence if you also issue stored value.

Payment institution licence requirements in Slovakia

Capital

The paid-in contribution to share capital must include at least one CRR Art 26(1)(a) to (e) item and reach EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only, or EUR 125,000 if you provide any of services a to e of the Act (Sec 64(2)(b)). The origin of the contribution and of other financial resources must be transparent, credible and lawful (Sec 64(2)(c)). Own funds then follow PSD2 Art 8 and 9.

Company form and substance

The applicant must be a business company that creates share capital and has a supervisory board (Sec 64(2)(a)). Its registered seat, head office and payment services must be in Slovakia, and it must perform at least one authorised payment service (Sec 64(2)(k)). Since 1 November 2024, appointing a supervisory board member needs no prior NBS consent, and the NBS no longer checks their fitness at licensing (Act 248/2024, as described on the NBS page for e-money institutions).

Fit and proper

Proposed members of the statutory body, procurators, senior staff and the head of internal control must be professionally qualified and trustworthy (Sec 64(2)(e)). Qualifying holders must be suitable, with transparent links to other persons (Sec 64(2)(d)) and the financial ability to bridge difficult periods (Sec 64(2)(o)).

Safeguarding, insurance and ICT

Funds received from users that are not passed on by the end of the next business day must go into a separate account at a bank, a branch of a foreign bank or a central bank, or into safe, liquid, low-risk assets (Sec 77(7)). If you do not do that for services a to f, you need an insurance contract or comparable guarantee from an insurer or bank outside your group (Sec 77(8)). A payment institution may not take deposits (Sec 77(6)). The NBS published an opinion on 1 April 2025 on securing this protection. For payment initiation and account information, EBA-GL-2017-08 on professional indemnity insurance applies. ICT risk follows Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), and AML controls follow Act 297/2008 and Directive (EU) 2015/849.

How to apply for a payment institution licence in Slovakia

  1. Map your services to PSD2 Annex I to fix your capital tier and own funds approach.
  2. Form the Slovak business company with its supervisory board.
  3. Pay in the capital and document where it came from.
  4. Prepare the file under Sec 64(3) and (4) of Act 492/2009 and the EBA guidelines on authorisation (EBA-GL-2017-09).
  5. Submit a written application to the NBS and pay the fee.
  6. Answer the NBS's questions. It decides within 3 months of a complete application (Sec 64(5)).

The NBS page lists three procedures: a new authorisation, a change of an existing authorisation and a registration for limited-scope providers. It splits its guidance into pre-application, filing and post-filing pages. The pages checked do not state the filing language or offer a pre-application meeting for payment institutions, so confirm both with the NBS.

A limited-scope registration and an AISP registration each cost EUR 1,700 (Measure 5/2021, Part 8 Item 4(b), (c)). Supervised firms also pay annual contributions to the NBS under Act 747/2004.

After authorisation

The NBS lists authorised entities in its register of financial entities. A payment institution may carry on operational and ancillary services, run payment systems and other business, but the NBS can require a separate institution if that other business threatens financial stability or supervision (Sec 77(1), (3)). Prior NBS consent is needed for qualifying holdings and for new members of the statutory body, at fees set in Measure 5/2021 Part 8 Item 3. To serve other EEA states you notify the NBS under PSD2 Art 28. The host state assesses the notice within 1 month, and for an agent or branch the home regulator decides within 3 months. The NBS asks regulated firms to follow its methodological guideline 4/2014 on internal control.

Payment institution in Slovakia compared

The capital tiers and the 3-month clock come from PSD2, so they match other states. Slovakia adds company form rules, a supervisory board and its own fee schedule. Compare the payment institution licence in Lithuania, Ireland and the Netherlands. In Slovakia you can also read the EMI licence and the MiCA CASP licence pages. The EU-wide rules are in our payment institution licence guide.

Payment institution in Slovakia FAQs

How much capital does a Slovak payment institution need?

EUR 20,000 for money remittance only, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation only, and EUR 125,000 for any of services a to e, paid in at the time of authorisation (Act 492/2009 Sec 64(2)(b)).

How much does the NBS charge for a payment institution licence?

EUR 3,400 for a payment institution authorisation and EUR 1,700 for payment initiation only, under NBS Measure 5/2021 Part 8 Item 1 as amended.

How long does the NBS take to decide?

Within 3 months of a complete application (Sec 64(5)). Your preparation time is extra.

Does Slovakia have a small payment institution?

Yes, a limited-scope provider registered under Sec 79a. Its transactions must average no more than EUR 3,000,000 a month over 12 months, and it needs paid-in capital of EUR 62,500, or EUR 10,000 for money remittance only (Sec 79a(2)). The NBS decides within 3 months of a complete application (Sec 79a(1), (4)). It cannot passport (PSD2 Art 32(3)).

Does a Slovak payment institution need a supervisory board?

Yes. The applicant must be a business company with share capital and a supervisory board (Sec 64(2)(a)). Since 1 November 2024 the NBS no longer checks the fitness of its members at licensing.

Can I apply from outside Slovakia?

The registered seat, head office and payment services must be in Slovakia (Sec 64(2)(k)), and the Act defines a payment institution as a legal person with its seat in Slovakia (Sec 63).

Does the NBS publish its documents checklist?

The NBS points applicants to the Act and the EBA authorisation guidelines as the main sources, and links forms and FAQs on its payment institution pages.

Can I passport a Slovak payment institution licence?

Yes, by notification through the NBS under PSD2 Art 28.
