Crypto License in Slovakia

A Slovak crypto licence is now a MiCA authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP), granted by the National Bank of Slovakia (NBS). The trade licence regime ended on 30 December 2025, and by 29 July 2026 the NBS reported six licensed providers. The NBS application fee runs from EUR 1,700 to EUR 3,400 depending on the services.

Who needs a crypto license in Slovakia?

You need CASP authorisation if you provide crypto-asset services as a business to clients in Slovakia or the EU: custody, a trading platform, exchange for funds or other crypto-assets, order execution, placing, order transmission, advice, portfolio management or transfers. The NBS states that a financial agent's or adviser's licence is not a CASP permit, so they must apply for CASP authorisation.

Other routes can fit better:

  • MiCA Art 60 notification, if you are already a bank, investment firm, EMI or another listed financial entity. An EMI may notify only for custody and transfer of the e-money tokens it issues. The ESMA register shows Tatra banka, a.s. notifying on 31 August 2026.
  • A CASP authorised in another EU member state. Since 30 December 2025 only entities authorised by the NBS or by a foreign supervisor may provide services in Slovakia.
  • An EMI licence, if your product is an e-money token. Only credit institutions and EMIs may offer them (MiCA Art 48(1)).

Slovak crypto license requirements

Capital

MiCA sets the minimum at EUR 50,000 for class 1 services, EUR 125,000 for class 2 (adding custody and exchange) and EUR 150,000 for class 3 (adding a trading platform). You hold the higher of that amount or a quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads, using projected overheads in your first year (MiCA Art 67(1)-(2), Annex IV). The NBS asks for evidence of compliance with Art 67 in the file and says firms set up less than a year before applying are exempt from the audited-accounts requirement (NBS crypto FAQ).

Management, shareholders and substance

Directors must have good repute, knowledge, skills, experience and enough time, and qualifying shareholders must have good repute (MiCA Art 68(1)-(2)). You need a registered office in a member state, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director (Art 59(2)).

Policies, ICT and AML

The file covers custody, complaints, conflicts, outsourcing, business continuity, AML/CFT controls and ICT under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), as listed in MiCA Art 62(2) and 68. The Slovak AML Act is Act 297/2008, and travel-rule duties follow Regulation (EU) 2023/1113.

How to get a crypto license in Slovakia

  1. Map your services to MiCA's ten crypto-asset services and fix your capital class.
  2. Email the NBS at crypto@nbs.sk with your business model and the intended services under MiCA Art 3(1) to request the pre-licensing dialogue it recommends.
  3. Form the company and prepare the Art 62(2) file.
  4. Prepare the application and annexes in Slovak. Annexes in another language need a certified Slovak translation. On written request the NBS may waive translation of technical documentation in Czech or in a commonly used financial language, usually English.
  5. Submit to the NBS and pay the fee for your service class.
  6. The NBS acknowledges within 5 working days and confirms completeness within 25 working days (MiCA Art 63(1)-(2)).
  7. It decides within 40 working days of a complete application, and information requests can pause the clock (Art 63(9), (12)).
  8. After authorisation your details go to ESMA for its register.

The NBS says the applicant's preparedness mainly determines how long the process takes. It states no average duration.

After authorisation

Check the NBS register of financial entities at subjekty.nbs.sk, which lists NBS-licensed entities, and ESMA's MiCA register. The NBS charges an annual contribution under Act 747/2004. To serve other member states you send your home regulator a passport notice, which it forwards within 10 working days. You can start when you receive its communication or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (MiCA Art 65). A change to the authorisation costs EUR 350, and prior consent for a qualifying holding costs EUR 600 (Measure 5/2021, Part 9 Items 2 and 3(b)).

Status of the Slovak crypto market on 23 November 2026

Some pages give 1 July 2026 as the Slovak end date. According to the NBS, Act 248/2024 cut the transition for trade-licence holders to 12 months, ending 30 December 2025. The NBS confirmed on 31 December 2025 that it had ended. 1 July 2026 is the EU-wide backstop in MiCA Art 143(3), which applies in states that kept the full period. The NBS release of 29 July 2026 says MiCA applies in full across the EU since 1 July 2026 and in Slovakia since 30 December 2025.

The NBS asked legacy firms in September 2025 to inform clients and wind down, to return client crypto-assets held in custody and to stop. Continuing without authorisation can bring an NBS sanction for unauthorised business. The notice also tells clients to use licensed providers and to move assets held with unlicensed ones to a licensed provider or a self-hosted wallet. The NBS does not publish a count of refused or pending applications in the pages checked.

Crypto license in Slovakia compared

The MiCA capital classes and the 40-working-day clock are the same in every member state. Fees, language and supervisory practice differ. Compare the MiCA licence in Lithuania, Malta and the Czech Republic. In Slovakia you can also read the EMI licence and payment institution licence pages. The EU-wide rules are in our MiCA CASP guide.

Crypto license in Slovakia FAQs

How much does a Slovakia crypto license cost?

The NBS charges EUR 1,700, EUR 2,500 or EUR 3,400 for the application, depending on the services (Measure 5/2021, Part 9 Item 1). Capital is EUR 50,000, 125,000 or 150,000 by class, or a quarter of fixed overheads if higher. Staff, translations, audit, ICT and advisers come on top, and our fee depends on scope: from EUR 60,000.

Can I still use a Slovak trade licence for crypto services?

No. The 12-month transition under Act 248/2024 ended on 30 December 2025. Only entities with a MiCA authorisation from the NBS or a foreign supervisor may provide crypto-asset services in Slovakia.

How many crypto licences has the NBS granted?

The NBS reported six licensed providers on 29 July 2026. The ESMA register file downloaded on 23 November 2026 shows 7 Slovak entries, 6 of them authorisations and 1 an Art 60 notification.

How long does the NBS take?

MiCA gives 25 working days to confirm completeness and 40 working days to decide (Art 63(2), (9)). The NBS says the applicant's preparedness mainly determines the total time.

Does the application have to be in Slovak?

Yes. The NBS requires the application and annexes in Slovak, with certified translations of annexes in other languages. It may waive translation of technical documents in Czech or English on written request.

Is there a pre-application meeting?

The NBS recommends a pre-licensing dialogue. Email crypto@nbs.sk with a detailed business model and the services you intend to provide.

Is the Slovak crypto licence valid across the EU?

Yes. A passport notice lets you start in another member state on receiving your home regulator's communication or on the 15th calendar day at the latest (Art 65).

Do financial agents and advisers need a separate permit for crypto services?

Yes. The NBS says a financial agent's or adviser's licence is not a CASP permit, and agents who want to provide crypto-asset services must apply for one.
