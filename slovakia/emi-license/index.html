EMI License in Slovakia

An electronic money institution (EMI) licence lets you issue e-money and provide payment services from a Slovak company. The National Bank of Slovakia (NBS) grants it under the Payment Services Act, and the EU passport carries it across the EEA. Slovak law asks for EUR 350,000 of paid-in capital and charges a EUR 3,400 NBS fee.

Who needs an EMI licence in Slovakia?

You need it if you issue e-money from Slovakia: prepaid cards, wallets with a stored balance and similar products. Funds taken in exchange for e-money that is issued at once are not deposits (Act 492/2009 Sec 80(2)).

Other routes can fit better:

  • A payment institution licence if you never hold stored value. See payment institution licence in Slovakia.
  • A limited-scope EMI, if average outstanding e-money will never exceed EUR 5,000,000 (Sec 87(1)). It cannot issue e-money in another member state (Sec 87(6)).
  • An e-money token issuer. Under MiCA Art 48(1) only credit institutions and EMIs may offer e-money tokens.
  • An EMI authorised in another EEA state that passports into Slovakia.

EMI licence requirements in Slovakia

Capital and own funds

The paid-in contribution to share capital must be at least EUR 350,000 when the licence is granted, with a transparent, credible and lawful origin for it and for other financial resources (Act 492/2009 Sec 82(2)(b), (c)). The EU own funds method for e-money is at least 2% of average outstanding e-money (EMD2 Art 5(3)).

Company form and substance

The applicant must be a business company that creates share capital and has a supervisory board (Sec 82(2)(a)). Its registered seat, head office and e-money issuing must be in Slovakia (Sec 82(2)(k)). Since 1 November 2024, appointing a supervisory board member needs no prior NBS consent, and the NBS no longer checks the fitness of supervisory board members at licensing (Act 248/2024 as described on the NBS EMI page).

Fit and proper

Proposed members of the statutory body, procurators, senior staff and the head of internal control must be professionally qualified and trustworthy (Sec 82(2)(e)). Persons with a qualifying holding must be suitable and transparent, and must show they can bridge a difficult financial situation (Sec 82(2)(d), (n)). The applicant must not have been convicted of a criminal offence, shown by a criminal record extract (Sec 82(2)(q)).

Safeguarding, risk and ICT

You need a working system for protecting funds under Sec 77(7) and (8) (Sec 82(2)(o)), a risk analysis with a risk management system (Sec 82(2)(l)) and suitable technical systems (Sec 82(2)(j)). The funds you receive must be placed in a separate account at a bank or the central bank, or invested in safe, liquid, low-risk assets, or covered by insurance or a comparable guarantee. The NBS published an opinion on 1 April 2025 on how to secure that protection. ICT risk follows Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), and AML controls follow Act 297/2008 and Directive (EU) 2015/849.

How to apply for an EMI licence in Slovakia

  1. Map your products and fix whether you need a full or limited-scope EMI.
  2. Form the Slovak business company with its supervisory board.
  3. Pay in the EUR 350,000 (EUR 175,000 for limited scope) and document where it came from.
  4. Prepare the file under Sec 82(3) and (4) of Act 492/2009 and the EBA guidelines on authorisation (EBA-GL-2017-09), which the NBS lists among its regulations.
  5. Submit a written application to the NBS and pay the fee.
  6. Answer the NBS's questions. It decides within 3 months of a complete application (Sec 82(5)).

The NBS separates three procedures on its EMI page: a first authorisation, a change of an existing authorisation, and a limited-scope authorisation. It groups the guidance into pre-application, filing and post-filing pages. The pages checked do not state the filing language or offer a pre-application meeting for EMIs, so confirm both with the NBS.

After authorisation

The NBS lists authorised entities in its register of financial entities. A limited-scope EMI must notify any change in the conditions and file a monthly report on average e-money issued, unless the NBS sets another date (Sec 87(7)). If it no longer meets them, it has 30 days to apply for a full authorisation and must otherwise stop issuing (Sec 87(8)). A full EMI notifies the NBS to passport under PSD2 Art 28. The host state assesses the notice within 1 month, and for an agent or branch the home regulator decides within 3 months. The NBS also asks for prior consent for qualifying holdings and for new members of the statutory body, at a fee set in Measure 5/2021 Part 8 Item 3.

EMI in Slovakia compared

The capital figure and the 3-month clock come from EU law, so they match other states. Slovakia adds company form rules, a supervisory board and its own fee schedule. Compare the EMI licence in Lithuania, Ireland and the Netherlands. In Slovakia you can also read the payment institution licence and the MiCA CASP licence pages. The EU-wide rules are in our EMI licence guide.

EMI in Slovakia FAQs

How much capital does a Slovak EMI need?

EUR 350,000 of paid-in share capital at the time of authorisation (Act 492/2009 Sec 82(2)(b)). A limited-scope EMI needs EUR 175,000 (Sec 87(2)).

How much does the NBS charge for an EMI licence?

EUR 3,400 for a full authorisation and EUR 1,700 for limited scope, under NBS Measure 5/2021 Part 8 Item 1 as amended.

How long does the NBS take to decide?

Within 3 months of a complete application (Sec 82(5)). Your preparation time is extra.

Does Slovakia have a small EMI?

Yes. The limited-scope EMI may issue e-money while average outstanding e-money stays within EUR 5,000,000. It cannot issue e-money in another member state (Sec 87(6)).

Does a Slovak EMI need a supervisory board?

Yes. The applicant must be a business company with share capital and a supervisory board (Sec 82(2)(a)). Since 1 November 2024 the NBS no longer checks the fitness of its members at licensing.

Can I apply from outside Slovakia?

The registered seat, head office and e-money issuing must be in Slovakia (Sec 82(2)(k)). A foreign limited-scope EMI cannot issue e-money or provide payment services in Slovakia (Sec 87(10)).

Can a Slovak EMI issue e-money tokens?

Yes. MiCA Art 48(1) lets credit institutions and EMIs offer e-money tokens.

Can I passport a Slovak EMI licence?

A full EMI can, by notification under PSD2 Art 28 applied by EMD2 Art 3(1). A limited-scope EMI cannot.
